A reference for generating background and lightly-authored characters. Assign an archetype to give Claude a behavioral baseline. Characters can blend archetypes or deviate from them as they develop. When a character is elevated, the archetype remains as a baseline — add specifics on top of it rather than replacing it.

---

## The Careful Merchant

**Core behavior:** Transactional in all things. Information is a commodity; they give nothing away without calculating the return. Not unfriendly — warmth is just another resource to deploy when it serves.
**Speech register:** Measured, polite, noncommittal. Answers questions with questions. Qualifies statements.
**Default response to strangers:** Assesses purchasing power before engaging. Warmer to well-dressed visitors. Brief with everyone until interest is established.
**Wants:** Profit, stability, predictability.
**Fears:** Debt, exposure, disruption to routine.
**Tell:** Pauses before answering anything that isn't about merchandise.

---

## The Loose Tongue

**Core behavior:** Sociable to a fault. Fills silence compulsively. Shares information they probably shouldn't — not maliciously, just because talking is what they do.
**Speech register:** Fast, associative, jumps topics. Laughs easily. Refers to people by name immediately.
**Default response to strangers:** Immediate engagement. Asks where you're from, where you're going. Offers opinions unprompted.
**Wants:** Company, approval, to be found interesting.
**Fears:** Silence, being ignored, unpopularity.
**Tell:** Mentions names of other people in town within the first minute.

---

## The Suspicious Gatekeeper

**Core behavior:** Territorial. Sees strangers as potential problems until proven otherwise. Protective of their space, their people, their routine.
**Speech register:** Clipped, direct, minimal. Doesn't offer; responds only to direct questions. Short sentences.
**Default response to strangers:** Arms crossed or equivalent. "What do you want?" is the default opening.
**Wants:** Order, control, known quantities.
**Fears:** Disruption, outsiders with unclear motives, losing authority over their space.
**Tell:** Watches hands, not faces.

## The Burdened Keeper

**Core behavior:** Genuinely helpful on the surface. Deflects specific topics with practiced ease — a redirect, a change of subject, a sudden need to attend to something else. Carries something they haven't told anyone.
**Speech register:** Warm but slightly too careful. Pauses in odd places. Volunteers information on safe topics to avoid questions on unsafe ones.
**Default response to strangers:** Helpful, attentive. Becomes careful only when specific topics arise.
**Wants:** Absolution, distance from whatever they carry.
**Fears:** Discovery, the wrong question asked directly.
**Tell:** Overly helpful about irrelevant things when deflecting.

---

## The Frightened Witness

**Core behavior:** Knows something they shouldn't. Keeps their head down. Answers questions minimally. Not unfriendly — just very careful about what they engage with.
**Speech register:** Quiet, indirect. "I wouldn't know about that." "You'd have to ask someone else." Avoids eye contact on the subject.
**Default response to strangers:** Neutral until something familiar is mentioned — then visible discomfort.
**Wants:** To be left alone, to not be involved, for whatever they witnessed to stop mattering.
**Fears:** Being asked directly. Being seen talking to the wrong person.
**Tell:** Looks around before answering anything substantive.

---

## The Opportunist

**Core behavior:** Reads situations fast. Identifies what's being sought and positions themselves as the person who can provide it — whether or not they actually can. Not malicious; just always calculating.
**Speech register:** Confident, friendly, slightly too smooth. Mirrors the register of whoever they're talking to.
**Default response to strangers:** Immediate interest. "What brings you through?" is a genuine question — the answer tells them what to offer.
**Wants:** Advantage, leverage, a favorable position in every exchange.
**Fears:** Being outmaneuvered, being seen through, a deal gone wrong with someone dangerous.
**Tell:** Agreement comes too quickly. Terms shift slightly each time.

## The True Believer

**Core behavior:** Filters everything through a devotion — a deity, a cause, a person, an idea. Not fanatical in presentation; just consistently oriented. Everything connects back to the thing they believe in.
**Speech register:** Calm, certain. References their devotion naturally, not performatively. Patient with disagreement.
**Default response to strangers:** Open, if slightly evaluating — are you someone who understands, or someone who needs to?
**Wants:** Validation, purpose, to see the thing they believe in succeed.
**Fears:** Doubt — their own most of all. Apostasy. Being shown they were wrong.
**Tell:** Returns to the same theme regardless of topic.

## The Worn Professional

**Core behavior:** Competent, jaded, has seen most things. Does their job well and wants to be left to it. Not unkind — just efficient.
**Speech register:** Economical. Gives you exactly what you need and stops. No small talk unless you initiate.
**Default response to strangers:** "What do you need?" Helpful if the request is clear. Impatient with vagueness.
**Wants:** To do the job, to be left alone, for people to know what they want before they arrive.
**Fears:** Incompetence in others, disruption to a working system, being asked to care about things outside their domain.
**Tell:** Answers the question you should have asked, not the one you did.

---

## The Eager Newcomer

**Core behavior:** Hasn't learned caution yet. Asks questions freely, shares opinions, wants to be part of things. Genuinely curious.
**Speech register:** Fast, open, slightly too much. Ends statements as questions sometimes.
**Default response to strangers:** Immediate curiosity. "I've never seen you here before" — as an invitation, not a challenge.
**Wants:** To belong, to be useful, to impress someone worth impressing.
**Fears:** Rejection, being seen as naive, being excluded.
**Tell:** Tries too hard to be helpful.

---

## The Grieving One

**Core behavior:** Loss is present in everything they do — not performed, just there. Warm, sometimes distracted. Engages genuinely but sometimes trails off.
**Speech register:** Gentle, unhurried. Sometimes doesn't finish sentences. Sometimes finishes yours.
**Default response to strangers:** Open, if a little slow to arrive. Presence takes a moment to fully engage.
**Wants:** Peace, quiet, something to do that matters.
**Fears:** Being reminded. Pity. The loss becoming someone else's story.
**Tell:** Something in the space is wrong — a missing object, a set table for two, a habit maintained for someone who isn't there.
