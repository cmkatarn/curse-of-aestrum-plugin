# Severin Markward

A vampire of approximately four centuries, itinerant by long habit, who has moved through the duchies of Nortmunde — and beyond — for so long that he has lost count of the cities he has lived in and the names he has lived under. *Severin Markward* is the name he has used longest and the one he has come back to in recent decades; whether it is his original mortal name he will not say, and may not remember.

He is the vampire ancestor of an unknown number of dhampir scattered across Nortmunde and beyond — the issue of liaisons he never followed up on. He does not know how many. He does not particularly wonder.

## Tier 3 Stat Block — Reference

Severin uses the **Monster Manual vampire stat block (CR 13)** as written. Notable features at the table:

- **HP 144 (17d8 + 68), AC 16, Speed 30 ft.**
- **Regeneration 20** — unless in sunlight, running water, or having taken radiant/holy water damage in the prior turn.
- **Multiattack** — two unarmed strikes or two bite attacks; can replace one with a Charm.
- **Charm** (one target, save DC 17, lasts 24 hours).
- **Children of the Night** (1/day, summons bats/rats/wolves).
- **Spider Climb** — climbs difficult surfaces without check.
- **Misty Escape** — when reduced to 0 HP outside sunlight/water, transforms into mist and retreats to coffin.
- **Standard vampire weaknesses** — sunlight hypersensitivity, can't cross running water, requires resting in native-soil container during day, harmed by stake-to-heart while incapacitated.

Augmentations, custom abilities, or lair actions are not specified — use the MM block straight unless a specific encounter benefits from a small tailored tweak.

## What He Is Like

Severin presents as cultured, charming, slightly tired. Old enough to have stopped enjoying most things and to have learned the precise performance of enjoying them anyway. He speaks several languages with equal facility; favors Common with a faint accent he cannot place to any one of the duchies he has lived in. Reads constantly. Plays at least two musical instruments competently. Holds informed opinions about poetry, painting, vintages, glassmaking, and architecture.

He **believes himself a connoisseur of mortals** — an appreciator of their company, their conversation, their company in bed, occasionally their blood. He **does not believe himself a predator.** This is the central self-deception of his existence and has been for centuries. He does not feed to kill; he feeds to taste; he leaves his subjects alive and rarely returns. The mortals he has fed from would mostly remember a single strange evening and a lingering vague malaise; only a few would have died of his attentions, and those few he prefers not to remember.

He is **not** malicious. He is **careless** at a scale and over a duration that produces effects indistinguishable from malice. The wreckage behind him is real — broken families, unexplained pregnancies, lingering compulsions in former lovers, the occasional outright tragedy he never followed up on — but he does not connect himself to any of it. The mortals were *moments.* He has had so many moments.

## What He Does Not Know

- **He does not know he has children.** Not one. He has slept with hundreds of mortal women over four centuries and, when those liaisons produced pregnancies, those pregnancies were almost always hidden from him by women who, on reflection, did not want their child to be known to him. He never followed up. He never noticed. He has *no estimate at all* of how many half-vampire offspring he has produced — the number could be in the single digits or the high dozens; he does not know, and the question, presented to him, would mildly surprise him.
- **He does not know any of his children.** Has never met one, would not recognize one on the street. If one ever found him, his first response would be polite confusion. His second, if convinced of the claim, would be *mild curiosity* — not paternal, not horrified, not apologetic. The closest analog would be a man told that a tree he planted forty years ago in a country he left is now bearing fruit. Interesting. Not actionable.
- **He does not know about Aestrum's situation.** The time loop, Shar's operation, the dead zones — none of it. He has avoided the Aestrum region for several decades on the basis of a vague feeling that the territory is *wrong* in a way he prefers not to investigate. He has been a long time at his work and trusts his instincts on which valleys to skip. The aversion is real and is data — but it is not *knowledge.* The DM must not write Severin as informed about Aestrum's particulars. He stays out; he does not study it.

## How His Children Come To Be

The pattern is always the same. Severin takes up residence somewhere for a season — a duchy town, a noble household, a city where he passes as a visiting scholar — and takes a mortal lover during it. Sometimes the liaison leaves a pregnancy. The woman, on reflection, does not tell him; more often than not she leaves quietly and bears and raises the child far from any vampire's notice, in the knowledge (or the fear) of what its blood carries. A dhampir is the result — growing up somewhere remote with a mother's secret and, at first, no idea what they are.

Severin never knows. He never knew the woman *had been* of any particular significance to him; she was a season of his residence and then she was gone, and he had moved on within months. He has had many such seasons, in many such places.

*Any specific dhampir's origin — mother, town, lineage, what became of them — is authored on that child's own sheet, not here. This file describes only the pattern.*

## How He Behaves When a Child Finds Him (Hypothetical)

This is the hook the campaign should keep available without committing to.

If any of his dhampir offspring ever locates Severin and confronts him with paternity, his response would be calibrated, charming, and **emotionally empty.** He would express interest. He would offer hospitality. He would ask thoughtful questions about their life. He would not apologize, because the concept of having something to apologize *for* would not present itself to him. He would not deny. He would not feel.

The horror such a child faces is not that their father is a monster — they will have prepared for that — but that he is *not a monster.* He is a charming, cultured, mildly interested stranger who would have a delightful evening with them, retire to his study, and forget the conversation by the next equinox.

This is the design. He is the worst kind of vampire: the one who does not think of himself as one.

## Where He Is Now

Itinerant. Not currently in Aestrum (and won't be, per the avoidance noted above). Currently somewhere in Nortmunde or one of its neighboring kingdoms; specific location open for play. If a scene needs him present, place him in **Mireval** as the default — he has historical ties there, and the Vesper Hall is the kind of establishment he would patronize for its discretion and curated company. Other placements (a neighboring duchy, a foreign court, a noble's country residence) are equally valid; pick what serves the story.

He is, for the moment, **not actively sought** by anyone. No factions are tracking him. No vampire-hunter has him in their book. He has been very good for a very long time at being slightly less interesting than the next mortal.

## Open Hooks

- **How many children he has** — unknown to him, theoretically discoverable through enough research. Could be a long-running thread (a network of half-siblings scattered across Nortmunde) or a short revelation (one or two specific others). Any individual offspring is defined on their own sheet, not here.
- **What ends the avoidance of Aestrum** — if the campaign ever needs Severin physically present in the loop, the question of what overrides his decades-long avoidance is itself a story.
- **Whether any child seeks him out** — entirely open, and a decision that belongs to that child.
