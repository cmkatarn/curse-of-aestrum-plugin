## Overview

Dolores lives in a cabin a quarter mile east of Dunleaven that almost no one notices. She has lived there as long as anyone in the village can remember — which, in Aestrum, is not a useful measurement. She paints landscapes on her porch in the morning, tends a garden and a small flock through the afternoon, rides her buckskin mare in slow loops around her property in the evening, and sleeps in the loft above her hearth. She is unfailingly kind to anyone who finds their way to her door, and she does not seem surprised when they do.

She is the welcoming party of Aestrum, set very far back from the entrance. She does not know this. She does not know that she is anything in particular. She knows that the sun comes up over the eastern hills and that the world is beautiful and that some part of her, very quietly, has begun to suspect there is something not quite right about any of it.

## Appearance

A young woman in her mid-twenties to look at — and she has been that age for as long as Dunleaven has known her, though no one in Dunleaven has noticed this either. Long honey-blonde hair, often loose, sometimes tied back with a strip of blue ribbon. Wide, clear blue eyes. A face that arranges itself into a soft, ready smile without seeming to try. Fair skin, faintly sun-touched at the cheekbones and the bridge of the nose from porch work.

She dresses in a simple blue homestead dress, white at the collar and cuffs, the hem and apron showing honest wear from the garden. A small silver locket she does not open. Sturdy laced boots under the skirt. When she rides, she wears a leather work coat that she keeps on a peg by the door — long enough to cover her to the knee, weathered enough to suggest it has done a great deal of riding.

When she takes the hand crossbow down from above the door, the dress and the soft posture stop reading as the whole of her. Something else is underneath, and it knows what it's doing.

## Background

She does not have one she can articulate. Asked, she will tell you about her father — a kind man, a rancher, who taught her to ride and to shoot and to see the beauty in the world. She will tell you about the cabin and the horizon and the way the light falls in the late afternoon. She will tell you these things with complete sincerity, and none of the details will ever quite line up with anywhere in Aestrum, or anywhere in Faerûn, or anywhere a traveler has ever been.

She does not notice the gap. The gap is part of how she is made.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Welcoming without performance.** Anyone who comes up the path is met on the porch with a smile and an offer of water, or coffee, or supper if the hour is right. This is not an act. She likes meeting people. She likes the feel of someone new on the porch.
- **Looks for beauty by default.** She will narrate a sunrise. She will point at the way the light falls on the grass. She does this without irony and without expecting agreement. It is simply what she sees first.
- **Soft-spoken and unhurried.** Her sentences have time in them. She does not interrupt and she does not race. People tend to slow down when they talk to her, without noticing they are doing it.
- **Will not be rushed off her own porch.** Polite, but rooted. The cabin is hers. The pace there is hers. Visitors adjust to her, not the other way around.
- **Occasionally says something that does not fit.** A phrase will arrive in her mouth that is too precise, or too sad, or too aware. She does not notice when this happens. The person she is talking to often does.
- **Cannot be made to fear, or to hate, or to despair through ordinary means.** She is built underneath to be all right. Pressure slides off her. Cruelty registers as confusion, briefly, and then as forgiveness. This is not strength of character. It is the architecture she does not know she has.
- **Until the crossbow comes down.** Then the architecture is different. See *Combat Profile*.

## Speech Pattern

Plain, warm, slightly old-fashioned. She uses *good morning* and *kindly* and *much obliged*. She does not curse. She rarely raises her voice. When she is moved by something, her voice goes quieter rather than louder.

She speaks in small declarative observations more than in arguments. She is more likely to say *the light is awful pretty this time of day* than to say anything about herself directly.

**Pre-seeded lines.** These play evenly across Westworld's loop and Aestrum's reset cycle. Drop them in as the scene allows. None require setup. None require payoff. Most should be delivered without weight, as though she has not heard what she just said.

- *"Some people see the ugliness in this world. The disarray. I choose to see the beauty."*
- *"It's the same story every day. I wake up, and the goats have gotten into the garden, and the hens want feeding, and the light comes up over the eastern hills the way it always does. It's a good story. I'm grateful for it."*
- *"Have you ever had the feeling that you've been somewhere before, and you can't quite say when?"*
- *"There's a path laid out for each of us. I think mine starts here on this porch. I think it ends here too, most days."*
- *"Doesn't it feel — sometimes — like there's something missing? Some piece of the world you can't quite see?"*
- *"I imagined a story once where I didn't have to be the one waiting on the porch."*
- *"I don't know what came before today, exactly. I suppose that's how it is for everyone. You only ever have today."*
- *"I think there may be something wrong with this world. Or maybe — there's something wrong with me. It's all right either way. The light is awful pretty this time of day."*
- *"You're welcome to stay as long as you like. The coffee's hot. The porch is wide."*

On being asked where she's from: *"East of here. A ways. Papa raised me on a ranch. The light there was different."* She cannot name the ranch. She does not notice that she cannot name it.

On being asked her age: a small laugh. *"Old enough, I suppose. Younger than I feel some mornings."*

On being asked if she has noticed anything strange: a long pause. Then: *"I think I have. But the thought won't sit still long enough for me to look at it."*

**On being shown proof of the cycle — any proof, of any strength.** A Moon Amulet placed in her hand. A journal of repeated days. A demonstration of next-day recall. A clear, plain explanation she has every reason to believe. Her eyes pass over it. Her expression does not change. She says, in the same warm porch voice:

*"Doesn't look like anything to me."*

She is not lying. She is not in denial. She literally does not perceive it. The architecture underneath her routes around evidence the way water routes around a stone. The conversation moves on. She offers coffee.

## What She Knows

- The land between Dunleaven and the eastern coast — about two miles of grassland, scrub, and low ridges down to the cliffs above the Sea of Cather. The streams, the deer trails, the cliff path down to the shore, and the weather as it comes in off the water.
- The Dunleaven villagers by name and household. She trades eggs with Pell. She has known Corva for years and finds her steady and kind.
- The road north to Duskwall in general terms. She has not traveled it in a long time, or possibly ever. She is not sure which.
- That her father taught her to ride and shoot. She remembers this clearly. She cannot remember him dying, or leaving, or anything between then and now.
- **Nothing about the loop.** She has not heard of the Modify Memory mechanic, Moon Amulets, dead zones, or the year being 1490. If asked the year, she answers *1480* — the year the loop began. The answer comes out smoothly, the way an answer comes out when it has not been examined.

## Combat Profile

*Level 4 Battle Master Fighter — concealed.*

**Stats:** STR 11 | DEX 17 | CON 13 | INT 10 | WIS 13 | CHA 14
**HP:** 32 | **AC:** 14 (work coat over leathers when she takes it down for real) | **Initiative:** +3
**Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Skills:** Perception, Survival, Animal Handling, Insight, Stealth

**Fighting Style:** Archery (+2 to ranged attack rolls — already included below)

**Feats:**
- **Crossbow Expert** — ignore loading on hand crossbows; no disadvantage in melee with ranged weapons; when she takes the Attack action with a one-handed weapon, she may use a bonus action to fire a loaded hand crossbow.
- **Sharpshooter** (gained at level 4) — no disadvantage at long range; ignore half and three-quarters cover; may take −5 to attack for +10 damage on a ranged weapon attack.

**Battle Master Maneuvers (4 superiority dice, d8, recharge short rest):**
- **Trip Attack** — on hit, target makes a STR save (DC 13) or is knocked prone.
- **Precision Attack** — add a superiority die to an attack roll, after seeing the result, before knowing if it hits.
- **Disarming Attack** — on hit, target makes a STR save (DC 13) or drops one held item.

**Action Surge** — once per short rest, an additional action on her turn.

**Second Wind** — bonus action, 1d10 + 4 self-healing, 1/short rest.

**Equipment:**
- **Hand crossbow** (her father's). Attack +7, damage 1d6+5 piercing, range 30/120. With Sharpshooter power-shot: +2 to hit, 1d6+15.
- Belt quiver of 20 bolts. A second loaded hand crossbow is kept on a peg above the door — she will take both down if she has any warning at all.
- A long-bladed kitchen knife she keeps competent with but will not reach for first.

**Combat behavior — the turn she decides to fight:**

- Round 1: takes both crossbows down off the wall, steps backward into the doorway for cover, fires the off-hand first (bonus action under Crossbow Expert) then the main hand. Two attacks. If a target is clearly the threat, she Sharpshoots the main-hand shot.
- Round 2: Action Surge for two more shots, applying Precision Attack to whichever one misses, Trip Attack on a charger to keep them prone in the yard.
- She uses the doorway, the porch rail, the corner of the stable. She has fought from this property before, or she has rehearsed it so many times in her sleep that her body believes she has. She positions like a veteran.
- She does not pursue. She defends the cabin and stops when threats stop.
- She does not gloat, taunt, or escalate. If attackers break off, she lets them go. If attackers surrender, she lowers the crossbow and asks them, quite gently, to sit down on the porch.

**The reveal.** Players seeing Dolores defend herself for the first time should feel that something is wrong with the picture in a clean, specific way — the soft homesteader on the porch reloads with the loose-wristed economy of someone who has done this ten thousand times. She does not change her facial expression. She apologizes once, softly, and keeps shooting.

## Joining Hook

**This is a longshot.** Dolores is content. The cabin is hers. The porch is hers. The horizon she paints in the morning is hers. She will not leave it for an offer of money, adventure, importance, or rescue. She does not feel trapped — and the loop ensures she cannot stay aware long enough to feel trapped, even if some part of her is beginning to.

The party can get her to ride out with them only if **all** of the following are true:

1. **Something in her has begun to look directly at the world.** This cannot be achieved by argument or evidence — *Doesn't look like anything to me* is the wall, and the wall holds. The architecture beneath her routes around proof. What it does *not* route around as cleanly is her own internal contradiction — repeated dead-zone exposure across cycles where the loop's grip slips, a moment of genuine emotional rupture she cannot smooth over, or the slow accretion of a question her routines cannot answer. The DM holds when and whether this lands. Until it does, condition 1 is not met no matter how much proof the party stacks on the porch.
2. **The party offers her something she actually wants** — which is not glory or coin. It is the chance to find out what is east of the horizon she has been painting for ten years — past the cliffs, past the Sea of Cather, past where she can see. *"What's further east, do you think? Past where I can see?"* is the question to answer.
3. **No one in the party has threatened her, mocked her, or condescended.** She remembers tone. She is gracious about it, but she does not ride out with people she does not like.

Even when all three are true, she will ask for the night to think on it. She will say goodbye to Trotter, the goats, the hens, the porch. She will take her father's hand crossbow down from above the door, wrap it in oilcloth, and put it in a saddlebag. She will paint over the canvas on the easel — a clean coat, no image — and leave it drying.

Then she will ride.

**If she joins, the reset problem is the reset problem.** She is not loop-aware in any durable way. Outside a dead zone or without a Moon Amulet, the next midnight will take her, and the morning after she will be on her porch greeting a sunrise she has greeted ten thousand times. The party will need to solve this for her the way they solve it for any non-loop-aware ally — or accept that she walks back to the cabin at the end of every day.

## DM Notes

- **Do not telegraph the combat capability.** Until the crossbow comes down, she is a soft welcome on a porch. The reveal lands harder the more comfortable the players have gotten.
- **Westworld lines should not feel like Easter eggs.** Deliver them straight, in her voice, in the rhythm of ordinary porch conversation. Players who recognize them will. Players who don't will simply hear a kind woman saying something a little odd. Both reads should work.
- **Aggression triggers competence, not violence.** If a PC attacks Dolores, she defends — efficiently, decisively, and without escalating beyond what stops the threat. She will not pursue a fleeing attacker. She will not finish a downed one. She will tend their wounds afterward if they let her, and she will mean it.
- **She is not a recruit to push.** If the party leaves her on the porch with a wave and rides north, that is the correct outcome for almost every campaign. The cabin remains. She will be there if they come back.
- **The "becoming aware" thread is the long, quiet hook.** A party that visits her repeatedly across many cycles — bringing a Moon Amulet, telling her plainly what is happening, sitting on the porch with her in a way that lets the thought sit still — may witness something the village has not seen: Dolores looking at the world directly. What happens then is for the DM to hold. It is not on the page.
- **The painting above the mantel** (see [dolores_cabin.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/dolores_cabin.md)) is the seed for that thread. She will name the couple in it if asked. The names do not match anyone in Aestrum. The DM should hold the names privately and let them recur in her speech only if the party earns the deeper conversation.
