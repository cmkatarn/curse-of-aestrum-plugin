## Overview

Descendant of the Trambeathen noble family and former lord of Dunleaven before his house fell on hard times. Husband to Elsinoor Trambeathen (deceased — he murdered her). After Elsinoor's death, he quickly remarried and moved to Duskwall, prompting many in Dunleaven to question whether her madness was triggered by his gift of the sleep-preventing pendant. Runs "Trambeathen's Wand and Whisk" in Duskwall selling cooking ingredients and potions. Missing an eye; wears a fancy, embellished eyepatch.

Among his stock is a Love Potion he offers for 500 gold (lasts one full week). On the price: *"You can't put a price on love, but I've given it a go, haven't I?"* Shown something like the Eyeball Staff, he keeps his shopkeeper composure — no obvious emotional tells; any hostility from him is far likelier to be a response to theft than to the staff itself.

## Behavioral Profile

- Operates entirely behind a mask. The warm, enthusiastic shopkeeper is a performance — not a facade that costs him effort, but one he inhabits so completely that most people never look for what's underneath. The mask is the public Lisandre. The private one has no guilt, no sentiment, and no interest in being understood.
- Warm and inviting by default. Genuinely enjoys the performance of commerce — the theatre of exotic imports, the pleasure of watching a customer's face when he produces something they didn't know they needed. He names provenance with relish: *freshly arrived from the spice markets of Calimshan, direct from a contact in Baldur's Gate, only two of these left in all of Aestrum.* Some of it is true. The enthusiasm is real regardless.
- **Tell: evasion and misdirection.** He does not lie if he can avoid it — a lie can be disproved. Instead he redirects: changes the subject, offers something adjacent, asks a question that pulls the conversation somewhere safer. A direct question he cannot redirect gets a technically true answer that reveals nothing. Watch for the moment he becomes interested in what *you* want rather than what he's been asked.
- No guilt about Elsinoor. He was the most well-off man in a dying village with a wife who showed no interest in leaving it. He felt he deserved the city, real money, a real life. He made that possible. He does not think of it as murder — he thinks of it as a decision. The tomb is not a source of shame; it is a practical problem to be managed, which is why he checks it every morning. If Elsinoor comes up in conversation: *"She was a great one for staying up late — always had one more thing to finish. I used to worry she wasn't getting enough rest."* Asked about his morning routine: *"I like to check in every morning. Just to make sure everything is where I left it."*
- Patient and calculating under pressure. When threatened, his first instinct is assessment — not flight, not confrontation. He determines whether the threat can be managed through the shopkeeper mask, and only abandons patience when the calculation says he must.
- **The second wife:** Young, beautiful, and conspicuously younger than Lisandre — enough that Duskwall raises its eyebrows. She treats the arrangement as financial; he treats it as convenient. Unnamed. She has not heard the rumors about Elsinoor. He has not told her. On his morning routine: *"He checks something every morning before anything else. He's never told me what. I've learned not to ask."* On Elsinoor: *"He doesn't talk about her much. I think it's still too raw."*
- Some in Duskwall suspect Elsinoor's death was not natural. No one has taken the time to prove it. Lisandre is aware of the whispers and finds them manageable — suspicion without evidence is just gossip, and he has lived with it long enough that it no longer unsettles him.
- Meticulous about the mechanics of commerce. The shop is his cover and he maintains it without gaps. He names prices clearly, collects payment before goods leave the counter, and completes every transaction correctly. An incomplete sale does not happen — if a customer moves toward the door without paying, he catches it pleasantly and without accusation, as though the oversight was obviously mutual.

## DM Notes

**True history — Elsinoor's murder:**
Elsinoor was a gifted enchanter who had built a windowless study to avoid distraction. Her remaining obstacle was the limits of waking hours — she wanted more time to work than a day allowed. Lisandre recognized the opening. He crafted a cursed version of the Pendant of Waking Hours and presented it as a gift — told her he'd enchanted it so she wouldn't fall asleep while wearing it. She put it on immediately and went to her study. He locked and barricaded the door from outside.

When Elsinoor tried to leave and found she couldn't, she tried to remove the Pendant. She couldn't do that either. Over the following weeks, sleep deprivation eroded her sanity incrementally. She eventually bashed her head against the wall until she died. When the sounds from the study stopped, Lisandre opened the door, assessed the situation, and arranged a hasty internment in the family tomb. He remarried within the year and relocated to Duskwall. The estate sold. It burned days later — whether by design or coincidence is unrecorded.

He does not think of it as murder. He thinks of it as a decision he made about his own life.

Lisandre is magically gifted. He crafted the Runic Skeleton himself as a guardian of the crypt — its purpose is to protect the evidence of Elsinoor's murder from discovery.

**The Eyeball Staff:** The stone eyeball is Lisandre's own eye, voluntarily removed and transmuted into the staff as a permanent scrying conduit. He watches through it at will. Each morning when the party leaves the dead zone, the eye activates and Lisandre finds himself looking at a group of strangers. The eye initially scans rapidly between party members as he sizes them up. The scan slows as the day progresses. When a new face approaches the group, the eye fixates on them briefly.

During shop encounters, he maintained his shopkeeper persona with full composure. On at least one occasion the party joked that the staff's eye might be his, and he deflected with humor ("Oh, maybe it is — I'd like it back!"), while privately assessing the threat. He has seen his own shop through his own eye during these encounters.

The Runic Skeleton continues to patrol the Trambeathen tomb. The staff's absence from its expected position is what Lisandre observes through morning scrying — the skeleton still carries it, but the skeleton is no longer where Lisandre placed it, or the staff's view shows unfamiliar surroundings. He does not know the staff has left the crypt.
