## Overview

The leader of the criminal underworld in Duskwall and head of The Shrikes. His true identity is Lucretius Tallwood — one of Evandur's brothers, believed killed when *The Survivor* was destroyed at their father's funeral. He survived the explosion and has since built a criminal empire in Duskwall, operating under a constructed identity using the Mirror of Illusion to adopt a disguise whenever conducting business.

Lucretius was blown clear of *The Survivor* by the explosion (1477 DR). He is now heavily scarred. In his true form he wanders Duskwall as a beggar; no one who knew the young Lucretius would recognize him. He retains no stored voice in the mirror — his voice is always his own.

As the surviving legitimate heir to Aestrum's dukedom, he holds Aestrum's ducal vote in the Nortmunde succession.

## Background with the Party

Confronted the party after their escape from the city lockup at a waterfront warehouse. Conscripted the party into the vault heist, offering 30% of the loot; made clear refusal was not an option. The assassination of Duke Evandur Tallwood is personal — a fratricidal death to mirror the one Evandur intended for him.

Galadiil Ilphekiir has no idea Luca is Lucretius. She arrived in Aestrum after *The Survivor* incident and never met the younger Tallwood brothers.

## Behavioral Profile

- Transactional in all relationships. Models people as tools with functions. If someone fits the model, he works with them. If they don't, he exploits them.
- Has known and lost several loves because of this. He is aware of the pattern and has not changed it.
- Knew Galadiil would never assist an assassination; deliberately framed the entire operation as a heist and worked hard to maintain that frame. He does not operate by accident.
- An opportunist, not a planner in the abstract. He builds plans around what appears. He recognized the alchemist's fire the moment it arrived and moved to acquire it.
- **Chapter 1 goal:** Evandur dead. The method is secondary. He will work with anyone temporarily if they advance that goal.
- **Chapter 2 goal:** Sovereign rule of Aestrum — without Setland's hand on it. His curse is not that he always needs someone dead; it is that someone is always standing between him and what is rightfully his. The obstacle changes. The pattern doesn't.
- Will not break the Setland arrangement openly until he has a clear path to survive doing so.
- Will use the party as a tool toward removing Setland's hold without disclosing the full geometry of his situation.
- **Memory:** Loop-bound. Does not remember the party unless they interact with him on the day the cycle breaks. The party knows him; he is meeting them cold. This asymmetry runs in their favor if used carefully.

## DM Notes

**Chapter 2 arc:**
When Evandur dies, Luca is elated. Then a Setland attaché arrives with Quellenna Ilphekiir's terms: *We know you have a rightful claim. There is no way in the nine hells you take that throne without our help. Here are our terms.* Setland recognition of his claim. Aestrum's vote goes to Malak in the succession. A Setland garrison remains in Aestrum during the transition "for stability." The dream arrives and is mortgaged before he can exhale.

He accepts — not because he has surrendered, but because he is a survivor who has already lived decades on worse odds. He is calculating his next move before the attaché leaves the room.

He becomes Duke of Aestrum. Malak's soldiers are in his castle. His vote is committed. The thing he organized his whole life to claim is finally his — and someone else is holding the deed.

He does not know Quellenna is Shar's instrument. He believes his constraint is political — Setland's military presence and Malak's backing.

**The alchemist's fire:** Luca purchased approximately 256 vials from Rylin T'Sarran using an elderly-man Mirror disguise. His plan: smuggle them into the bowels of Duskwall Castle and kill Evandur in a poetic echo of the fire that was supposed to have killed Lucretius himself on The Survivor.

**Miklas connection:** Luca identified and recruited Miklas of Thundertree — the deranged former vizier who designed the Castle Vault maze — hoping his knowledge would provide a way through. Miklas provided a "map" that proved useless. Luca had him killed and displayed in Shrike fashion.

**Vote mechanics:**
- If party assists Luca and keeps him alive through the Setland invasion: he sides with them against Setland and casts Aestrum's vote for Amblecrown.
- If party does not assist him: Setland's dominance forces his hand — he votes Malak du Leon under duress.

## Recognition and the Daily Hand-off

`recognition_state: disguised_daily`. **Nobody can identify Luca on sight** — not his
own lieutenants, not the party, not a stranger in the street. Each morning he takes a
new appearance with the [Mirror of Illusion]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/items/chapter_1/mirror_of_illusion.md)
and presents himself to his lieutenants to be authenticated.

**The hand-off.** He arrives with the aura the position carries, and confirms he is the
genuine article with a credential — a trinket produced, a passcode given, a
call-and-response completed. His **voice is always his own**; the Mirror stores none,
so the voice is a standing tell the disguise never covers.

**What the protocol is actually for.** Not *"we cannot tell who you are"* — a lieutenant
who has heard him has the voice. It exists because **anyone holding an illusion could
stand in that spot and claim to be him.** It is an anti-impersonation credential, and
that makes it:

- **observable** — a party who watches a hand-off can learn the shape of it;
- **forgeable** — a party who learns the day's credential can be Luca to a lieutenant;
- **interceptable** — reach the lieutenants before he does and the day's chain of
  command is yours;
- **fragile** — if the Mirror is ever taken from him, the disguise ends and the
  protocol has nothing left to authenticate. He would have to run the Shrikes as a
  scarred beggar whose face nobody has been permitted to see.

**Narration discipline.** While he is disguised, **never name him through the
disguise** — not in narration, not in another character's thought. He is whoever the
Mirror is showing until someone in the fiction has grounds to know otherwise. The
party's own knowledge of his identity is theirs; it does not license the narrator to
identify him for a character who lacks it.
