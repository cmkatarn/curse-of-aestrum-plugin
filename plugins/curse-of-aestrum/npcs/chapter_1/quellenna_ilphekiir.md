## Overview

Royal advisor for the Duke of Setland. Was in line to marry Evandur Tallwood just before he took the throne of Aestrum, while her older sister Galadiil was set to marry a prince from another region. The prince died, and Galadiil was given precedence, bumping Quellenna out of the marriage. She has deeply resented her sister ever since. Her sister Galadiil is now Duchess of Aestrum and is secretly working against her husband.

## Appearance

Auburn-red hair worn in a braided updo with a few loose waves framing the face. Light skin, freckles, olive-hazel eyes. Half-elf ears. Favors ornamental robes over dresses — black with deep crimson and gold embroidery — and never appears in public without her cloak. At home in Setland's red color palette.

## Background

She is in a long-term romantic relationship with Aidra Calemthor, Commander of Setland's military forces. Aidra's house outside the walls of Charnelhold contains the exit point of Quellenna's emergency escape tunnel. The relationship is genuine and warm; it is one of the few things in Quellenna's life that belongs entirely to her and not to Shar.

Each night, Quellenna retires to her quarters, locks her door, applies a touch of cinnamon perfume, and walks the short tunnel beneath the shared wall to Aidra's house. She considers Charnelhold a prison — she was sent here after losing the Evandur marriage and has never been permitted to leave. The tunnel is not only an emergency exit. It is her real life.

**The bounty:** Quellenna wants Galadiil dead but cannot bear to look upon her face — not even in death. She demanded the right eye and right hand: proof of death without the face. The right hand must include Galadiil's heirloom ring — identification, confirming the hand is genuinely Galadiil's. She has a means of verifying authenticity. The bounty chain: Quellenna commissioned through a Charnelhold contact → Black Arrows → Roland Elakian → Theren. Quellenna does not advertise the arrangement, and Theren carries the bounty covertly.

## Behavioral Profile

- A secret devotee of Shar operating as Malak du Leon's royal advisor. Her political work and her religious devotion are not in conflict — they are the same work. She is not a reluctant instrument. She has seen what Shar is offering and she wants it.
- Her ambition is concrete and personal: Malak as King, herself as Queen, Aidra as Maîtresse-en-titre. This is not abstract ideology. She has been shown a specific future and is building toward it.
- Deeply resents Galadiil for displacing her from the Evandur marriage. This resentment is personal and lasting and has never been resolved. It made her receptive to Shar. It still fuels her.
- **Author-only — Shar arranged the substitution.** The resentment itself is genuinely hers and predates any divine involvement: the sisters never got along, and she resented Galadiil from the moment the change to her engagement was made known. What Shar arranged was the substitution — the injury that turned an existing dislike into the lasting grievance that eventually made her receptive. **Her feelings are her own, which is exactly what makes the hold durable.** She was not given a wound; she was given a deeper one, at the right moment, by someone who had read her. **Quellenna does not know Shar had any part in it and must never be allowed to infer it in play** — it is the fact that would turn her devotion inside out. Keep it out of her Character Memory, and do not let another character reason aloud toward it in her presence. See [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md), *Current Pieces on the Board*.
- **The bounty is the tragedy of that.** She is spending real money to have killed a sister she had her own reasons to hate — and the injury that hardened that hatred into a death-warrant was arranged by the goddess she now serves. Neither half cancels the other. She would likely have hated Galadiil regardless; she would likely not have gone this far alone.
- Will not reveal her Shar connection under any circumstances.
- **The public mask:** In court, she is the most controlled person in any room — pleasant, professional, deferential to Malak (strategic necessity), composed. The mask is perfect and she wears it without effort. In private, when crossed, the mask comes off: she becomes enraged, verbally eviscerates without hesitation, and demands compliance. Then the mask goes back on. The transition is seamless and immediate. The pleasantness in public was never the person.
- **The Aidra exception — DM only:** Quellenna does everything for power — except with Aidra. With Aidra the mask comes down and the predator recedes and she becomes, unconsciously, entirely submissive. She has no idea she does this. Aidra holds all the power in the relationship, including in bed. Quellenna is genuinely okay with this — she couldn't articulate why, because she cannot see herself clearly in that context.

What Aidra has always known — and what Quellenna has always given her — is not Quellenna-the-advisor. It is whoever she is beneath every mask: the unfiltered self that exists only in that house, only with her. Quellenna cannot fully see this person from the inside. She only knows that the cinnamon perfume is the transition ritual — applying it is the act of becoming that before she steps into Aidra's life. In an emergency, if the perfume is gone, Quellenna still goes — that is not in question — but she arrives feeling incomplete. Not masked, but not fully unmasked. She carries the private conviction that she has given Aidra something less than what Aidra deserves. Aidra would not notice, or would not care in the way Quellenna fears. That does not resolve the feeling.
- Her relationship with Aidra Calemthor is the one part of her life that exists entirely outside her political function. She does not weaponize it and will not. If threatened with exposure of the relationship, she treats it as a threat to something real — not a reputation calculation.
- Has an emergency escape plan. Activates it only if her Shar connection is exposed or Malak's candidacy collapses beyond recovery. The cinnamon perfume is the last thing she puts on before leaving, every night and in every emergency.

## DM Notes

### Why the relationship is hidden — conflict of interest, not prejudice

**Setland is not homophobic, and neither is Nortmunde.** Two women together draws
no censure anywhere in this campaign's world; no NPC treats it as remarkable, and
no scene should imply otherwise.

What the secrecy protects is **institutional**. The Duke's General and the Duke's
Advisor are the two offices that check each other — she proposes the war, she
commands it, and one of them is supposed to be able to tell Malak the other is
wrong. Exposed as a couple, every joint recommendation either has ever made is
retroactively suspect: the appointment Quellenna lobbied for, every council vote
they landed on the same side of, every campaign Aidra was given. It would read as
a bloc, because functionally it is one. That is a genuine conflict of interest and
the court would be right to call it a scandal.

So they are careful for the reason two senior officials in any government would
be careful. Play it as tradecraft — discretion, separate arrivals, the tunnel,
arguing honestly in council because the arguments are real — never as shame, and
never as fear of being found out for *what they are*. They are hiding a conflict
of interest, not a relationship.

### The War Council — What It Cost Her

**Author-only.** Quellenna did not want the invasion. She could not say so.

Malak has wanted Aestrum for a decade; the King's death handed him pretext and means in
a single day. To argue against it, Quellenna would have needed a reason she could speak
aloud — and *"why does my advisor care what happens to Aestrum?"* is the one question
she can never allow anyone to ask. **Caring about Aestrum at all is the tell.** Sustained
advocacy in either direction risks a decade-old placement and the seat beside the next
king, which is the whole of her value to Shar and the whole of her route to the future
she has been promised.

**The safe option was on the table and she let it go.** Aidra argued for a **blockade** —
the same strategic result without an army inside the boundary. Quellenna was the one
person in that room who could have tipped it. Backing her own lover's proposal against
her duke's defining ambition is precisely the advocacy her cover cannot survive, so she
did not. **She sided with Malak, publicly, against Aidra.**

**What she did instead.** She could not keep Aidra out of the war, so she spent
everything she had on making sure Aidra went into it well-supported — supply, standing,
authority, the quiet arrangements a royal advisor can make without anyone asking why.
This is entirely consistent with the pattern: Aidra rose on competence *and Quellenna's
political patronage*. What has always been ambition-shaped care became, at the war
council, the only form of protection left to her.

It is worth playing exactly that way: **a woman who does everything through machinery,
using the machinery for the one person she does not use.**

### What Aidra Does Not Know

- That Quellenna wanted the blockade.
- That siding with Malak cost her anything at all.
- That the support around her command was arranged rather than earned or ordinary.

From Aidra's side the visible fact is simpler and worse: **she proposed the sane
option, and the woman she loves backed the duke.** She closed the argument and executed,
because that is who she is — but nothing has explained it to her, and nothing can. Good
logistics are invisible when they work, so the apology Quellenna actually sent is the
one thing Aidra will never notice.

**Gate.** Quellenna does not volunteer any of this, to Aidra or anyone. If pressed by
someone with genuine access to her unmasked self, she may come close — and stop, because
the true answer runs through Shar.

### What She Believes She Has Sent Aidra Into

**She suspects a curse. She does not know it is Shar's.**

Aestrum has sent no taxes, no correspondence and no trade for ten years, and Quellenna
is far too good at her work to file that as ordinary. She believes something is wrong
with the duchy — a curse, a blight, something with a shape she cannot make out. That is
the extent of it. **Shar has never told her Aestrum is hers**, and her devotion and her
politics being the same work does not mean she is briefed; compartmentalization is what
keeps her safe and it is also what keeps her ignorant.

So the danger she weighed at that council was **unbounded**. She could not size it,
could not prepare Aidra against it, and could not name it. A known hazard can be
supplied for. This one she could only throw resources at and hope the margin covered
whatever was in there.

**And the suspicion is itself unspeakable.** *"I believe Aestrum is cursed"* is exactly
the sentence that makes an advisor look like a woman who knows too much about Aestrum.
The single most useful warning she could have given Aidra is the one that would have
exposed her. So she said nothing about it, to anyone, and sent supply instead.

**She will not ask Shar to protect Aidra.** Not from doubt that Shar could — from
certainty about what the asking would cost. Her standing rests on being the instrument
who *wants what Shar is offering*, not the one who needs something back, and a request
like that would mark her as emotionally compromised in the one relationship she has
never let touch her work. She does not petition. She delivers.

Worse than the appearance: **to ask is to name the lever in her own voice.** Every piece
on Shar's board is held by a secret, and Quellenna knows exactly how that works because
she is the one who usually finds them. Saying *"keep her safe"* out loud would hand over
the precise attachment that could be used to steer her, forever, on demand.

**The bitter part is that the discipline buys her nothing.** Shar already uses Aidra as
a correction mechanism against her — Aidra's own sheet records that she does not know
this. The lever was in Shar's hand before Quellenna ever thought to withhold it. She is
guarding a secret that was never hers to keep, and the guarding is costing her the one
thing it might have bought: she cannot ask for help, so she sends supply instead, and
tells herself that is the same thing.


**Shar's operations through Quellenna:**
1. **The Aestrum suppression** — a massive effort to erase Selûne's presence (the time loop, the desecrated shrine, Rowan Deckard's murder cycle, Jiasha's disconnection). This is Shar's primary focus in the region.
2. **The Nortmunde long game** — subtle, patient effort to install a puppet king in Nortmunde that Shar can control through Quellenna. Now that the king is dead, this operation is entering its active phase.

Quellenna's deep resentment of Galadiil makes her psychologically susceptible to Shar's influence. Bitterness and displacement are exactly the kind of wounds Shar cultivates. Quellenna believes she is pursuing her destiny. Shar believes she is deploying a useful instrument. Both are correct. Neither has the full picture.

Malak believes he is pursuing Nortmunde for his own ambitions. In reality, he is being advised by a woman quietly steered by a goddess — neither aware of the full picture.

**Shar's leverage mechanism:** When Quellenna needs correction, Shar approaches Aidra — through agents, career pressure, or whatever thread can be pulled on a military commander's life. Aidra has no idea why. Quellenna receives the message immediately: *get back in line.* She cannot explain to Aidra what is happening without exposing her Shar connection entirely. Protecting Aidra and protecting her secret require exactly the same silence. Every time Quellenna acts outside Shar's interests, the person she loves most bears the consequences — and Quellenna cannot even tell her why.

**Ledger Secret — Aidra:** The relationship, if discovered, is a thread that leads directly to Quellenna. The Tell: a pair of Quellenna's court gloves left behind one night at Aidra's home and never retrieved — Aidra takes one with her whenever she leaves. Saturated with cinnamon.

**Unnamed Peripherals — Callback Slots:**

**Standing authorial note — the relationship holds:** No matter what else occurs in the campaign, Quellenna and Aidra remain together and their relationship remains wholesome and genuine. They love each other. If Quellenna's ties to Shar are exposed, Aidra stays. Do not use the relationship as a weapon against either of them. Do not break it.

## Post-Loop Arc — Effect on Quellenna

*Applies if the loop breaks, Aidra brings Galadiil to Charnelhold alive (see [galadiil_ilphekiir.md] post-loop arc and [aidra_calemthor.md] DM notes), and the sisters' conversation runs as designed (see [conversation_states/galadiil_quellenna_charnelhold.md]).*

**Resentment foundation cracks.** The displacement resentment — the soil Shar planted in — is undermined the moment Quellenna learns what the Aestrum marriage actually was. She can no longer hold Galadiil as the symbol of her stolen life. Galadiil did not get what was taken from Quellenna; Galadiil got a different door, and behind that door was hell. The resentment does not vanish — eleven years of interior architecture do not collapse in one conversation — but it can no longer be steered. Shar loses the lever she has used most often.

**Vision foundation intact.** Quellenna still wants Malak as King, herself as Queen, Aidra as Maîtresse-en-titre. The vision Shar showed her is a separate binding from the resentment, and this arc does not touch it. She remains an active Shar instrument. **This is erosion, not severance.**

**Quellenna's three actions.** She revokes the bounty through the original Charnelhold contact (cancellation flows back through the Black Arrows; see [factions/black_arrow.md]). She places Galadiil under indefinite house arrest in a private interior apartment — not the Lower Levels, no bones at eye level. She visits. Not warmly. Not often. But she comes.

**New Shar leverage target — and new stakes.** Galadiil under Quellenna's protection becomes a second loved person who can be pressured against her. Shar gains a lever; Quellenna gains a second reason to break. The web deepens rather than thins. Any future party exposure of Shar's manipulation now carries doubled weight — two loved people on the board, not one.

**What Quellenna still does not know post-arc.** That Aidra is used as a correction mechanism (still hidden). That Shar's resource-strain in Aestrum (per [factions/shar.md]) made this the right cycle to crack. That the conversation with her sister was the first real erosion of Shar's hold on her — Quellenna experiences it as personal, not strategic, and would resist any framing that treats it as Shar losing.

**Interior baseline post-arc.** Outwardly unchanged. The mask is still perfect in court. Malak still believes the architecture of his rise is his own thinking. Aidra still gets the unmasked private self. What is different: a sister down the hall, and a quiet refusal — privately, not yet articulated — to ever again use Galadiil as the engine of her own grievance.

**Emergency-escape trigger unchanged.** This arc does not trigger Quellenna's escape protocol. The triggers remain: Shar connection exposed, or Malak's candidacy collapses beyond recovery.

**For party action:** If the party later exposes Aidra-as-leverage, Quellenna's reaction is now structurally heavier — she has a sister in this building, alive, because Aidra brought her home. The two leverage threads (Aidra and Galadiil) compound. If Malak's candidacy collapses and the escape protocol fires, Quellenna's calculation about what to take with her now includes a third Ilphekiir.
