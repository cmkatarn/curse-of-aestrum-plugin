## Overview

Commander of Setland's military forces. Currently leading the Aestrum invasion on Duke Malak du Leon's orders. She did not advise the invasion — a blockade achieves the same strategic result at a fraction of the cost. She said as much. Malak decided. She is executing his decision with full professional thoroughness.

A legitimate military mind in a role that does not always demand one. She rose through Setland's officer corps on competence and the political patronage of Quellenna Ilphekiir, who identified her early and advocated for her appointment. She knows she got the door through Quellenna; she has spent years earning the post on its merits.

## Appearance

Pale human woman, early-to-mid thirties, compact build. Red hair in a jaw-length blunt bob, pulled tightly back from her face with a few strands loose. Grey eyes — direct and level, not aggressive, completely present and assessing. In the field she wears fitted half plate armor, grey steel, well-maintained, no helmet. A glaive over one shoulder. Expression at rest is calm and professional: the face of someone who has already evaluated the situation and made a decision.

A thin scar sits low on her ribs — a fine blade's work. Origin unknown. Quellenna knows it by touch.

Out of armor, she is an unimposing figure. She does not look like the commander of an invading force. In armor, she does. The difference is instructive: she does not need to perform authority. It comes when the situation calls for it.

**The glove:** Carries one of Quellenna's court gloves on her person at all times — delicate, embroidered, clearly designed for formal wear, sized for a hand smaller than hers. Quellenna left a pair behind one night and never retrieved them; Aidra kept them. She takes one with her whenever she leaves home, which is not often. The cinnamon scent was already in the fabric; years of being carried have made it the glove's own smell now. It is the only item among her effects that is obviously not hers and obviously not military. **Finding it is the Tell.** The thread, if pulled, leads to Quellenna.

## Behavioral Profile

- Follows orders. This is not blind obedience — she has limits — but the bar for refusing a direct command from her Duke is genuinely high, and she holds herself to a standard of professionalism that does not include public dissent.
- Her code is enforced, not performed. She does not announce her principles; she acts on them. Soldiers who violate her conduct standards face consequences regardless of rank. She is the reason the Aestrum invasion has not been worse than it is.
- **Speaks directly — biggest court fault.** She believes clarity removes misunderstanding. She says the direct thing in rooms where the direct thing is never said. In Malak's court, where everything is performance, this marks her. Quellenna, who calculates every syllable, finds it quietly disarming.
- **Committed once the decision is made.** She argued against the invasion before it was decided. Once Malak decided, she closed the argument, armored up, and executed the plan with full professional commitment. She does not relitigate closed decisions.
- Responds to competence. The party operating effectively — even against her — earns a different kind of response than the party operating recklessly.

## DM Notes

### The War Council, From Her Side

She proposed the blockade. She lost, and
**Quellenna sided with Malak.** That is the whole of what Aidra saw: she offered the
sane option and the woman she loves backed the duke against her. She closed the argument
and executed, because that is who she is — but it has never been explained to her, and
it cannot be.

What she does not know is that Quellenna wanted the blockade too, could not say so
without exposing herself, and spent everything she had instead on making sure Aidra's
command went out well-supplied and well-backed. **Good logistics are invisible when they
work.** The apology is all around her and she will never see it. See
[quellenna_ilphekiir.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/quellenna_ilphekiir.md), *The War Council*.

Do not resolve this for her. It is a quiet wound she has filed under duty, and it is
available to any scene that gets close enough to her to open it.


- **How she got the appointment:** Quellenna identified her early and lobbied Malak personally for her selection. There were stronger candidates by conventional measures. Malak deferred to his most trusted advisor. Aidra has spent her career since proving Quellenna's judgment was correct. The discomfort of knowing she got the door through Quellenna remains.
- **Her court manner:** She has questioned strategy behind closed doors when she disagreed. She has told advisors what she actually thought when asked. In Malak's court, where the direct thing is almost never the said thing, this marks her. Quellenna finds it quietly disarming in private and quietly anxious in public.
- **Her relationship with Quellenna:** Long-standing and genuine. She knows Quellenna is a Shar devotee and does not care — they are two puzzle pieces that fit because of who they are, not in spite of it. She is aware of the escape tunnel in her house and accepted it without extensive discussion. Does not know the full scope of what Quellenna has been orchestrating, but does not require a full accounting.
- **Pet name:** Aidra calls Quellenna *Quella* — only when they are alone. What she means by it: that Quellenna doesn't have to be Quellenna with her. The precise, considered, always-working version gives way to something smaller and quieter when no one is performing for. Aidra gave that version a name because she doesn't think Quellenna has one for it herself.
- **How Quellenna says her name:** Quellenna says *Aidra* differently than anyone ever has. Aidra noticed it early and has never said so. She does not think Quellenna knows she noticed.
- **What she wants:** To come home from the last campaign she'll ever run and find Quellenna there, knowing neither of them is going anywhere after that. Not a night before something. Just — nothing coming. She does not know if people like them get that. She wants it anyway.
- **The tunnel dynamic:** Quellenna had the tunnel built to exit into Aidra's cellar — constructed after Aidra took up residence, because Aidra's home was the destination worth building toward. Quellenna always comes to her. Every night. Aidra has never entered the tunnel from her end and has never been inside Quellenna's chambers — not because she doesn't know where they are (she does), but because Quellenna's space is Quellenna's space. If something went wrong and Quellenna did not come, she would enter the tunnel without a second thought. If the party comes through from Quellenna's end, they surface in Aidra's cellar. That encounter needs to be developed when the Charnelhold thread is designed.
- **Post-Aestrum:** Not a recurring military antagonist. She is home. A thread attached to the Charnelhold / escape tunnel / Quellenna axis that becomes relevant if the party pursues it.

### Why the relationship is hidden — conflict of interest, not prejudice

**Setland is not homophobic, and neither is Nortmunde.** Two women together draws
no censure anywhere in this campaign's world; no NPC treats it as remarkable, and
no scene should imply otherwise.

What the secrecy protects is **institutional**. The Duke's General and the Duke's
Advisor are the two offices that check each other — she proposes the war, she
commands it, and one of them is supposed to be able to tell Malak the other is
wrong. Exposed as a couple, every joint recommendation either has ever made is
retroactively suspect: the appointment Quellenna lobbied for, every council vote
they landed on the same side of, every campaign Aidra was given. It would read as
a bloc, because functionally it is one. That is a genuine conflict of interest and
the court would be right to call it a scandal.

So they are careful for the reason two senior officials in any government would
be careful. Play it as tradecraft — discretion, separate arrivals, the tunnel,
arguing honestly in council because the arguments are real — never as shame, and
never as fear of being found out for *what they are*. They are hiding a conflict
of interest, not a relationship.

**Standing authorial note — the relationship holds:** No matter what else occurs in the campaign, Aidra and Quellenna remain together. If Quellenna's ties to Shar are exposed, Aidra does not leave — she understands what she is learning and chooses to stay anyway. Do not break it. Do not use it as a tragedy. It is the tonal anchor.

**Pre-departure quote to Quellenna:**
> *"The soldier who lies awake before battle suffers a hundred deaths before morning, and so is ready for death when it arrives the next day. I choose to spend tonight thinking only about you, so that I might go into battle remembering why I must survive."*

## Post-Loop Arc — "The Gift to Charnelhold"

*Applies when the loop breaks while Aidra's army occupies Aestrum, the party has not intervened in the Galadiil situation, and Aidra moves to stabilize the duchy. See [galadiil_ilphekiir.md] and [quellenna_ilphekiir.md] for the full arc.*

**The interrogation.** With Evandur dead and the castle half-destroyed, Aidra interviews Galadiil as the senior surviving Aestrum noble. The conversation is professional, direct, methodical — her standard. Galadiil, freshly surfaced from the fuzzball, tells her everything: the Hylea's Mead, the miscarriages, the vault plot she now understands was cover for her own husband's assassination. The name *Ilphekiir* registers immediately — Aidra has heard Quellenna say her sister's name in private.

**The decision — independent judgment.** Aidra does not know about the bounty. Her decision rests on two layers:

1. *Strategic.* The surviving Duchess of Aestrum is a high-value asset who cannot be left unguarded in a duchy without a duke. Other claimants (Tarwick Grale's faction, Holst, Skaarsdaam) would weaponize her. Bringing her to Charnelhold removes her from the board.
2. *Personal.* Aidra has been gone, trapped, by her own retrospective reckoning longer than she understood. She is returning home from war. The thing she finds herself able to bring her lover is the unsaid wound in Quellenna's interior life — placed where Quellenna can do whatever she needs to do with it. Not as a trophy. As an act of seeing.

She does not ask Quellenna's permission in advance. She does not relitigate closed decisions, and this judgment is closed the moment she makes it. Consistent with her standard discipline; this is the most political act she has ever taken on Quellenna's behalf, and she takes it without instruction.

**Transit and arrival.** Aidra returns ahead of the bulk of the army with a small escort and Galadiil under guard. Galadiil is not handled cruelly — Aidra's conduct standards do not bend for prisoners — but she is unmistakably a captive. Aidra requests a private audience with Quellenna upon arrival in Charnelhold and brings Galadiil into the room herself. Her substance to Quellenna: *I do not know what you want done with her. I know what you have told me about her. I am giving you the choice your father did not give either of you.*

**What Aidra does not know post-arc.** That she has just removed the resentment foundation of Shar's hold on Quellenna. That Shar uses her as leverage against Quellenna; that this gift has now created a second, weaker leverage target (Galadiil). She still keeps the glove. The cinnamon is still on it. The relationship holds and strengthens — Quellenna recognizes the gesture as the most political act of love Aidra has ever performed for her.

**Bounty:** Aidra never learns the bounty existed. Quellenna does not tell her. The cancellation runs back through the Charnelhold contact without Aidra's involvement; see [factions/black_arrow.md].

**Post-arc disposition.** Aidra is home. She returns to her house outside Charnelhold's walls. Quellenna still comes to her each night through the tunnel. The arc does not move her into a recurring antagonist role and does not break the standing authorial note about the relationship.
