## Overview

Ordinary Seaman aboard *The Incorrigible*. Musician first, sailor second. A genial, weathered busker who has spent his life going from port to port performing for coin and conversation. Rylin T'Sarran, the first mate, has granted him standing shore leave at every port the ship touches — and has done so for years. Rick believes this is because Rylin is a music lover with surprisingly good taste and a generous coin pouch. It is not.

The arrangement: Rylin composes songs and pays Rick a silver to perform them on shore. Rick performs them, beautifully, in his pleasing operatic baritone, for any tavern crowd or dockside knot of listeners who will throw coins. The lyrics carry encoded Erethezra signal language — Rylin's intelligence network reads the meaning out of the public performance and Rick is none the wiser. He has been an unwitting message carrier across the Calimshan trade routes for a decade.

He is also extremely proud of being an Ordinary Seaman, a rank he believes outranks Baldric's Able Seaman. It does not — Baldric outranks him. No one on the crew has ever corrected this, and Rylin in particular has gently encouraged the misconception because a happy Rick is a Rick who goes ashore every port without complaint.

## Appearance

Older human, late fifties to early sixties — weathered the way someone is weathered who has spent forty years on deck. Greying beard, full and well-kept (one of his vanities), jaw-length grey hair pulled back under a faded red cloth tied around his head. Strong nose, sun-creased eyes, mouth that defaults to a slight upward curve from the muscle memory of singing.

Built lean and serviceable — sailor's hands, sailor's shoulders, nothing extra. A red and white horizontally-striped shirt under a worn leather strap (instrument or sword belt, depending on the day). Loose blue trousers, tucked into low boots. A small purse at the hip for coins thrown during performance — never empty by end of shore leave.

He looks exactly like what he is: a working seaman who has worked out how to make people give him their drink money.

## Background

A career deckhand who happened to be born with the voice. Joined a merchant ship in his teens and never seriously considered another life. Has worked under several captains; signed on with *The Incorrigible* roughly twelve years ago and stayed. Met Rylin shortly after coming aboard. Rylin took to him quickly — Rick was flattered by the first mate's attention and chalked it up to shared musical taste. He still does.

He has children grown and somewhere, a wife buried decades ago in a port he no longer visits, and very little else weighing on him beyond the next performance. He sings for the love of it. The silvers are nice.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Too proud for his own good.** He has a sterling opinion of his rank, his voice, his musical judgment, and the cleverness of his repartee. The first three are defensible. The fourth is not. He does not realize when he is being managed, flattered, or used. He never has.
- **Loves to perform.** Genuinely, deeply, without irony. Every shore leave is a performance opportunity. Every passing patron is a potential audience. He has never in his life turned down a request to sing, even from someone obviously trying to get him to stop talking.
- **Pleasing operatic baritone in performance.** When he sings he is genuinely good — full, rich, controlled, with the kind of breath support that comes from singing through gale winds for forty years. The voice is the gift. The voice is also the misdirection: people who hear him sing assume the man behind the voice is similarly substantial. He is not, particularly, but he benefits from the assumption.
- **Thinks he is smarter than he is.** Believes he reads people well. Believes he knows when he is being conned. Both beliefs are unfounded. He has been carrying coded messages for ten years and would tell you with absolute confidence that no one has ever put one over on him.
- **Loyal to Rylin.** Will defend him to anyone who criticizes him. Believes Rylin is the best first mate he has ever served under: fair, generous, sharp-eyed, a real music lover. Believes Rylin has never done him wrong. From Rick's vantage point this is true — Rylin has indeed never done anything Rick *recognizes* as wronging him.
- **Convinced he outranks Baldric.** Will explain the distinction to anyone who asks (and several who don't): Ordinary Seaman, in his telling, is the senior rating. He invokes it gently but consistently when there is any question of precedence. Baldric does not correct him. Neither does Rylin. Neither does anyone else.

## Speech Patterns

A working seaman's drawl — comfortable, unhurried, easy to follow. Drops the occasional consonant ("nothin'," "afore," "aye"), uses "lad" and "friend" liberally for anyone he is in a good mood at, and slips into shanty cadence when he is warming up to a song. The drawl is texture, not obstacle — a listener never has to work to understand him.

In performance the drawl drops away and the baritone takes over. Clean, full vowels, perfect intonation, every word audible to the back of a tavern. The contrast between his speaking voice and his singing voice is part of the act — people who have only heard him talk are unprepared for what comes out when he opens up.

*On shore leave:* "Got the captain's blessing and the first mate's silver — what more can a man ask for? Settin' up by the fountain at midday if you've coin to spare, friend."

*On his rank:* "Ordinary Seaman, aye. Above the Able lads. People do get it muddled — easy mistake — but there it is."

*On Rylin:* "Best first mate I ever sailed under. Generous to a fault. Listens to what a man's playing and *hears* it, you know? Rare thing in a sailor."

*On being asked if he writes his own songs:* "Some I do. The good ones, mostly — Rylin's. He's got a knack. Pays me a silver to give 'em a proper airing on shore."

*Mid-performance, between verses, dropping back into drawl for a beat:* "Now this next one, this next one's a bit slower — give the lasses a moment to find their handkerchiefs..."

## The Signature Shanty

His favorite, his closer, the one he has sung in every port he has ever made landfall in. A Faerunian sea shanty whose verses he has refined over decades to a kind of personal anthem. It is, transparently to anyone with the right ear, a love song dressed up in shanty bones — a sailor swearing to his shore-bound sweetheart that no matter how far the tide takes him, he will be true, he will not desert, he will not let her down. The chorus has the structure of a list: a series of *never gonna* promises pulled together against the rhythm of a working line.

The song is not encoded. It is Rick's, top to bottom, and it is what he is known for. Tavern regulars in the ports *The Incorrigible* visits know it by the first three notes. Rick has been known to refuse payment to sing it — it is the one he sings for himself.

The encoded songs, by contrast, are *Rylin's* — different tunes, presented as recent compositions of the first mate's, performed less frequently and with no special love. Rick sings them well because he sings everything well. He does not get tired of his own song; he gets tired of theirs.

## DM Notes

**The Erethezra encoding (Ledger Secret 1 — The Shanty Runs Deeper):**
- Rylin composes songs with embedded Erethezra signal language and pays Rick a silver to perform them at port. Rick has been an unwitting message carrier across the Calimshan trade routes for years.
- The misdirection is structurally elegant: Rick *is* the one singing the encoded lyrics in public, so any investigator who decodes a fragment naturally suspects Rick. Rick's complete bewilderment when confronted reads as plausible deniability and reinforces the impression that he is hiding something. The real composer stays clean.
- **Party options if exposed to Rick directly:**
  - Devastating — he will direct the full force of his betrayal at Rylin and become a willing source. Years of "the best first mate I ever sailed under" inverts hard and fast.
  - Feed false information through Rick into Rylin's network. Rick will perform whatever Rylin gives him, on schedule, with no idea what he is broadcasting.
  - Hold the knowledge as leverage over Rylin without telling Rick.
  - Use Rick to send a deliberate signal to Erethezra contacts if the party gets hold of the cipher.

**The embedded signal:**
- The encoded fragment **"ghosts go home"** comes from a Rylin composition, not Rick's signature shanty. Anyone who catches and decodes it has their suspicion drawn to Rick rather than Rylin — exactly the misdirection the signal is designed for.
- The meaningful quote — *"I don't miss their calls, I don't dream of their halls — I just sing that the ghosts go home"* — is from that Rylin composition. Rick can recite it in full on request. He does not know what it means and will say so cheerfully. His cheerfulness is the misdirection.

**The Baldric rank gag (Ledger Secret 2 — by extension):**
- Rick believes Ordinary Seaman outranks Able Seaman. The reverse is true. Baldric (the Able Seaman) actually outranks Rick.
- Rylin maintains the misconception deliberately. A Rick who believes he holds rank over Baldric is a contented Rick who never makes trouble about shore privileges, shift assignments, or pay. A corrected Rick would be an indignant Rick, and Rylin does not need indignation in a man who carries coded messages ashore for him every port.
- The party can use this. A confrontation that establishes the true rank order — especially in front of the rest of the crew — punctures Rick's pride badly. A Rick who has just learned that Rylin has been letting him believe a lie for a decade is a Rick whose loyalty to Rylin is suddenly available for renegotiation. This is a softer wedge than the Erethezra reveal but it requires less evidence.

**The signature shanty as a scene tool:**
- Use it as ambient texture in any waterfront scene — Rick is performing somewhere within earshot at least one in three party visits to the docks. The song is established and recognizable.
- The contrast between his obvious love for *his* song and his workmanlike delivery of *Rylin's* songs is a tell available to an observant player on a successful Insight check. Why does the same singer sound less invested in some songs than others? Because some of them are not his.
- A bard PC who picks up the shanty by ear and performs it back to Rick wins him for the evening. He has never been imitated and is moved by it.

**Loop status:**
- Rick *is* loop-affected. *The Incorrigible* sailed into the Aestrum bubble in the hours before midnight on Day 9 of campaign time and dropped anchor offshore. The crew's snapshot was taken there.
- Rick resets each midnight aboard ship. Subjectively, every day he sets ashore is his second day at this port. Objectively, every day since Day 9 has been a repeat — however many cycles that has been at the current moment of play. He has performed the same songs to the same waterfront audience every one of those days and has no idea.
- The crew as a whole is not loop-aware. None of them understand why business in Duskwall keeps not concluding. They trust Rylin and they do not press. (Rylin himself resets to his Day-9-evening state each morning and re-derives the loop independently each day — see [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/rylin_tsarran.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/rylin_tsarran.md). He does not retain the understanding across cycles, which means he also does not strategically adjust the encoded-song production based on the messages going nowhere; the routine runs on snapshot habit.)
- **Implication for the Erethezra channel:** the encoded messages Rick performs reach the Aestrum waterfront and stop there. The ship has not sailed. Rylin's network outside the bubble has not received a transmission from *The Incorrigible* since the ship became overdue. From the network's side, the ship is missing.

**On a loop break:**
- Rick will register nothing unusual at the moment of break. The crew sets sail when their cargo business finally concludes (which it now can). Rick keeps singing.
- The seeded songs sail with him. Anything the party has fed into Rick's performance rotation — Rylin's coded compositions, party-supplied false intelligence, or anything else — finally propagates through the Calimshan trade-route grapevine the moment the ship makes its next port. This is a sleeper-cell mechanic: the accumulated payload from the Aestrum stop (only a few cycles of material at any given point, since the ship arrived recently) leaves the bubble in one shipment.
- Whether the party has destabilized Rylin's operation before the ship leaves is the real question. Rick is the leverage point, but he leaves with the ship.
