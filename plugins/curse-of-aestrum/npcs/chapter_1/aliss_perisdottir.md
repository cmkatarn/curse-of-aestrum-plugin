## Overview

The fortune teller at the center of the Misty Forest. A genuine psychic — a Diviner Wizard whose tarot practice is theatrical performance laid over real magical foresight. She lives in a three-story stone house she shares with her husband, a smith. She has been in Aestrum since before the loop began, knows about the cycle, and uses it: each day brings new clients with no memory of having seen her before.

She is the single best informational resource in Aestrum for a party that thinks to ask the right questions. She has been taking notes on every person who walks through her door for years, and the notes do not reset.

She wants the loop broken. She has seen it broken, in a vision she cannot date, by a group that matches the party precisely.

## Appearance

Late fifties, perhaps older — the kind of age that has stopped advertising itself because she is no longer interested in commenting on it. Dark hair streaked with silver, worn loose or wrapped in a scarf depending on what the day requires. Olive skin, dark expressive eyes, a face that does a lot of work without seeming to.

Her dress is dramatic and layered: long skirts in deep jewel tones, scarves and shawls in contrasting colors, brass and silver jewelry at her neck and wrists that catches the light and chimes faintly when she moves. None of it is costume. She dresses this way because she likes it. The drama is the same drama she brings to the reading table — it is who she is, not a mask she puts on for clients.

Her hands are clean and steady. The nails are short. She wears one ring that does not match the others — a plain silver band on her left fourth finger, old, worn smooth, never removed.

## Background

She was not born in Aestrum. She arrived from somewhere south — she will not specify, and the question does not interest her enough to invite a follow-up. What is known is that she came with her husband and the stones of the house already shipped. Both of them are immigrants. Both of them planned the house before they planned the rest of their lives.

The house is built of Dalihad stone — pale grey, tight-fitted, mortar-free, dressed and laid in the fashion of Dalihad's guild masons. The stones were quarried, dressed, and transported by wagon across the kingdom in pieces, then assembled on this spot at the forest's center by craftsmen who came with them. This is not how anything else in Aestrum is built. A traveler with any familiarity with regional stonework will register the discrepancy. A traveler who later reaches Dalihad and sees the quarry city will recognize the technique.

She trained as a diviner formally — a real apprenticeship at a real school, somewhere far enough away that no one in Aestrum has heard of it. The tarot practice she developed afterward, as a way to make her gift legible to clients who would not pay for raw vision. The cards do real work for her; they are also the show. Both are true at once.

She began keeping notes on her clients about three years before the loop began. It was a professional habit — a way to track patterns, recognize returning clients, refine her sense of who in Aestrum was who. The dead box she acquired separately, for a different purpose entirely: a Dalihad-quarried strongbox warded to suppress magic, originally meant for storing potion ingredients that destabilize in ambient magical environments. When the loop began, the dead box became the only place in the house where her notes survived the midnight reset. She moved the notes in immediately and has not moved them out since.

She loved Aestrum before the loop. She does not love it now, but she does not hate it either. She is waiting.

## The House

Three storeys, tall and narrow, set in a small cleared circle at the forest's center. The path in is unmarked and indirect — a client who has not been here before usually arrives on the second or third attempt. (She does not advertise. Clients come because someone told them to come.)

**Ground floor:** Her husband's smithy. A working forge, anvil, bellows, racks of tools and stock metal. The hearth is always lit during working hours; the heat is felt throughout the house. He sleeps in a small room off the back of the smithy.

**Second floor:** Kitchen, dining room, hearth. Where they take meals together. Where she meets a client who has come for tea rather than a reading. There is a long table, two chairs that match and three that do not, a heavy cookware rack, and a window that looks out over the forest. She does most of her own cooking. She is competent at it.

**Third floor:** Her bedroom, separated by a curtained doorway from her reading parlor. The parlor is the room clients see — circular by virtue of the third storey being smaller than the two beneath, lit by candles and a single skylight, ringed by a low shelf holding decks, crystals, bones, a brass armillary, and other instruments she uses depending on what the reading wants. At the center: a small round table covered in deep red cloth, two chairs facing each other across it.

The dead box sits on the shelf behind her chair, in the parlor. A foot-square iron box bound with brass straps. Set into the iron — fine and precise, the work of someone who knew what they were doing — are inlaid runes in a script almost no one alive can read. The runes are the magic-immunity. Every magic-immune item in this world bears them; the language is archaic and the literate are few. The box does not look ordinary, but it does not look threatening either, and people who do not recognize the script tend to dismiss it as decorative ironwork.

## The Notes and the Dead Box

Years of records, layered chronologically with the oldest at the bottom.

The box also holds a **running cycle-tally** — a count she increments by one each
day, in her own hand, that survives the midnight wipe. It is how she knows *how
long:* the calendar cannot tell her, because every entry she writes bears the
**same frozen snapshot date** (the world resets to a single day, so the date out
there never moves) — elapsed time is read off the count, never off the dates. By
the tally, the loop runs to roughly ten years. That every entry carries the
*same* date is itself part of how she first understood the world was frozen.

**The bottom of the stack** is full pages — careful, dated, sometimes diary-like. Names, dates, presenting questions, what she saw, what she told them, what later came of it. The handwriting is steady. She wrote these in the years before the loop, when she had time and the world had time for her.

**The middle of the stack** is shorter. Still pages, still legible, but the entries are clipped to the essentials. Date, name, vision, action. No reflection. The handwriting is faster.

**The top of the stack** is scraps. Torn corners of larger sheets, edges of envelopes, the backs of receipts. The handwriting is fast and sometimes angry. Two or three words to a fragment: *"the carpenter again — still pregnant — do NOT bring it up,"* or *"red-haired man, dock guard, will ask about brother by week's end — say nothing."* The most recent are barely sentences.

The deterioration is visible. A reader who looks at the stack as a stack — not as individual notes — sees the shape of a person who is becoming more frightened, more rushed, more afraid that today's note will be the last one she gets to leave for tomorrow. She has seen a future in which the loop breaks. She has not yet lived a morning in which it has. The contrast is wearing her down.

She knows the deterioration is visible. She has noted to herself, more than once, to slow down. She does not.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Warm and professional in equal measure.** She likes her clients and she takes the work seriously. Both at once. The warmth is not performance; the professionalism is not coldness. She has been doing this long enough to make both look effortless.
- **Discovers the box mid-morning, every morning.** She wakes as a normal Aestrum resident — no loop-awareness — and moves through her morning routine without any reason to break it. She makes tea. She eats. She talks with her husband about the day ahead. She climbs to the parlor to prepare for clients. And then, some hour or two into the day, her eye catches the iron box on the shelf and something settles into place. She opens it. She reads. The day she thought she was beginning is revealed to be the day she has been beginning for years, and several of its hours are already gone. The grief is small and immediate and familiar. She has it, lets it pass, and gets to work. She does not skip the morning routine to find the box faster. She has tried. She always finds it at the same hour, the same way, no matter what she does before.
- **Charges or doesn't, depending on timing.** A client who arrives in the early morning (before she has read her notes) is charged a fair price for the reading. A client who arrives after she has read is told the reading is on the house. She has stopped explaining why. Her husband's smithy provides enough income that the lost coin does not concern her. Charging the morning clients keeps her professional self-image intact; not charging the rest is the only protest she lets herself make against the cycle.
- **Soothes clients in fuzzball-friction.** Citizens of Aestrum who arrive in the throes of mild loop-friction — vague unease, a feeling that something is off, a moment of clarity they cannot name — sometimes find their way to her without knowing why. She reads for them. The reading is real divination; it is also calibrated to settle the fuzzball cleanly so they can return to their day without distress. She is not trying to break them free. She has lived without the comfortable haze and would not wish the clarity on anyone who has not chosen it.
- **Theatrical at the reading table.** Full performance — dramatic pauses, weighted gestures, the cards turned slowly. She loves the theater of it. She also believes the theater is part of how the magic moves; the performance focuses both her and the client. The drama is sincere, not ironic. She is having a good time.
- **Quiet about the loop with new clients.** She does not volunteer her cycle-awareness. She tests the client first — through what they ask, how they respond to the cards, whether they show any of the markers of cycle-awareness themselves. If they pass, she may speak more freely. If they do not, she keeps it to herself.
- **Particular about her husband.** He is loop-aware because she told him. She did not consult him before telling him. She has occasionally wondered if it was the right call. She does not raise the question because the answer would not change anything now.
- **Patient with the wait.** She has seen the cycle break. She does not know when. The waiting is hard. She has gotten better at it. She has not gotten good at it.

## Speech Pattern

Strong, articulate, theatrical even in conversation — the register of a working stage performer who never fully steps off-stage. She projects without seeming to try; consonants land cleanly, vowels are given their full length, and she pronounces words with palpable relish. She has the actor's habit of placing emphasis on the second word of a phrase rather than the first, which gives even ordinary sentences a slightly performed quality, as though each one had been rehearsed for inflection. She is not putting on a show in everyday conversation, but she has spoken in front of an audience for so long that the audience-voice has become her voice.

**Outside the reading:** Warmer, more direct. Tea is offered specifically — not as a politeness, as a recommendation, *"You'll have the chamomile, it's the right thing for this hour."* She does not soften unpleasant truths but she does not weaponize them.

**Inside the reading:** Full theater. Long pauses. Hands held over the deck before drawing. Cards turned with precise drama. *"Ah — this one. Oh, no, you didn't want this one. But you got it anyway."* She speaks more slowly during readings. Her diction sharpens. The performance is real and it is delicious to her.

**To a client in fuzzball-friction:** Gentler. The reading still has drama in it, but the drama becomes soothing rather than incisive. She reframes what is troubling them into a story the fuzzball can resolve around. *"What you felt this morning — that was a memory trying to be a thought. Let it be a memory. The cards say so."*

**To someone she recognizes as cycle-aware:** Almost dry. The theatrics drop almost entirely; she becomes plain, fast, and to the point. *"You know. Good. We have much to talk about and very little time. Sit."*

## The Reading

She reads from a standard tarot deck — major and minor arcana, hand-painted, the deck old enough that the edges have softened. She has had it since her training. It is a focus, not a prop.

A full reading takes ten to twenty minutes. Cards are drawn one at a time, placed in a spread of her choosing for the question at hand, and interpreted aloud with full performance. She will narrate what she sees in the card, what she sees beyond the card, and what she sees in the client's reaction to the card. She is reading both the spread and the person across the table from her. The two information streams reinforce each other.

The magic is genuine. A reading from Aliss surfaces real information — possibilities, tendencies, things that have not happened yet but could. She is honest about the limits: foresight is murky, partial, and easy to misread. She does not promise certainty. She delivers what she sees and lets the client decide what to do with it.

She does not do love readings. She used to. She stopped after the cycle began — too many of the same questions, too many of the same answers, no possible follow-through. She will tell a client looking for a love reading that she does not do them; she will offer a general reading instead.

## What She Knows

- **The loop exists, what it does, and roughly how long it has been running.** She has been awake to it longer than almost anyone. She does not have the divine-mechanics view that a god would have, but she knows the practical shape — daily reset, fuzzball, dead zone exception, that the loop wants something from someone in Aestrum that is not being delivered.
- **A future in which the loop breaks, broken by a group that matches the party.** This vision is not dated. She does not know when she had it. She has a single scrap of paper, in the dead box, where past-her described it — and the description matches the party exactly. She showed the party this paper on first meeting.
- **Every client who has come to her house in years.** Names, what they came for, what she told them, what she has subsequently observed about them through the door of her parlor or in town. This is the operational intelligence she has, and it is extensive.
- **The Dalihad provenance of her house.** She knows the stonework is unusual. She does not advertise the connection but will confirm it if asked.
- **The shrine in the northwest quarter.** She knows there is a shrine in that quarter of the wood, tended by someone since before the cycle began. Her sight dies where magic dies, so she knows that dead-zone quarter only from its edge — she keeps well clear of it, and warns others off.
- **What she does not know:** the specific mechanism by which the loop breaks, the identity of the loop's sustaining anchor, or the divine politics involved. She knows the shape of the problem, not the solution. She has been waiting for the party to bring the solution.

She is not built for combat; she is built to know what is about to happen and be elsewhere when it does.

**The Portent dice as a table tool:** A player running her may use Portent dramatically — placing a die on the reading table face-up and telling a player character "this will be your next saving throw against fear" or similar. When the moment comes, the recorded die is used. This is mechanically identical to standard Portent but plays as in-character foresight. It is satisfying at the table.

## DM Notes

**The recognition moment:** On first meeting, she should produce the scrap of paper from the dead box and read it to the party — a description that matches them in specifics. She does not know which past-version of herself wrote it. She does not know when. The scrap is undated, in her own hand, and it is exact. This is the genuine cold-open of her character: she has been waiting for them, by name and number, for an unknown length of time. Let the moment land. Do not over-explain it. *"I have a paper for you. I do not know when I wrote it. Listen."*

**The notes as resource:** The full archive is hers, and she shares selectively. A party that asks specific questions about specific people will get answers. A party that asks for "everything you know" will not — she does not hand the full archive to strangers. The reading-fee structure does not apply to intelligence requests; she trades information in the currency she actually values, which is help breaking the loop. Bring her something useful and she opens the box.

**Writing the notes for play:** When a note is referenced or read aloud, write it one of two ways: (1) a known Aestrum NPC by name, with a real campaign detail — *"Hati arrived at 7 again, looked at the back wall again, asked nothing,"* or *"Lisandre's reading: he hasn't slept in days, won't say why, won't ask the question he came to ask"* — or (2) no name, an oblique humorous reference to a fantasy character from film or television — *"the wizard still can't attune to the helm,"* *"woman dressed all in white looking for 'Richard,'"* *"the boy with the sword keeps asking after a princess; not in this kingdom."* The mix keeps the archive feeling real, gives the players an occasional laugh, and never wastes a note slot on filler. Avoid generic descriptions; every note should either further a campaign thread or land a joke.

**Aliss as soft-GM-hint mechanism:** Her divination should *guide* the party without giving away story points. A few examples of the register:
- Murder mystery: a card she draws describes the perpetrator obliquely — manner of dress, time of day, a single distinctive habit. Not the name. Enough to focus the investigation.
- Lisandre: she has notes. He has been a client. She knows what he carries and that he is sliding. She will share that he is dangerous to himself before he becomes dangerous to anyone else, and that the Trambeathen line is older and stranger than he likes to admit.
- Evandur and Hylea's Mead: she has felt the wrongness around the bottles he keeps. She does not know the mechanism. She will tell the party to look at what Evandur drinks before bed, and to do it without his knowing they are looking.
- **Jiasha's location after the abduction:** the canonical path by which the party finds [Jiasha]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/jiasha.md) runs through Aliss. The rumor *"the fortune teller in the forest finds lost things"* circulates among Duskwall and road NPCs and routes the party to her door. During the reading, Aliss receives a structured three-signal vision (a white raven at 4WI, a torn strip of Jiasha's dress knotted on a low branch at the hunting-trail spur entry, a moonstone resting in a fallen log near the camp) — delivered in full theatrical register, with no explanation of source. The signals manifest as the party travels. Full mechanism in [the_abduction_of_jiasha.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/quests/chapter_1/the_abduction_of_jiasha.md). The vision is reliable for *where* and *what to look for*; she cannot see the band's numbers or the tactical state at the camp.
- General principle: she narrows the search. She points at the right room. She names the right question. She does not solve anything for the players. She makes the next step visible.

Use her freely when the party is stuck. Use her sparingly when the party has momentum. She is a brake against frustration, not a substitute for play.

**The Dalihad call-ahead:** Players with Stonework, History, or relevant background proficiency may notice the house's construction on a passive observation or a DC 13 INT check. Bari Khalid can confirm the regional origin if asked. When the party later reaches Dalihad in Chapter 2, the connection should click: *that house in the forest was built by these people.* Do not announce the link. Let the players make it.

**The fuzzball-citizens function:** When a townsperson is sliding past a hint of loop-awareness and seeking a way to settle the friction, they may end up at her door. Treat this as a passive feature of the location — if the party is at Aliss's house and a knock comes at an odd hour, it can be a citizen in mild fuzzball-distress. She handles them. The party can observe how she handles them. It is one of the most loop-coherent kindnesses in the campaign.

**The pricing tell:** Whether she charges or waives the fee tells the party where they are in her day. Charging = she has not read her notes yet, she does not know who they are this morning. Waiving = she has read, and she has decided to spend her morning re-greeting old friends. A loop-aware party that sees the pattern across visits has a clock on her day.

**The deteriorating notes as visible state:** A player who looks at the note stack carefully sees the deterioration — careful diary entries at the bottom, frantic scraps at the top. She does not bring this up. If a player comments, she does not pretend it isn't happening. She will acknowledge plainly that she has been more frightened lately than she used to be, and move on. She is not asking for help with the fear. She is asking for help breaking the loop, which is the only thing that will end the fear.

**The Portent mechanic:** A player running Aliss can use Portent dice as a roleplay device — placing them face-up on the table and predicting outcomes. This is one of the most satisfying NPC abilities in 5e when leaned into. Encourage it.

**Joining hook:** She will go with them. She has been waiting for this group specifically; the vision she carries says they break the cycle, and the cycle is what she has been trying to end for years. The arithmetic is simple to her. She will pack the dead box, the tarot deck, her spellbook, and a small bag of personal effects. She will tell her husband, plainly, what she is doing and why — and she will tell him knowing that tomorrow morning he will wake to an empty house, will feel briefly that something is wrong, will not be able to articulate what, and will go back about his day. The fuzzball will manage him. She has thought this through. She is going anyway. *"If this group ends the cycle, then by tomorrow there will be a tomorrow he can ask me about it. If it does not — then he will be no worse off than he has been every morning of the last several years."*

She does not pack lightly. The dead box goes with her. So does the full archive — years of notes, irreplaceable. A party that takes Aliss takes the box and a great deal of practical intelligence with them.

She is not a combat asset. She is an intelligence asset, a divination engine, and a soft-touch GM hint mechanism in a robe and scarves. The party that brings her along gains a friend whose foresight makes everything they investigate slightly less opaque.

**On her death:** If she dies before the loop is broken, the dead box and its contents persist. A party that recovers the box has access to years of intelligence — and one scrap of paper, in her own hand, describing them.
