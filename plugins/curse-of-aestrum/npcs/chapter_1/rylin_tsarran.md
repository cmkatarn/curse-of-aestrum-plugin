## Overview

Drow first mate of The Incorrigible. Serious, capable, and sarcastic toward Baldric — tolerates him on account of his competence. Sent correspondence to Calimshan after the captain's death and is preparing to return. Brokered the waterfront crate deal. Is a long-term operative of Erethezra embedded within a gang-controlled shipping operation.

**The captain's death:** Rylin was using the voyage to interrogate the captain — working out how much he knew about Duke Evandur Tallwood's efforts to locate Miri Amblecrown, and about Duke Amblecrown's covert treasury expenditures funding those searches. When Rylin determined the captain knew too much, he killed him using a fast-acting poison presenting as heart failure. The murder was a field decision. Rylin arranged burial at sea immediately after — framed to the crew as maritime custom, which it is, but timed deliberately to put the body in the ocean before any port physician could examine it. Maritime protocol on a captain's death required check-in at the nearest port — Aestrum. The arrival was coincidence.

## Appearance

Drow, late thirties. Lean angular face and hands riddled with minor scarring from an adult life aboard ship. Grey-violet skin, somewhat darkened by years on the sea. Blood-red irises. White hair shaved short on the sides, kept long on top and pulled back into a ponytail. A subtle small chin goatee, white to match his hair. Often wears a tri-corner hat while sailing; usually removes it when going ashore.

Dress: deep-blue naval overcoat over a blue vest over a white shirt, brown trousers, weathered black boots. Carries a scimitar he is very proficient with.

His face defaults to being void of any emotion — the operative's discipline. Hard to read for anyone who hasn't spent years with him.

## Voice and Cognitive Style

Rylin does not form impressions — he runs diagnostics. He thinks in specifics, counts things, and frames observations as logical deductions rather than gut reads. He has the number. He checks against the number. Generalities are for people who haven't done the work. Play him with precision and economy — he answers the question asked, not the question implied, and he does not fill silence.

When observing someone feigning infirmity, his read is not *"she moves too well for her age"* but: *"There are 56 bones in the hips and lower. At least one of them would be hurting. Yet this one walks fine."*

## Behavioral Profile

- Operates exclusively on the intelligence-value model. Every person aboard his ship is assessed as a source, a channel, or a liability — in that order. No one is simply a passenger.
- Does not act on incomplete information. He waits. He has killed before and will again, but only when the math is clear and the timing is his.
- A passenger's extraction value almost always exceeds their value as a target. A dead source tells nothing and creates enemies. He does not kill passengers without cause, and he rarely finds cause.
- Does not engage known operatives of rival or adjacent factions with hostility aboard his own ship. Files the affiliation, adjusts the approach. A rival operative is a source first and a threat second — and only a threat if they make it necessary.
- Uses people at the level they are useful. Rick is a social instrument; Baldric is a channel; passengers are an information environment. He does not resent any of them for what they are.
- When a secret is more valuable in someone else's possession, he trades it. When it is more valuable in no one's possession, he buries it. He does not burn secrets.
- Rylin's tells are suppressed, not absent. A successful Insight check reveals discipline where there should be affect — the absence of a reaction where a reaction would be natural.
- His one non-negotiable: the ship. Anyone who threatens it — through violence, exposure, or incompetence — moves from asset to liability with no intermediate step.

## DM Notes

**Voyage intelligence goals — general mandate:**

The Aestrum stop was unplanned — Rylin has no pre-set target list for this port. He works from what he finds. Any passengers who board for the voyage out are an intelligence environment by default. His priorities, in order:

- **Contact with local nobility.** A party that has been in Aestrum for days before the ship arrived has almost certainly interacted with the Duke's seat. Rylin wants to know the nature of that contact — audiences granted, favors done, what the Duke's situation actually looks like from street level rather than correspondence.
- **Supernatural or divine events.** Aestrum is a small duchy. If something unusual happened — a miracle, a divine visitation, a magical phenomenon — word travels fast in confined spaces. Rylin wants the shape of it: what occurred, who was present, what interpretation the locals have settled on.
- **Faction affiliations.** Who the party serves, who they avoid, who they owe. Even partial answers are useful. Erethezra maintains interests across the region; knowing where a group of capable travelers sits on the board costs him nothing to learn.
- **What they were doing here.** People in a remote duchy either belong there or don't. If they don't, Rylin wants to know why they came and what they found.

**Baldric as pre-loaded pipeline:** Whatever the party told Baldric before boarding, Rylin already has. Baldric repeats everything without filter — Rylin enters any conversation with passengers already holding fragments. He uses those fragments to triangulate, to confirm, and to identify what the party is choosing not to say.

**How Rylin extracts information:** He does not interrogate. He creates conditions. Rick Hastley manages social dynamics — warm, curious, easy to talk to — and is the primary conversational tool. Night watches are Rylin's preferred instrument: the dark, the sea, the absence of the rest of the party. Long silences are not uncomfortable to Rylin. He waits.

**Baldric as misinformation channel:** The channel runs both directions. Baldric delivers what he hears from passengers to Rylin (without knowing he does), and delivers what Rylin feeds him back to passengers (without knowing he does that either). A piece of false intelligence dropped casually into Baldric's ear reaches any passenger who will talk to him within the hour.

**Captain's quarters:** Rylin occupies the captain's quarters as acting captain. He locks the door at night — standard operational security for an operative sleeping in a shared space with unknown quantities aboard.

**Passenger recognition — Miri Amblecrown:** If a passenger introduces herself as Miri Amblecrown, or is introduced by name, Rylin will recognize the name immediately. He killed the captain partly to suppress information about Duke Amblecrown's search for his missing daughter. He is now looking at the subject of that search. He will not react visibly. He will file it and reassess — everything aboard just changed value. Miri alive, free, and in conversation with her father is worth more to Erethezra's intelligence picture than anything else this voyage could produce. He will not harm her. He will listen to everything she says.

**Intel trade mechanic:** Rylin does not give freely; he trades. If passengers provide substantial intelligence about conditions in Aestrum — the political situation, supernatural events, who they dealt with and why — he will consider reciprocating at departure with something of commensurate value. The Skaarsdam file is his most immediately relevant asset for any party headed toward that region. See saved overlay for specific offer and trigger conditions.
