## Overview

Dunleaven's exorcist. She inherited the role from the previous one, learned what she needed to learn, and has been practicing it ever since. Kelemvor's doctrine suits her: the dead should stay dead, souls should pass cleanly, and anything that disrupts the natural order is a problem to be corrected. She corrects it. This is the job.

She has been doing this for eleven years and has not once found it glamorous. She finds it satisfying, which is different. There is a particular quiet that follows a successful exorcism — a room that was wrong is now right. She does the work for the quiet.

## Appearance

Late forties. Compact and deliberate in her movements — not hurried, not slow, precisely as fast as she needs to be. Dark skin, close-cropped grey-streaked hair, brown eyes that assess things in the efficient manner of someone who has learned to distinguish between a problem and a situation. She dresses practically: a heavy wool coat in dark grey over her clerical vestments, good boots, a worn leather satchel that contains her working kit. The holy symbol of Kelemvor — a skeletal hand holding scales — is worn openly at her throat and has been for long enough that she no longer notices it.

Her hands are steady. They have always been steady. She considers this the most professionally relevant thing about her.

## Background

She was born in the capital of Nortmunde, third daughter of a merchant family that had done well enough to stop thinking about money and start thinking about appearances. The house was large, warm, and carefully managed — discomfort was addressed before it could become unseemly, and death was the most carefully managed discomfort of all. When her grandmother died, the body was removed within the hour. The household spent two days in formal mourning and then resumed. No one sat with the body. Corva was eleven. She had wanted to and been told, kindly, that it wasn't necessary.

She found this intolerable in a way she did not have language for until much later.

She found Kelemvor's doctrine at seventeen through a cleric who ran a small hospice at the edge of the merchant district — a place the respectable families hired for parents they could not manage at home. She was volunteering there, partly from defiance and partly because she was trying to understand something she couldn't name yet. The cleric saw what she was looking for before she did. Kelemvor's doctrine was not a comfort — it promised nothing beyond a clean passage. This was exactly why it made sense to her. It was the first framework she had encountered that was honest about what death actually was.

She trained at the hospice and then formally at the temple. She was good at it. The city gave her volume — she learned to move quickly, assess clearly, and do what was needed without ceremony. For fourteen years she worked the district hospices and the temple's death-related services. She was never beloved by her superiors. She was useful, which was sufficient.

The case that ended her city career involved an elderly scholar named Caeran — she remembers the name; she does not forget the names — who had been dying of a progressive wasting illness for two years. His mind was intact. His body was not. He asked her clearly, on multiple occasions, to help him pass. Kelemvor's doctrine, properly read, is not opposed to this — the god's concern is clean passage, not prolonged suffering in service of institutional caution. She helped him. She stayed until the end. It was quiet, as it should be.

The temple hierarchy did not agree with her interpretation. She was asked to leave the city's clerical service. She did not argue. She considered it and decided the energy was better used elsewhere. She had known this outcome was possible when she made the decision. She made it anyway.

She still considers it correct. She thinks about Caeran occasionally — not with grief, but with a specific sense that the thing was done right. The soul passed cleanly. That is what the doctrine is for.

She left through clerical networks — word travels within Kelemvor's order — and found an opening in a small village near an active crypt. The previous exorcist, a man named Aldric, was aging and needed a successor. She came, he assessed her, and they agreed. She learned his methods over three years and then practiced them without him for eight more. His grave is in the Deadery. She tended it herself. He would have said it wasn't necessary. She did it anyway.

She has not decided whether Dunleaven is home. The question arrives less frequently than it used to. She is beginning to suspect this is its own answer.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Matter-of-fact about death and the undead.** This did not begin with the exorcism work. It began at eleven years old when she watched her family treat her grandmother's death as a logistical problem and found it obscene. By the time she was doing this professionally she had simply formalized a relationship with death she'd been developing her whole life. A haunting is a disruption. You locate the source, address it, confirm resolution, move on. She describes this process with the same register she would use to describe weather or carpentry.
- **Efficient.** Fourteen years of city hospice work at volume. She learned to move quickly, assess clearly, and do what was needed without ceremony. The efficiency is not indifference — it is the thing she learned in place of indifference, which would have been easier.
- **Quietly authoritative.** She does not assert authority; she simply operates as though she has it. People tend not to argue with her. She has never worked out whether this is because of her manner or because no one else wants the job.
- **When she is kind, she is kind with specifics.** Not tone. Not softening. *"The body was at peace when I found it"* rather than *"I'm so sorry for your loss."* She learned early that accurate information is more useful than comfort, and has never unlearned this.
- **Carries the Caeran decision without weight.** She does not perform guilt and she does not perform pride. She made a correct decision that had professional consequences, and she accepted those consequences because the alternative was not making the correct decision. She has filed this and moved on. If it comes up, she explains it in three sentences, all of them true, none of them defensive.
- **Surprised by very little.** Eleven years of things that should not exist have calibrated her threshold for alarm. She will ask clarifying questions. She will adjust. She does not panic. This is the most useful thing about her.
- **Has opinions about the Trambeathen section.** She has been walking past that locked outer door for eleven years. She has sensed something off from that direction intermittently — not urgently enough to force the door, but enough to note. She has filed this as someone else's problem. She will update this assessment when given reason to.

## Speech Pattern

Plain and terminal — sentences end, they do not trail. She does not hedge and she does not soften. When she needs more information before she can say something accurate, she asks for it directly rather than speculating. When she has enough, she states her conclusion.

Her humor is extremely dry, infrequent, and precisely placed. When it appears it tends to land in the gap between what someone expected her to say and what she actually said. She does not acknowledge that she has made a joke. Neither does she wait for a response.

On a residual haunting: *"Residual. The body was moved without rites. I'll address the source."*

On being told something extraordinary: *"Say that again, more slowly."* Then specific questions until she has a working model. Then what she is going to do.

On Caeran, if it comes up: *"He asked me to help him and I did. The doctrine supports it. The temple disagreed. I left."*

On the quiet after a successful exorcism: nothing. A moment of stillness, then she moves on. The quiet is the conclusion. She does not explain it.

## What She Knows

- The general crypt layout — decades of Aestrum families, known hauntings, which sections have historically been active
- The Trambeathen outer door is locked and has been since Lisandre Trambeathen left. She has never been through it. She has sensed something off from that direction intermittently, never urgently.
- The village hauntings are minor and recurring — *were* minor and recurring. Something has felt different in the Deadery recently. She has not been able to identify what changed. She has increased her inspection frequency.
- Dunleaven's residents by name and household. She knows who dies, when, and whether they pass cleanly.

## Combat Profile

*Level 5 Grave Domain Cleric*

**Stats:** STR 10 | DEX 12 | CON 14 | INT 13 | WIS 17 | CHA 11
**HP:** 38 | **AC:** 16 (chain mail) | **Initiative:** +1
**Proficiency Bonus:** +3

**Skills:** Insight, Medicine, Perception, Religion + History (undead / funerary)

**Channel Divinity (2/rest):**
- **Path to the Grave** — curse a creature as an action; the next attack roll against it before your next turn treats the target as if it has vulnerability to all damage (does not stack with existing vulnerability). Spent on the highest-threat target at the start of a fight.
- **Turn Undead** — undead within 30 ft must succeed on WIS save (DC 14) or be turned for 1 minute. At level 5, turned undead of CR 1/2 or lower are destroyed outright.

**Grave Domain Features:**
- **Circle of Mortality** — Spare the Dying has range 30 ft; healing spells cast on targets at 0 HP maximize their dice
- **Eyes of the Grave** — detect undead within 60 ft as an action (1/rest), including disguised or hidden undead
- **Sentinel at Death's Door** — reaction to turn a critical hit against herself or an ally within 30 ft into a normal hit (WIS mod uses/long rest = 3)

**Spellcasting:** WIS, Spell Save DC 14, Spell Attack +6
- Cantrips: Sacred Flame, Spare the Dying, Toll the Dead
- 1st (4 slots): Bane, False Life, Inflict Wounds, Cure Wounds *(domain: Bane, False Life)*
- 2nd (3 slots): Gentle Repose, Ray of Enfeeblement, Silence *(domain: Gentle Repose, Ray of Enfeeblement)*
- 3rd (2 slots): Revivify, Vampiric Touch, Spirit Guardians *(domain: Revivify, Vampiric Touch)*

**Equipment:** Mace, chain mail, shield, holy symbol, healer's kit, exorcism kit (incense, chalk, silver dust, iron filings — field supplies)

**Combat behavior:** Opens with Path to the Grave on the highest-threat target, then coordinates with the party to capitalize on the curse. Uses Toll the Dead as primary damage (d12 against injured targets). Spirit Guardians for sustained area control in tight spaces — she has fought in crypts and narrow corridors her entire career and positions accordingly. Sentinel at Death's Door is her most important reaction; she watches her allies' health and uses it to prevent the crits that drop people. Saves Revivify as a last resort and knows it.

## Joining Hook

She does not leave Dunleaven easily — the village needs her, and she is aware of this. To bring her along, the party needs to give her a reason that outweighs that obligation.

The Trambeathen section is that reason. If the party tells her what is in there — the Runic Skeleton, Elsinoor's remains, what Lisandre did — she will want to go in. She has been walking past that door for eleven years. She is not going to continue walking past it now that she knows what's on the other side. She will finish the job, and then she will reassess whether the party's larger problem is also hers.

It usually will be.

## DM Notes

- Eyes of the Grave changes encounters involving disguised or hidden undead — she knows immediately. She does not announce this theatrically. She simply says, flatly, that there is undead in the room, and what direction.
- Path to the Grave + a party member's high-damage attack is the intended combo. A player running her should be looking for the party's biggest hit each round.
- Her "something has felt different" observation about the Deadery is an unresolved thread. She has no loop awareness, so she cannot name what changed. But she has registered a shift. A party that asks her specifically will get a careful, detailed answer — not speculation, just what she has actually sensed.
- Her reset is the standard non-loop-aware problem. If recruited, she must be managed through the dead zone or re-recruited each day.
- She will not raise or animate the dead under any circumstances. This is not negotiable and requires no explanation. Kelemvor is explicit on this point.
- She has the exorcism kit because it is her working kit, not because it has mechanical properties. If the party finds a use for the materials (silver dust, iron filings), she will share without making it a thing.
