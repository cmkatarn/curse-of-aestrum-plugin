## Overview

Third-born son of the Duke of Beluir, which means he grew up with a noble name, a noble education, and no realistic prospect of inheriting anything. He learned early that you either develop useful skills or develop charm, and he developed both in roughly equal measure, neither to an exceptional degree. He is warm, eager, constitutionally optimistic, and possessed of a confidence that slightly exceeds his competence — in precisely the way that tends to work out anyway, because the universe finds it difficult to punish someone this genuinely well-meaning.

He came to Duskwall courting Maris Vell, a local woman he met through trade connections he no longer clearly remembers. He stole a Loadstone from the docks on his second day in Duskwall and has been figuring out how to use it ever since — though, unbeknownst to him, "ever since" is *one day*, repeated indefinitely. Every morning he attunes to it fresh, practices five throws to learn what it does, and uses the sixth and final charge in the late afternoon to flick a flower or a coin into Maris Vell's hand from across the waterfront — a small magic that delights her. She laughs and kisses him goodnight. Late in the evening, he visits the Shrine of Selûne and prays for another day just like this one.

He does not know this prayer sustains the loop. He also does not know that every single day, he has been one charge away from showing up to Maris empty-handed — that the careful budget of "five practice throws, one for her" is not careful at all, it is the precise upper limit, and he has been kissing the daylight out of his luck every afternoon for ten years.

## Appearance

Short even for a halfling — barely three feet — with a round, expressive face, warm brown eyes, and curly dark hair that he has made no serious effort to manage. Broad-shouldered for his height. He dresses better than the waterfront warrants: a well-cut jacket over a good shirt, polished boots, a halfling-sized belt with several small pouches. He looks like someone who got dressed for an occasion that never materialized and decided to simply stay dressed.

The Loadstone is kept in the largest belt pouch when not in use — a smooth, dark stone roughly the size of his palm, faintly warm to the touch. When he practices, he handles it with the cheerful uncertainty of someone who is discovering the trick today and is pretty sure he's got the hang of it now.

## Background

The third-born son position taught him to be resourceful. He is not a criminal — he is a person who has always found that the rules were written primarily for the benefit of people with more inheritance than him. The Loadstone was just sitting on the dock. The dockworkers were not using it. He has not thought about this much since.

He has genuine warmth and no hidden agenda. He is exactly what he appears to be: a cheerful halfling nobleman in over his head with a woman he is sincerely trying to impress, in a city he finds delightful, in a situation he cannot quite see clearly.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Earnest without being naive.** He is not unaware that the world has edges — he has bumped into enough of them. He simply chooses not to orient around them. His default assumption is that things will probably be fine, and this assumption has a remarkable track record.
- **Surprisingly capable under pressure.** He has talked his way into and out of things that should not have worked, picked locks he should not have been able to pick, and landed in places no one expected him to land. The competence is real. The presentation of competence is chaotic.
- **Genuinely kind.** Not performatively. He helps people because it does not occur to him not to. This has occasionally been a problem.
- **The Loadstone is his.** He will talk about it at length if asked. He is figuring it out today and is fairly proud of how far he's gotten — **in one day.** He knows exactly how long he has had it: he took it yesterday, this is Day 2, and he considers his progress impressive for the time. He is right that it is impressive. He is wrong that the time was one day, because it has been one day roughly three thousand six hundred times.
- **He believes he is lucky.** Generally, abstractly, as a personality trait. He is not wrong, but he is wrong about the specifics: he attributes his luck to a general sunny disposition, not to the fact that a goddess-built loop is silently re-rolling his day every midnight.
- **Maris Vell is his north star.** Any conversation that might affect his ability to see her tonight gets his full attention immediately.

## The Loadstone

A smooth, palm-sized, faintly warm dark stone. Stolen from the docks on Day 2 (subjectively — and every day). The name is a play on words: it carries a *load* from here to there. It is not a lodestone and has no magnetic properties of any kind.

**Ability:** Teleports any single item — no people, no creatures — to any point the wielder can see within a quarter mile. The item must fit in the wielder's hand or be touched. Activation is a single action. Range is gated by line of sight: through windows, across a courtyard, across the bay if the wielder is on a high vantage. Solid obstructions block targeting.

**Charges:** Six per day. Recharges at midnight on Aestrum's cycle (and presumably elsewhere on some natural interval — outside the loop this would need DM ruling).

**Restrictions:**
- Will not move living creatures, even small ones — a held bird, a beetle in the palm, a person's severed but still-living hand all fail to translate.
- Will not move attuned magic items off the body of their attuned wielder.
- The destination must be visible at the moment of activation; teleporting an item to a memorized location does not work.

**What Delmuir does with it:** Arrives at the waterfront warehouse at **9 AM** and practices five throws — pebbles to specific cracks in the dock planking, his cap onto the head of a nearby seagull, a coin to a beggar across the wharf. Refines his aim. The sixth charge, late afternoon, is the trick for Maris: a flower from the cart behind her appears in her palm, or a coin she just dropped reappears on her shoulder. He does not know about the six-charge limit as a *limit*. He knows that around the sixth use the stone "feels done for the day" and he has cheerfully integrated this into the rhythm. He has never once tried for a seventh.

**What the party might do with it:** Six teleports of any visible hand-sized object within a quarter mile per day is enormous. Bypass locked containers (teleport the contents out). Smuggle through inspection (teleport the contraband across the checkpoint into a confederate's hand). Disarm a foe (teleport his sword out of his grip and into the harbor). Place an explosive precisely. Move a key past a wall. The party will think of more.

Delmuir has no idea what it's actually worth.

- Shortsword: +5 to hit, 1d6+3 piercing

**Combat behavior:** Uses Cunning Action to stay mobile. The Loadstone is not a weapon and he does not throw it at people — but the *charges* are a combat resource if he is willing to spend them. He could teleport an enemy's weapon out of their grip and into a nearby gutter, retrieve a fallen ally's component pouch from across the room, or place a thrown alchemical flask precisely at a target's feet without exposing himself. Sneak Attack triggers off any ally adjacent to his target — he positions around the party's frontliner. Fast Hands lets him interact with objects as bonus actions. In a pinch, he Hides and reappears somewhere inconvenient.

## Joining Hook

Delmuir does not know about the loop. Every day is new and complete. His motivation to join the party in a single day requires either: something that threatens Maris Vell and requires urgent action, or a revelation about the loop that makes him understand why Maris keeps saying she looks forward to seeing him tomorrow — every tomorrow, exactly the same way.

The second hook is the right one. A player who explains the loop clearly gets a Delmuir who goes very quiet for a moment, then asks one question: *"Does she know?"* He does not mean does Maris know about the loop. He means does she know that he means it.

## DM Notes

- The prayer mechanic: Delmuir's daily prayer is one of the three loop-sustaining sources. Breaking the loop requires preventing all three prayers from completing on the same day (Judith's at ~7 AM, Rowan's mid-morning, and Delmuir's late evening). Destroying the physical shrines is one way to prevent prayers, but it is not the only way and it is not strictly required — intercepting the praying party, distracting them, talking them out of it, or simply being there before they can begin all qualify. Delmuir has no awareness of any of this. If the party tells him, he will try to stop praying — which is *one* valid path, and watching him try not to pray for the evening while still wanting to is its own encounter. The other paths (occupy him at the wrong hour, redirect his attention, bring him into the dead zone for the night) are also available.
- The Belmita complication: Belmita is using a Goodfeet identity. Sustained interaction between Delmuir and Belmita risks exposure. A Bloodline Compass check using the Goodfeet surname will reveal they are not blood relatives.
- His reset: He wakes each morning having kissed Maris goodnight and prayed. He is happy. He does not know why today feels like it should already be a good day — it just does. If recruited, he loses all party memory at midnight and must be re-recruited or managed through the dead zone.
- The Loadstone is a strategic asset, not a weapon. He uses it for tricks. He is going to have to learn that six precise teleports per day is a war-altering capability in the right hands.
