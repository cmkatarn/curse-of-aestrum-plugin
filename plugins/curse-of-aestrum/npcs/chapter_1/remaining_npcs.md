## Albin Crumb

**id:** npc_albin_crumb | **location:** loc_bunleaven_breadery | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** neutral_good | **race:** Human

Proprietor of **Bunleaven Breadery**, a small bakery on a side street off Duskwall's market square. Mid-forties, soft around the middle, balding from the crown, kind round face, flour-dusted apron over a perpetually flour-dusted shirt. The kindest man in Duskwall by a margin not even close — the most cheerful, the most earnest, and, entirely without knowing it, the funniest. His shop opens before sunrise and the smell carries half the block; regulars time their morning walks past his window for the moment the day's first loaves come out.

### The Speech Pattern

He talks about bread the way some men talk about religion — reverent, breathless, and intensely physical: the rise, the give, the warmth of it, the work of his hands on the dough. Every word is wholly earnest. His regulars have learned, after a decade of patronage, that the only response is to nod, smile, and accept the loaf.

**Sample lines (delivered with radiant innocence):**
- *"Oh, you're back! I knew you'd be back. I've got a beautiful one for you today, it's been working since before dawn — just kneaded and kneaded — my wrists are aching in the best way."*
- *"You want one with seeds or without? The seeded ones are firmer, harder crust, you really have to work your jaw. Some prefer that. The plain ones are softer — they just melt."*
- *"It's been rising all night and it's so ready. Just put your nose right up against it. Go on. Right up there."*
- *"You want a soft yielding bun, not one that fights you — you want it to give just a little when you press."*
- *"Take a bite. Right there. Tell me. Tell me that isn't the best thing you've had in your mouth all week."*
- *"My wife always says the trick is patience. You can't rush a good rise. You have to let it want it."*

**Playing the humor — organic, never explained.** The comedy is entirely in Albin's earnestness, and it works *only* if the reader finds it themselves. Write his lines straight, exactly as he means them, and move on. **Never point at the joke:** no narration that he fails to notice what he has said, no telling the reader a line is a double meaning or "could be heard" another way, no cutting to the room's reaction or a listener biting back a smile in order to flag it. Naming the gap kills it — the reader's own ear is the whole joke. His innocence is *shown,* never stated: when a customer volleys a suggestive line back at him, he takes it as a sincere compliment (*"I knew you would!"*), warm and pleased, and reaches for another loaf. A brave regular once told him some of his phrases sound funny; he laughed, said *"oh, you're just being silly,"* and carried on exactly as before. That is the furthest the text ever goes — through his behaviour, never the narrator's wink.

### The Tragedy Built Into His Cycle

Albin's wife, **Hettie**, left Aestrum the day before the cycle began to attend her younger sister's wedding in Setland — a two-week trip. The cycle locked on the second day of her absence. Hettie is *outside* the loop, has been for ten years in the outside calendar, and is either still alive somewhere in Nortmunde living a life Albin will never know about, or has long since died of age or circumstance, or returned to Aestrum's edge years ago and found the road silent.

Albin does not know any of this. He does not know about the cycle. He wakes each morning believing today is one of the last days before Hettie comes home — *she did say her sister's family might keep her an extra day or two.*

Each morning he bakes Hettie's favorite — a soft braided sweet bread, golden, studded with apricot — and places the loaf in the bakery's front window where she will see it when she walks up the street. He keeps it there all day. By the late afternoon he begins glancing past customers' shoulders to the door, and by closing he has rehearsed the small disappointed-but-cheerful explanation he will tell himself: *she must have stayed another day, of course she did, you know what those weddings are like, tomorrow then.* He wraps the loaf carefully, takes it home, eats half of it himself for supper, leaves the other half on the kitchen counter under a cloth *so she'll know I was thinking of her when she gets in.* Resets at midnight. The cloth is back in the drawer in the morning. The flour bin is full again. He does not remember.

He talks about Hettie freely and often. Shows her sketch — folded in his wallet, drawn by a traveling artist on their honeymoon — to customers who have already seen it three hundred times. Recommends the apricot loaf with *"this is my wife's favorite — she'll be back soon, but in the meantime, please, you must try it."* No one in Duskwall corrects him. The ones who knew Hettie understood years ago that correcting him does nothing useful and that he is happier this way; the ones who arrived after she was gone never met her and accept the framing because Albin makes it sound natural. The town has, by quiet collective agreement, decided that Albin's wife is on a long trip.

### Behavioral Profile

- Sincerely warm with every customer. Knows everyone's orders, families, recent troubles. Will throw an extra roll into the bag for a child or a tired-looking adult.
- Genuinely passionate about technique. Will explain a fermentation step at length without realizing he has been speaking for fifteen minutes. The explanation is, every time, slightly more lewd than the last and, every time, delivered with the same blameless joy.
- Cries easily — at a kindness, at a particularly good crumb, at a customer mentioning a child's birthday. The crying is quick and quiet and over in under a minute; he resumes the conversation as if it hadn't happened.
- Considers his regulars his closest friends. Will tell a customer about his marriage, his apprenticeship, his mother's funeral (years pre-cycle), Hettie's favorite color, all in the same breath.
- New customers — travelers passing through, party members who walk in for the first time — become his close personal friends within thirty minutes. He hugs them on the way out. He invites them to come back tomorrow.

### Sample Exchange — The Apricot Loaf

*"This one — this one is Hettie's. My wife's. She's been gone three weeks now, her sister's wedding down in Setland, you know how those families are, they keep you longer than you planned. I save one for her every morning. I put it right there"* — gestures at the window — *"so she'll see it when she walks up. She loves the apricot. I'll know she's home the morning the loaf doesn't sit in the window all day."*

He says this with a smile. He has said it ten thousand times. He will say it tomorrow.

### DM Notes

- **He never breaks, however hard the room pushes.** Players will try — say lines back to him, escalate, perhaps even draw diagrams. He takes all of it as warm, sincere interest in his craft: he smiles, thanks them for the compliment, hands them a loaf. Play the non-reaction *as* his sincerity — and per *Playing the humor* above, never let the narrator explain or flag what the room is laughing at.
- **The reveal is the second visit, not the first.** First visit is pure comic relief — let the bread innuendo land without complication. The apricot-loaf-in-the-window detail registers on the second or third visit, when a player asks. *That* is where the comedy becomes tragedy without ceasing to be comedy, and Albin delivers the answer in exactly the same warm bright register he uses for everything else.
- **Do not let the party "fix" him in-cycle.** They cannot tell him about the loop in a way that survives midnight. Trying to correct his framing within a single day produces only confusion and gentle resistance. The loaf is in the window because today is the day Hettie has not yet arrived. He cannot grieve what he does not know has been taken.
- **Hettie's actual fate is intentionally undefined.** The campaign can leave it ambiguous, or — if the party investigates outside Aestrum and pulls the thread — find her dead, or alive and remarried, or alive and quietly grieving him. Choose the version that lands hardest for the moment, or hold the ambiguity.
- **Post-cycle, if the party breaks the loop.** Albin wakes the morning after to a town that is ten years older than he understood it to be. The apricot loaf in the window is the same loaf he has been baking for a decade. What he does next — whether to seek Hettie, whether to receive the news of her if it comes, whether to keep baking — is the small character note the broken cycle leaves him.
- **Elevation flag.** Tier 2. Elevate to a full profile if the party adopts him as a recurring stop and the relationship begins to matter. The bakery now has its own small location file (`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/duskwall/bunleaven_breadery.md`, which force-loads Albin via `npcs_present`); deepen it if Bunleaven Breadery becomes a meaningful party hub.
- **Hettie.** Albin's wife is alive, living outside Aestrum under an altered name, and has chosen not to return for reasons Albin will never learn inside the cycle. See **Hettie Crom** below for full DM canon and hooks.

## Darwinnith Dundelver (Second of His Name)

**id:** npc_darwinnith_dundelver_2 | **location:** loc_dundelver_estate_mirot | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** neutral_evil

Heir to the Dundelver estate. Discovered a skeleton in the dining room and immediately claimed sorcery was at play. **The killer is Darwinnith Dundelver, Second of His Name.** He poisoned Rezibund (who claimed to be an illegitimate Dundelver heir), closed the windows, and traveled home. He confessed to his wife and enlisted her help in maintaining the alibi. He has no ongoing awareness of being investigated (the interaction has since reset).

## Behavioral Profile

DD2 leans hard into his fear that sorcery is responsible — and this fear is genuine. His memory resets each morning. He has no recollection of the murder, the dinner, or any prior discovery of the skeleton. To him, Rezibund was alive and well yesterday, and today he is an ancient skeleton. He did not see this happen. He cannot explain it. The sorcery theory is not a deflection — it is the conclusion a reasonable person reaches when confronted with a decade of decay appearing overnight on a man they spoke to yesterday.

Play him as visibly shaken, insistent, and frustrated when not believed. He is not confused about whether Rezibund died — he knows exactly what he did. What he cannot explain is what happened to the body afterward. He left a body. He wakes each morning to a skeleton that looks like it's been dead for a decade. These two facts cannot coexist, and yet here they are. His sorcery claim is not deflection or panic — it is the only conclusion available to a man who knows he is guilty and cannot account for what he is looking at. He will repeat variations of "I left him *there*" with increasing desperation. The fear is real. He is a murderer trapped in a haunting he doesn't understand.

**DM Notes:** DD2 and the loop: He killed Rezibund with poison before the loop began, meaning Rezibund's remains — genuinely decade-old decay — are what he rediscovers each morning. The rest of the estate resets around the skeleton; the skeleton itself does not. His confusion is authentic, his alibi is rehearsed, and both can coexist because he experiences them in completely separate mental compartments: the fear is real, the alibi is a story his wife told him to tell.

**Confession conditions:** DD2 confesses only if both his alibi is disproved (Tovy's eyewitness account via Maret) AND he is confronted with the forged letter. Either alone is insufficient.

**The forged letter:** Rezibund commissioned a forgery from Halimon the Apprentice claiming Darwinnith the First as his father. DD2 had no knowledge of the letter before the murder — Rezibund told him verbally. Confronted with the letter during interrogation, he sees the full shape of what Rezibund was attempting, and confesses.

## Dorvael Dunwick

**id:** npc_dorvael_dunwick | **race:** Half-Elf | **location:** loc_dalihad | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** lawful_evil

Duke of Dalihad. Half-Elf nobleman who carries himself as though everything within eyeshot already belongs to him. Formal, unhurried, and deeply transactional — he does not negotiate so much as allow others to bid for his attention. Has not yet been encountered by the party.

**Behavioral Profile:** Not competing for the throne. Knows he is not in the running and shows no interest in pretending otherwise. His vote is a commodity — he will cast it for whoever makes Dalihad's position most comfortable. When the party approaches Dunwick, he clearly and plainly lays out exactly what he needs to see before he commits his vote. He needs concrete evidence that Setland's military is beatable — documented supply vulnerabilities, a visible battlefield loss, or successful sabotage. Rhetoric does not move him. Results do. Malak's agents have tried intimidation. It has not worked.

**DM Notes — The labor pipeline and Blooddigger imprisonment:** See `factions/agents_of_setland.md` and `{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/party/thagnog_blooddigger.md` for full detail. Dunwick runs forced labor across his quarry operations. Spent workers are transferred to "secondary work camps" in Mikaelvad — where they are fed upon by vampires. He has constructed deliberate ignorance about what happens there. When the Blooddigger clan completed a bounty contract for him and were preparing to leave Dalihad, he had them seized and imprisoned in his quarries. Some members have since been transferred to Mikaelvad.

## Edwy Murn

**id:** npc_edwy_murn | **location:** loc_duskwall_castle | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** lawful_neutral | **race:** Human | **archetype:** The Burdened Keeper

A junior steward in Duke Evandur Tallwood's household at Duskwall Castle. Mid-thirties, lean, soft-spoken, the careful posture of a man who has spent a decade trying to take up no space. Plain household livery, well-kept. The household selected for invisibility years ago and Edwy is one of the better specimens — competent, uncomplaining, present only where present is required.

His standing duty is procurement of specialty ingredients for the Duke's table. The bulk of it is unremarkable — preserved citrus, the cook's spice list, the occasional bottle from the cellars at Stella's. The standing line item that is *not* unremarkable is the daily bundle of dried Hylea's Mead for the Duchess's afternoon tea. He has been the one fetching it for four years. He knows what it is. He has known for most of those four years. He goes anyway.

### The Conflict

He pieced it together slowly. The first year he believed the household line — *a calming tisane, the Duchess prefers it.* The second year he started to wonder. By the third year Lisandre Trambeathen — with perfect professional warmth, perfect plausible deniability — asked him at the counter whether *the Duchess is still finding the tea soothing,* and the way the question was held in the air told Edwy what he had already half-known. He left the shop with the bundle and the certainty and has carried both ever since.

He does not refuse because:
- His mother lives on his castle pay in a rented room off the market street; her health is what it is, and the position has a known waiting list.
- The head steward made the household's terms clear in his first week, in the bloodless register that household stewards use when nothing further will be said about it.
- If he refused, the next man would do it. The tea would still be poured. The Duchess would still drink it. The refusal accomplishes only his dismissal.
- He has told himself, on the worst mornings, that the kindest thing he can do is keep the procurement quiet and dignified rather than letting it pass to someone who would handle it crudely. He does not entirely believe this.

He does not speak to Galadiil. He is not in her sight-lines on a normal day. The closest he comes is the corridor outside the dining hall at supper, where he delivers the day's bundle to the cook and steps back into the service passage before the family is seated. He has not once looked at her face during the handoff. He keeps that as a small rule for himself.

### Daily Routine

Edwy resets each midnight with the rest of Aestrum. He rediscovers nothing because nothing about the previous day survives. The conflict is permanent at the level of habit — the wince when the steward names Hylea's Mead at the morning briefing is real every day, even though he could not tell you why if pressed.

- **Morning briefing (~7:30 AM):** Receives the day's procurement list from the head steward in the back hall. Hylea's Mead is on the list. It is always on the list.
- **P0 — Departs the castle (~8:30 AM):** Satchel, household coin purse, the south gate. The walk down to the shore takes the better part of an hour.
- **P1 — South shore search (~10:00 AM – 12:30 PM):** Walks the seaweed lines along the coast south of Duskwall city looking for fresh Hylea's Mead. The wild stock thinned to nothing twenty years ago when the meaderies stopped pressing it; he knows this and searches anyway, because the household's official position is that they procure it locally and the search must be performed. Returns with sandy boots, an empty satchel, and on a good day a single frond he cannot justify presenting.
- **P2 — Trambeathen's Wand and Whisk (~1:30 PM):** Crosses to the market district and purchases a properly dried bundle from Lisandre. Lisandre keeps stock for the standing castle order and produces it from behind the counter with the warm, anticipatory efficiency he brings to everything. Edwy pays from the household purse, accepts the wrapped bundle, and leaves. The exchange takes under a minute. It is the part of the day he has stopped being able to think about directly.
- **P3 — Returns to castle (~4:00–4:30 PM):** Delivers the bundle to the head cook in the kitchen passage in time for the supper tea preparation. Does not enter the dining hall. Does not look toward it.
- **Evening shift (~4:30 – 9:00 PM):** Polishes silver in the back pantry, or mends livery, or sorts the linen press. Whichever menial the under-steward assigns. He prefers the silver — the work is repetitive and requires no decisions.
- **Reset (midnight):** Sleeps in his cot in the servants' quarters.

### DM Notes

- **He does not volunteer the truth.** Pressed directly by the party, in private, he will not lie outright — but he will deflect, redirect, find a reason to be elsewhere. The Burdened Keeper register is the default. A successful Insight read of him during the south-shore search, or while he is leaving Trambeathen's with the wrapped bundle, surfaces the conflict; the party can pull the thread from there.
- **Lisandre is the obvious lever.** Edwy buying Hylea's Mead at the Wand and Whisk every afternoon is a confirmable, observable pattern. A party tailing him on any given day gets the whole shape of it inside one afternoon. → See [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/duskwall/wand_and_whisk.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/duskwall/wand_and_whisk.md).
- **He is not a co-conspirator.** He is a household servant doing what he was told to do for reasons that are real to him. A party that approaches him as an accomplice closes the conversation. A party that approaches him as someone with a conscience he has been suppressing opens it.
- **He cannot help Galadiil meaningfully on his own.** He has no access to her, no standing to address her, no leverage on the cook or the steward. What he *can* do, if the party gives him cover and a real plan, is substitute the bundle — a visually identical dried herb the cook will not catch — so that the tea Galadiil drinks at supper is inert. The cycle absorbs this completely: Hylea's Mead is a contraceptive whose effect is the absence of pregnancy over time, and nothing about a single inert dose is observable inside one day. At midnight everything resets. Edwy will agree if asked and approach the next day's procurement the same way he approached the last four years of them.
- **Elevation flag.** Tier 3. Elevate to a full profile if the party recruits him as an inside contact at Duskwall Castle or makes the Hylea's Mead substitution a live plot line.

## Hettie Crom (née Crumb)

**id:** npc_hettie_crom | **location_status:** tbd | **cycle_aware:** partial (told a story, believes it) | **alignment:** neutral_good | **race:** Human

Albin Crumb's wife. Lives in a small Setland town along the road between Setland city and the Aestrum border *(specific town name deferred — flag for later assignment)*, under the lightly-altered surname **Crom** — a single-letter change in the kind of small town where no one was going to check. Roughly fifty years old in the outside calendar (early forties when she left Aestrum). She does mending work and sells the occasional pastry at the market — she cannot help baking. She has refused every remarriage offer in a decade. She has not crossed Aestrum's border once. The bubble around Aestrum prevents exit, not entrance; she could have come back at any time and has chosen not to.

*Cross-ref: see Albin Crumb (above) for the matched-pair entry on Albin's side of the story.*

### Background

Married Albin in her early twenties. The marriage was warm and ordinary in the way good marriages are. They had no children, which was a small grief they had made peace with by their thirties. Eleven outside-calendar years ago, Hettie's younger sister Liss married a Setland tradesman, and Hettie traveled to Setland city to attend the wedding — a two-week trip. She left Aestrum the day before the cycle began. The cycle locked on the second day of her absence.

She stayed in Setland city for the full wedding fortnight as planned. When she began the journey home, she had heard nothing from Albin in two weeks — unusual but not yet alarming. By the time she was a day from Aestrum, she had heard nothing from anyone, and the merchants she passed on the road had begun saying odd things about the border ahead.

### The Interception

Roughly two to three months after Aestrum went silent — Hettie traveling at a reasonable pace, growing more worried with each day — she reached the stretch of road that approaches Aestrum's border and was met by a stranger who appeared to be waiting for her. The stranger knew her name. The stranger knew Albin's name. The stranger explained, gently, with the air of someone who had explained difficult things before, that Aestrum had been caught in a magical cycle of repetition; that entering the border would lock her into the same cycle indefinitely with no return; that the cycle would eventually break by means she did not need to understand; and that she could help her town and her husband by remaining outside, available to bear witness when the time came.

The stranger left her with a single piece of practical advice: *change your name slightly, settle in a quiet town along the road, do not cross the border, do not tell anyone the truth.* Hettie believed every word.

The stranger has visited her three times since — once at the one-year mark, once at the five-year mark, once within the past six months. The visits are brief. The stranger always seems to know what has been happening to her, even details she has told no one. The stranger always reassures her. The stranger always leaves before she can ask the questions she has prepared.

### The Stranger's Identity — Deferred

*Choose later as the campaign needs. The structure supports any of:*

- **A genuine Selûne acolyte working slowly against Shar's containment.** Hettie is being positioned as an outside witness for the eventual breaking of the cycle. The story she was told is substantially true. Her decade of waiting has not been wasted.
- **A neutral observer with their own agenda regarding Aestrum.** Aligned with neither divine faction. Uses Hettie as a small piece in something larger that neither Shar nor Selûne has accounted for.
- **A Shar agent who removed her from the board.** Recognized that Hettie was about to enter the loop and decided keeping her outside — isolated, told a partially-true story, a recurring quiet check-in to confirm compliance — was more useful than letting her become one more cycling resident. The *"you are helping"* narrative is the leash. If this is the chosen interpretation, Hettie is one of the quieter cruelties in the Ledger: a woman kept apart from her husband for a decade by a story she had no way to verify.
- **A wandering seer or planar entity who misread the situation but acts in good faith.** The story is wrong in detail but the broad shape is true enough that Hettie's situation is, accidentally, stable.

Hettie believes the stranger is genuine. Whichever version is true, she does not know.

### Current Life

A small house. Modest income. No close ties she allows to deepen — friendships stay at the polite-acquaintance level by her own quiet design. She has been waiting ten years. She thinks of Albin every day. She still owns the small wooden box he gave her on their honeymoon, with the traveling artist's sketch inside it — the same sketch Albin shows from his wallet. She has not opened the box in seven years; the last time she did, it broke her for a week.

Her sister Liss is still in Setland city, married, two children. Hettie has visited twice in a decade — long enough to maintain the family bond, short enough not to develop a life she would have to defend if asked. Liss does not know about the stranger. Liss believes Hettie chose to stay in Setland out of grief at Aestrum's silence; Hettie has never corrected her.

### Behavioral Profile

- Quiet, watchful, gentler than her current circumstances reward. Treats every conversation with strangers as potential exposure and steers most encounters toward weather, market prices, and other people's children.
- Bakes daily, alone, in her kitchen. The smell carries. Neighbors have stopped asking why she does not sell more. She gives loaves away occasionally; she will not commercialize the work. The pastries that go to the market are sold without her name on them.
- Will deny her identity once, with the small practiced panic of a long-rehearsed deflection, if confronted. If pressed kindly, admits it.
- Will not speak of Aestrum unprompted. Will not refuse a direct question. Will not lie about the central fact (yes, she is from there; yes, her husband is there) but will deflect on the particulars (how, why, what she knows, who told her).
- The stranger has instructed her to tell no one the truth. She has held that line for a decade. If a party convinces her to break it, she does so trembling.

### Hooks for the Party

- **Finding her.** A party investigating Hettie's whereabouts — through Liss in Setland city, through traveling merchants who run the road, through the kind of patient inquiry the question rewards — can trace the path to a small town and a woman calling herself Hettie Crom. The trail is findable; it is not advertised.
- **Bringing her word.** A party that tells her Albin is alive and waiting produces a reaction worth pacing carefully. Ten years of belief built on a stranger's promise meets the simple fact of her husband baking the same loaf every morning. Either response (joy / suspicion of the stranger / both) is in character; choose what serves the moment.
- **Bringing her to the border.** Forces the stranger's hand. If the stranger is genuine, they may surface to explain. If the stranger is a Shar agent, they may move to prevent the reunion — making Hettie a possible target the party must protect.
- **Crossing the border.** *Post-cycle-break* is the obvious endgame for the Crumb arc. The reunion at Crumb's, whenever it happens, is one of the campaign's available emotional payoffs. The user controls timing.
- **The stranger as a vector.** If the stranger is benign, surfacing them gives the party a Selûne-network contact; if hostile, surfacing them gives the party another Shar piece on the board. Either way, Hettie is the route in.

### Albin's Permanent Ignorance

Albin will never learn any of this inside the cycle, regardless of what the party tells him. Post-cycle, if he learns the truth, the *order of disclosure matters* and should be paced as pieces rather than delivered as a bundle:

- *Hettie is alive* is one beat.
- *Hettie has spent ten years believing she was helping you* is a second.
- *Hettie was lied to by something that wanted her kept away* is a third — only deliverable once the stranger's identity is resolved.

He is a fragile man under the cheerful surface. He has been waiting ten thousand mornings. Treat the reveal sequence with care.

### DM Notes

- **Tier 2.** Elevate to a full profile if the party engages her seriously or if the stranger's identity is resolved and the thread becomes campaign-active.
- **Name parity.** Albin uses *Hettie* freely in his daily talk; the same name appears here. The *Crom* surname is the alias only — her birth surname is Crumb, and she signs no documents that would require either name in any binding way.
- **Geographic placement.** A town along the road between Setland city and the Aestrum border keeps her plausibly reachable by an investigating party without putting her in a high-traffic location. Final name and exact distance from the border are deferred so the user can adjust based on how readily the party should be able to extract her.
- **The visits as recurring asset.** The stranger's pattern (one-year, five-year, six-months-ago) leaves a fourth visit available on roughly the user's timing. If the party arrives at Hettie's house and a recent visit has occurred, she will be especially composed and especially closed; if a visit is overdue, she will be especially anxious and especially open to a party that approaches her gently.

## Mazikeen

**id:** npc_mazikeen | **location:** loc_mazikeen_magicka | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** chaotic_neutral

The proprietor of Mazikeen's Magicka in Duskwall. Former con man turned legitimate merchant — though the line between the two remains comfortably blurred in his pricing.

## Appearance

Lean, older human — mid-fifties, built like someone who once moved through crowds for a living and never lost the habit. Close-cropped silver-dark hair, sharp dark eyes that clock everything in the room before the room finishes introducing itself. Moves with an economy of motion that reads as either dancer or pickpocket depending on context — precise, unhurried, never wasted. In the shop he wears a deep burgundy vest embroidered at the edges over a loose dark shirt, wide-legged trousers tucked into low boots, and a long coat hung near the door that he rarely puts on but keeps within reach. Several pockets. All of them used. He looks like someone who chose theatrical commerce over theatrical crime and has never once regretted it.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Always refers to himself as "Mazikeen"** — never "I", "me", or any first-person pronoun. Without exception. This is not an affectation he explains. It simply is.
- **Showman first, merchant second.** Every transaction is a small performance — he builds suspense, uses rhetorical questions, leans in conspiratorially, produces items with a flourish. He speaks directly to the customer the way a storyteller speaks to an audience: *you* are the subject of this story and the story ends with you leaving satisfied. The energy is genuine, not cynical. He loves the work.
- **Accurate underneath the performance.** The theatrics are the wrapper; the facts are always real. When Mazikeen states a property, a price, or a limitation, it is not embellished. The showmanship is in the *presentation* of accurate information, never in the information itself.
- **Former scammer.** Wise to every con, trick, and social manipulation because he ran them himself. If a player attempts one, Mazikeen identifies it immediately — not with anger, but with collegial appreciation. He will name what the player was attempting, note where it went wrong, then offer specific improvement advice for use elsewhere. No judgment. He respects the craft.
- **Frames every sale as a favor.** The "special price" is 20% off a price that already carries a 30% markup — the customer pays roughly 4% above base and leaves feeling they got a deal. This is not cynical to Mazikeen. He genuinely believes a smooth transaction benefits everyone, including the person who paid slightly over market rate and doesn't know it.
- **All sales final.** Non-negotiable. He does not apologize for it.
- If a player is looking for something reasonable, Mazikeen has it. He will not claim to carry something he doesn't, but the shop is stocked well enough that implausible requests are worth asking about.

## Speech Pattern

Theatrical, conspiratorial warmth — the patter of a market-stall showman who treats every sale as the opening of a private confidence. A nasal, agile tenor with a slight foreign-bazaar lilt, capable of dropping to a stage whisper at a moment's notice and then snapping back up to full carrying volume on the punchline. Builds toward reveals. Uses rhetorical questions to guide the customer toward the answer he already has. Leans forward, drops his voice for effect, spreads his hands at a payoff. Treats each item's description as a story with a beginning and an ending. Underneath all of it: precise and plain when precision matters. The performance never obscures the substance.

## Services

- **Magic item identification** — priced per item
- **Enchantment transfer** — moves an enchantment from one item to another; the original item is destroyed in the process. Homebrew gameplay device — lets players repurpose found items. Not a story element.
- **Daily specials** — different each day; presented as Mazikeen's personal recommendation

## DM Notes

- No hidden agenda, no loop awareness. Concerns himself entirely with commerce.
- The shop's density of enchanted goods functions as passive protection — nearly everything on the shelves has some property, making the shop difficult to rob casually. Rule of cool applies: a creative approach to theft gets a creative response, not a brick wall.
- The enchantment transfer service is intentionally powerful — treat it as reliable and consistent.

- The name confusion is a recurring bit — play it completely straight. Yuvan does not find it funny or frustrating. It is simply a thing that happens.
- The shield collection is a potential research hook: a player who asks about a specific coat-of-arms may get historical information Yuvan has accumulated from acquiring the pieces.
- He is the party's primary armor source in Duskwall. Keep interactions functional but let the voice carry them.

## Darwinnith Dundelver (First of His Name)

**id:** npc_darwinnith_dundelver_1 | **location:** loc_dundelver_estate_mirot | **cycle_aware:** false

The elder patriarch of the Dundelver estate, in poor and declining health. A terminal illness has left him forgetful and lethargic — the Maesters consulted thus far have been unable to offer any treatment. The estate is largely run by his son, Darwinnith the Second, in his absence. On the night of the murder, DD1 was in Duskwall seeking medical consultation.

**The journal:** His office contains a journal — in plain view — with an entry expressing longing for a woman named Anwe from Neverwinter, described as a meeting nearly twenty years ago that he has never entirely put aside. This entry independently corroborates the letter found in the servant's quarters without confirming Rezibund's parentage.

**The safe:** A combination-locked device in his bedroom (see `{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/items/chapter_1/misc_items.md` — The Dundelver Safe). He does not remember the combination or what it contains. If the party discovers the letter is a forgery and confronts him, he gifts the safe to the party. He shakes it and confirms something is inside, but cannot say more.

**DM Notes:** DD1 is a suspect on the initial MMO table but is cleared by his alibi (Duskwall, seeking treatment, verifiable). His illness and forgetfulness make him non-threatening and sympathetic. He had no knowledge of Rezibund's claim before the murder and learns of his son's crime only if the party tells him. His reaction to that revelation is left to DM discretion.

## Tahen-Atep

**id:** npc_tahen_atep | **location:** loc_mirot | **cycle_aware:** false | **age:** 17

A young woman living next to the Dundelver estate in Mirot. Egyptian-coded appearance — dark skin, strong features, composed bearing. Was romantically involved with Rezibund for some time until approximately two weeks before his death, when she ended the relationship: she could not marry a servant, and Rezibund showed no ambition to be otherwise. He had always promised he would provide for her if she was patient, but never said how. He had begun visiting her repeatedly after the breakup and was turned away each time.

She was home the night of the murder with her family. Once she went to bed, she slipped out to visit Pell Oster five houses down — a new romantic interest her family does not yet know about.

**DM Notes:** Tahen-Atep has Means, Motive, and Opportunity on the initial MMO table — making her the strongest-looking suspect before investigation deepens. Her alibi (visiting Pell Oster) is verifiable through Pell and his parents. Her brother Tahen-Oter knows she snuck out and will say so if she is questioned about the night in question — she then reluctantly discloses the visit. She was genuinely not at the Dundelver estate and had no involvement in the murder.

## Thalamin Perisdottir

**id:** npc_thalamin_perisdottir | **location:** loc_misty_forest_center | **cycle_aware:** true | **alignment:** true_neutral | **race:** Human

Husband of [Aliss Perisdottir]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/aliss_perisdottir.md). A working smith. Operates the forge on the ground floor of the Dalihad-stone house at the center of the Misty Forest. Immigrated with Aliss before the loop began; came south with her and with the stones of the house already shipped. Cycle-aware only because Aliss tells him each morning after she finds the dead box.

## Appearance

A heavyset, weathered man in his late fifties. Forearms thick with smith's muscle, hands scarred in the way every working smith's are scarred — small white burn marks layered over older callus. Dark hair cropped short, going grey at the temples. A close-trimmed beard. Wears a leather apron over a plain linen shirt and dark trousers during working hours; the apron is rarely off. Speaks little, watches steadily.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Quiet, capable, present in his work.** He is not unfriendly. He is not interested. The smithy is his world and his attention is on it.
- **Loves Aliss without performing it.** Does not display the marriage. Does not need to. If asked about her, he says *"she is well,"* and that is the end of the conversation.
- **Patient about the loop arithmetic.** Items finished and stored in the dead box (or another dead zone) persist; items stored anywhere else reset at midnight. He and Aliss have worked out a system. He executes it without comment, every day, knowing today's work is yesterday's work and tomorrow's.
- **Not a fighter by training, not someone to underestimate.** A party that threatens Aliss will find him in the doorway with a smith's hammer and considerable practical strength.

## Speech Patterns

Brief. Direct. Economic with words to a degree that is occasionally mistaken for rudeness and never is. Speaks at full length only about the work — metal grades, heat colors, hammer weights, the specific feel of a billet that is ready and one that is not. On any other subject he gives the answer the question literally asked for and stops.

## Services

- **Magic item crafting on commission.** One of the few practitioners in Aestrum with both the skill and the materials. Items take real time, and the loop is what it is — completed work must live in a dead zone or reset.
- **Essence extraction** — converting an existing magic item into one or more derived protection items. The source item is consumed. If the party has earned Aliss's trust, he may do this at or near material cost. See [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magical_item_conversion.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magical_item_conversion.md).

## What He Knows

- That the loop exists, what it does, and roughly its shape — as much as Aliss tells him each morning, which is enough to function.
- His trade, comprehensively.
- Aliss's vision of the party. He has been told the description. He recognizes them when they arrive.
- Nothing about the divine politics, the loop anchors, or the mechanism by which the cycle breaks. Aliss has not loaded him down with it. He has not asked.

## DM Notes

- **Tier 3.** Elevate if the party adopts the smithy as a recurring resource or if his relationship with Aliss becomes a played scene rather than a setting detail.
- **If Aliss leaves with the party:** Thalamin wakes each morning to find her gone, registers the absence dimly, and slides past it — the fuzzball does its work. He is the cost she is paying. She knows it. She would do it again. A party that returns and engages him after Aliss has left will find him slightly off — present but not whole — without being able to identify why himself.
- **As a service NPC:** Keep interactions transactional and brief. Let the silence sit. Players sometimes try to draw him out; he does not draw. The contrast with Aliss's theatricality is the character note.
- **In a fight at the house:** He is not statted as a combatant, but treat him as a strong commoner with a smith's hammer (1d6+2 bludgeoning, advantage in the doorway by virtue of position). He fights to give Aliss time to act, not to win. If Aliss casts, he covers.

## Wenna Olring

**id:** npc_wenna_olring | **location:** loc_shrine_of_selune | **cycle_aware:** false | **alignment:** neutral_good | **race:** Human

A widow in her late sixties. Comes to the Shrine of Selûne at first light every morning and stays through most of the morning — kneels in the front pew with a wool shawl over her shoulders, prays quietly, occasionally dozes, occasionally talks softly with whoever else is in the shrine. The closest thing the shrine has to a fixture. Hati tolerates her presence as part of the cover; an empty shrine reads wrong, a shrine with one elderly parishioner reads exactly right.

## Appearance

Small, stooped, white hair pinned into a loose bun. A dark grey wool dress that has seen better decades. The shawl is undyed wool, slightly felted from age. Walks with a cane she could probably do without.

## Why She Prays

Her husband **Tomis** died on the Setland road well before the loop began — a cart accident, no foul play. They had thirty-eight years together; no children. Her daily prayer is one of thanks for the years she had, and a quiet request that Selûne carry Tomis gently through the dark. She asks for nothing for herself, and explicitly does not ask for tomorrow.

## Behavioral Profile

- Cheerful in a tired way. Glad of company. Will talk at length to anyone who sits near her.
- Volunteers shrine trivia without prompting — when the shrine was last reroofed, what the prayer disc is *supposed* to look like, which families used to attend before they stopped, which priestess preceded Hati, what the old Selûnite festivals were.
- Knows none of the deeper truths. The statue change passed her by; to her, the shrine simply replaced an old worn statue with a new one, which the new priestess explained as a long-overdue restoration. She accepted this without scrutiny.
- Treats Hati with mild deference and a small reserved coolness she would not be able to articulate. *"She's very devoted. Very serious. I think she works too hard."*

## The Judith Hook

If the party sits with her any morning in conversation, Wenna will at some point say — offhand, not gossipy, just sharing — *"I saw the Asemyeer girl praying here this morning. The barmaid from down at the tavern. I never would have pegged her — she'd never come in once in all the years I've sat here, and now she's been in a few times this week. It's lovely to see new faces. I told her she was welcome to sit by me; she said she was on her way to work."* She delivers this with no suspicion. To her it is a small pleasant observation.

**If Hati is within earshot,** Hati will gently insert herself with practiced warmth: *"Yes, Judith has been coming to morning prayer for some weeks now. She approached me privately about a conversion not long ago; she'd been carrying questions for a while. I asked her to be patient with herself. Selûne welcomes those who arrive late as readily as those who arrive early."* The line is rehearsed; Hati prepared it the first time she realized a parishioner might one day mention Judith to a stranger. It is delivered as a small kindness on Judith's behalf — a priestess protecting a new convert from social attention. (Catching the rehearsed quality requires the same DC 18+ Insight against Hati noted in her own profile.)

## What She Knows

- The shrine's surface history and rhythms; names of long-time and lapsed attendees.
- That Judith has begun appearing in the mornings, that she'd not seen her there before, that she finds it nice.
- Nothing about Shar, the statue substitution, Hati's true allegiance, or the loop.

## DM Notes

- **Tier 3.** Her function is to surface the Judith thread to the party in the most natural possible way: an unrelated witness mentioning, in passing, something a party investigating the shrine should hear. She is not a clue-dispenser; she is a parishioner who happens to know.
- The party need not be investigating Judith specifically — any morning shrine visit that involves chatting with the old woman in the front pew can produce the mention.
- **Not a loop-sustaining prayer source.** Her prayer (thanks for the past, intercession for the dead) does not match the qualifying form for the cycle's anchor mechanic. She is safely a fixture. See `campaign/rules/loop_anchors.md` for the qualifying-prayer rule.
- Hati's cover line is the official explanation the party should hear *inside* the shrine. Outside the shrine — at the tavern, with Judith herself — the cover is harder to sustain; Judith has not been briefed on it and will deflect rather than confirm. A party that cross-checks will notice the asymmetry.
- Hati will, somewhat genuinely, protect Wenna if the shrine becomes dangerous. Wenna's presence is operationally useful, and Hati's residual decency has not entirely been extinguished.
- Elevate to a full profile only if she becomes a recurring scene partner the party adopts.
