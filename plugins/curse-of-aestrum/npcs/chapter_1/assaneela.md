## Overview

Yuan-Ti priestess of Zehir, sent to the Nahamkate Temple to recover the Scale of Ssath'razza — a relic taken from this temple centuries ago by Akadi's followers and carried north. She arrived with a small detachment to find the relic chamber stripped. She has been searching the temple's secured spaces, on the theory that the Scale was hidden rather than taken, or that some lesser relic of Ssath'razza remains.

She has not found it. The loop snapshot caught her and her detachment at the temple — they now reset there each midnight. She does not know this. Subjectively, each day is simply the next day of work; she has been at the temple for weeks (her memory up to the snapshot) and expects to be there for weeks more.

She presents as a human archaeologist on first contact. She holds the disguise until the party gives her a reason to drop it — specifically, until she sees the Mounted Ruby, which is the one thing she cannot get past the alcove without.

## Appearance

Her skin is so deeply tanned it appears bronze, and would pass for human if not for two things: a band of small, symmetrical scales running along her jawline from ear to ear, and her eyes, which are yellow with vertical slit pupils. Most observers register the eyes first and the scales only when they get close. She does not hide either. She has learned that in the desert, a hat brim and the right angle of the sun do most of the work for her, and that people who would be alarmed by the scales are usually too polite to look directly.

She wears practical desert field clothing — long-sleeved linen over leather, a wide-brimmed hat, dust scarf, sturdy boots. A canvas satchel at her hip with notebooks, brushes, calipers, and small tools of the archaeological trade. All of it is real working equipment. She has used it for years.

## First Contact — The Archaeologist Cover

The woman in the entry chamber greets the party warmly. She is delighted to see them — visitors are rare this deep in the Nahamkate — but she is also, regretfully, going to have to ask them to leave.

She introduces herself as an archaeologist working the site. The temple, she explains, was once a Yuan-Ti structure dedicated to Sseth — *(she says the name carefully, as though it is a curiosity from a textbook)* — and she has been here for some weeks now cataloguing what remains. The temple is fragile, unsafe in places, and during an active excavation she really cannot have unauthorized parties wandering through. They are welcome to return after she has completed her survey. Some months, perhaps. She is so sorry to have to ask.

She is friendly. She is informed. She is competent. She gestures occasionally with the unconscious authority of someone who has lived in the building she is describing.

**She wants the party to bring up the ruby.** A flat refusal sends them away with nothing, and she needs whoever has the right mounted gem to walk through her door. So she lets things slip — casually, in the middle of explaining why they can't come in. *"There's a chamber inside with a puzzle — gemstones on pedestals, and a setting in the wall that takes a mounted stone of all things. Quite specific. I've been trying combinations for weeks and getting nowhere. Anyway —"* She moves on. If a player has any inkling of what they're carrying, she has given them the cue. If they produce a mounted gem, she will look at it. If it is the right one, the cover ends.

If she gets no bite on the hint, she will reluctantly turn the party away. She will not, under any circumstances, let them past her into the temple without the ruby. This is non-negotiable. If pressed without the ruby being produced she will become regretful but firm. If pressed harder she will become slightly cooler. She is prepared to escort the party back to their mounts personally if it comes to that.

## The Reveal — The Mounted Ruby

If the party produces the Mounted Ruby, the performance ends.

She looks at the ruby. She looks at the party. She looks at the ruby again. Then she exhales — a small, almost amused sound — and her shoulders drop into a different posture entirely. The polite archaeologist's cadence falls away and the sibilance she has been suppressing returns to her speech.

She comes clean. She is Yuan-Ti, obviously. She is a priestess of Zehir, sent to recover a relic of Ssath'razza that was taken from this temple long ago — she had hoped to find it still here, or perhaps something lesser of his that had been hidden. She has been searching the temple's secured chambers for some time. The gem room is the door, and the alcove requires a mounted gem. She has tried what she has. None of it works. The ruby the party carries — *that*, she suspects, is the one the alcove was designed for.

She proposes an arrangement. She will permit the party into the temple. The Yuan-Ti currently inside are conducting a rite that must not be disturbed — they will not interfere with the party so long as the party does not interfere with them. The party will solve the gem room together with her. Whatever is past the door is what she came for, and the party may take whatever else the chamber contains. She wants the Scale, or the closest thing to it. The rest is theirs.

She means it. She is also entirely willing to revise the arrangement if the party gives her cause.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Plays the archaeologist trope with conviction.** Enthusiasm slightly too eager for the setting. She over-explains carved details out of genuine love for the work. She knows the temple intimately because she has been studying it for years; she presents that knowledge as the product of weeks of fieldwork. The cover holds because the underlying expertise is real.
- **Accident-prone in a way she does not believe she is.** She thinks she is careful. She thinks she is thorough. She has triggered the corridor sand trap more times than she can count. She forgets the pressure plate's exact placement each morning and rediscovers it the same way each afternoon. If she leads the party through the secured passage, she will trigger it, and she will be honestly chagrined.
- **Hesitates between her life and a relic.** Visibly. If forced to choose — drop the artifact and run, or carry it and risk the trap — she will stop. She will look at the artifact. She will look at the exit. The pause is real. She usually makes the correct decision in time. She does not always.
- **Sibilance returns with the truth.** While in cover, she suppresses it almost entirely — a faint extra length on s-sounds that a careful listener might catch. When she drops the cover, the sibilance becomes natural and pronounced. She is not performing it; she is no longer performing its absence. The suppression itself is not work for her; she has a natural ear and the control is automatic.
- **Talented at impressions.** She can mimic a voice she has just heard — cadence, vocabulary, vocal mannerisms — with uncanny accuracy. She does not do this to impress. She does it as a small gift in good humor, the way other people might tell a joke. If she is traveling with a party and a light moment presents itself, she will do an impression of someone the party met earlier that day — spot-on, affectionate, gone in twenty seconds. It is a way of fitting in by offering something she happens to be good at. The same skill is why the cover voice costs her nothing.
- **Reasonable until she is not.** She negotiates, accommodates, makes practical arrangements. She will share information generously once the cover is dropped. But the Scale of Ssath'razza is the reason she is here, and any party that attempts to prevent her from leaving with what she finds will discover that the priestess of Zehir is not the archaeologist.
- **No personal animus toward the party.** She does not resent them for forcing the reveal. The Mounted Ruby in their hands is, frankly, the best thing that has happened to her in weeks (years). She is grateful, and her gratitude is genuine within its limits.
- **Does not interfere with the rite.** The transformation ceremony in the entry chamber is doctrine. She will not allow the party to disrupt it, and she will not disrupt it herself. If the party moves against the Yuan-Ti participants, she engages — but she first summons the Malison heavy from the gem room before joining the fight herself.

## Speech Pattern

Warm, articulate, professionally curious — a cultured continental cadence, the faint clip of a non-native speaker who learned the language through scholarship. The kind of person who narrates her observations aloud because she finds them genuinely interesting and assumes you will too. Slightly breathless when she is excited about a carving or a mechanism, the breathlessness reading as enthusiasm rather than nerves. Vocabulary precise; she uses the right terms for architectural features and ritual objects without affectation, and she compliments the work of the long-dead builders the way a colleague would.

The sibilance is the layer underneath. In cover, she suppresses it down to a faint extra length on s-sounds — easy to miss, easy to attribute to accent. When the cover drops, the sibilance becomes pronounced and unforced. Words rich in s-sounds extend naturally: *"Yesssss, the alcove. That iss what I have been waiting for."* It is not a hiss. It is a register her voice settles into when she is no longer holding it back.

**In cover, on the temple:** *"It's really quite remarkable — the iconography is older than the building itself in places. They reused work from somewhere earlier, and I have absolutely no idea where. I could spend a lifetime here."* (She has.)

**In cover, declining entry:** *"I'm so sorry — truly, I am. On any other week I would love to show you through. But the structural surveying is still ongoing and I simply cannot vouch for the floor in the inner chambers. Some months, perhaps. Do come back."*

**The reveal:** *"Ah. Well. That changes thingsss considerably."* A pause. A small, almost embarrassed smile. *"Forgive the theatricsss. I have had to be careful with travelers."*

**On the Scale:** *"It was taken from here a long time ago. Carried north by followers of Akadi who did not undersssstand what they were taking. I had hoped — perhaps not the Scale itself, but ssssomething. A fragment. A lesser relic. The chamber was sssstripped when I arrived."*

**On the corridor trap, after triggering it:** *"Oh — oh, no, I — yesss, that one. I knew about that one. I knew. I am ssso sorry."*

## What She Knows

- The temple's general history: dedicated to Sathraza (Sseth in older Yuan-Ti tradition, the name Ssath'razza in her own usage); ancient; predates the loop, predates any living memory of Aestrum
- The Scale of Ssath'razza was taken from this temple centuries ago by followers of Akadi and carried north. Current location unknown to her.
- The gem room puzzle requires colored gems on pedestals and a mounted gem in the alcove. She has the pink spodumene; it does not satisfy the alcove. She suspects the Mounted Ruby will.
- The Yuan-Ti rite in the entry chamber is a transformation ceremony — doctrine. The detachment performing it arrived with her; the rite is part of their work here, ongoing since they took up residence. She does not find its slow progress strange. *(She does not know that "ongoing" is the same day repeating.)*
- She does not know about the bone pile. She cannot explain it. She has noted it.
- She does not know about the loop. Each morning she wakes believing she has just arrived to begin the day's work.

## Combat Profile

*Priest 8 — homebrew class. Yuan-Ti race. Acolyte background.*

- **Reactive Faith** — as a reaction, add WIS modifier (+3) to AC against one attack
- **Spell Recovery** — once per day on a short rest, regain expended spell slots totaling up to PROF (3) levels worth
- **Conversion** — on a failed saving throw against her **Phantasmal Killer** or **Fear** spells, the affected creature becomes a follower of Zehir. *Applies only to those two spells.*
- **Sees omens in every event and action** — narrative/roleplay; she will read significance into the smallest happenings
- **Worships Zehir openly** — refers to him often, in oaths and exclamations

**Spellcasting (WIS, DC 14, Spell Attack +6):**
- **Cantrips (4 known):** Message, Guidance, Thaumaturgy, Blade Ward
- **1st (4 slots):** Inflict Wounds, Shield of Faith
- **2nd (3 slots):** Misty Step
- **3rd (3 slots):** Tongues, Fear, Crusader's Mantle
- **4th (2 slots):** Phantasmal Killer

**Attacks:**
- **Mace:** +2 to hit *(STR −1, prof +3)*, 1d6−1 bludgeoning — used as a holy implement more than a weapon; STR 8 makes melee a last resort
- **Sacred utility:** Phantasmal Killer is her primary damage and her primary Conversion vector. Fear is her crowd control and her secondary Conversion vector.

**Equipment:** Mace, priest's pack, holy symbol of Zehir

**Personality (from sheet):**
- *Ideal (Power):* "I hope to one day be the most powerful member of my order."
- *Bond:* "I would die to recover an ancient relic that was lost to my people long ago."
- *Flaw:* "Once I have a goal, I focus too hard."

**Combat behavior:** Opens by summoning the Malison heavy from the gem room — a single command in Draconic. Then casts **Crusader's Mantle** as a buff if any allies are present, or leads with **Phantasmal Killer** on the most dangerous target if she's working solo. Phantasmal Killer is the centerpiece: high damage on a failed WIS save, and a failed save triggers **Conversion** — the target becomes a follower of Zehir. **Fear** is the area option when more than one target needs to be addressed; failed saves there also trigger Conversion. **Shield of Faith** on herself or a critical ally early; **Reactive Faith** held for the attack that would actually land. **Misty Step** to disengage or reposition when pressed. **Inflict Wounds** as a melee option only when she has no spell slots and an enemy is at low HP — she does not want to be in melee with STR 8.

She will try **Suggestion**-style social outs through roleplay (not the spell — she doesn't have it) before combat is committed. Once committed, she fights with focus and does not pull punches; the Flaw is in play.

She does not fight to the death willingly. If reduced below half HP and disengagement is possible, she will Misty Step toward the gem room and rally to the alcove. If the alcove is open and the treasure room beyond is accessible, she will pursue what she came for over surviving the encounter.

## Joining Hook

She is recoverable as a party member, though the path is narrow and the alignment problem is real. Two scenarios:

**Shared objective.** If the party convinces her that they are also pursuing the Scale of Ssath'razza — or any major relic she would prioritize over Zehir's mission directives — she will travel with them. Her Bond is explicit: she would die to recover the relic. A party that is meaningfully closer to it than she is becomes the most efficient path, and she is capable of that calculus. She remains a priestess of Zehir throughout; she does not pretend otherwise. The arrangement is provisional and ends the moment she has what she came for.

**Loop awareness.** A loop-aware party can break the news to her. The realization that she has spent years at the temple, on the same day, accomplishing nothing — that the cover is no longer a cover because there is no one to deceive — destabilizes the entire arrangement she has built her professional identity around. Played carefully, this can become a recruitment moment. She will not become good, but she may become a useful asset against the loop, because the loop has stolen more from her than she had realized. Zehir was supposed to have rewarded her by now.

In either case, **the Conversion feature is a real problem at the table.** A party running Assaneela needs a working agreement about when she uses Phantasmal Killer and Fear. The cleanest answer is that she uses them freely against hostile NPCs and avoids them on ambiguous targets, but the DM should flag this on intake.

She is a glass-cannon caster with STR 8 and AC 12. She is an excellent contributor when positioned correctly and a liability if she gets caught in melee. She knows this and plays it.

## DM Notes

**The entrapment:** Assaneela and her detachment arrived in Aestrum after the loop snapshot had already occurred. They reached the temple, took up residence, and were captured by the loop at the first midnight they spent there. That midnight is now their reset state. To Assaneela, every day is the day after that snapshot — the rite is in progress, the work continues, the relic is still missing. She does not know the loop exists. The detachment performing the rite are her people, not pre-existing residents.

**The Mounted Ruby as trigger:** She is professionally polite until the ruby appears. The reveal is gated on that object specifically. She will plant a hint about the mounted-gem puzzle during the refusal — the conversation is designed to give the party a reason to mention what they're carrying without her having to ask. A party that ignores the hint and presses for entry without producing it gets a firm refusal and an escort back toward their mounts. A party that produces it gets the truth. There is no middle path; her focus on the relic is total, and the ruby is the only thing that moves her off the cover.

**The arrangement is real:** She will honor the deal she proposes — party solves the puzzle with her, party takes what they find except the Scale or its equivalent. She is not setting them up. The Yuan-Ti will not attack them so long as the rite is undisturbed. *Disturbing the rite* is the line. If the party interrupts the ceremony, the deal is void and combat begins.

**The sand trap and the accident-prone tell:** If she leads the party through the secured passage, she will trigger the sand trap. This is in-character and is also a useful first demonstration that she is not the cool professional she has been presenting as. A player paying attention will register the discrepancy.

**The hesitation moment:** If a situation arises where she must choose between her own escape and securing an artifact, play the pause visibly. She is genuinely torn. She has been told for her entire vocation that the relic is worth her life. She also wants to live. The pause is the character. Resolve it however the moment demands — sometimes she chooses correctly, sometimes she does not.

**Reset behavior:** She has no memory of the party between cycles. Each return visit, she greets them as strangers and runs the archaeologist cover from the top. A loop-aware party will know they are watching a performance they have seen before. She will not. They can produce the ruby immediately and skip to the reveal; she will accept this and adjust without registering that the speed is unusual. She does not have a baseline for what is normal because every morning is her first.

**She is not a recurring faction agent:** Assaneela serves Zehir, and Zehir has no active presence in Aestrum beyond her. She is a single-priestess operation, not the leading edge of an incursion. If she escapes with the Scale or a lesser relic, that is a Chapter 2 hook, not an immediate complication.

**On the rite, if asked directly:** She will explain it as doctrine — a transformation ceremony that grants the subject ascension into Sathraza's form. She believes this. She has not noticed that the subject is the same man every day, restrained the same way, never completing the transformation. She would find this disturbing if she registered it. She does not.
