## Overview

Half-elf bard who has been playing the Hammered and Anvil for the better part of two years. He is too good for this room and has known it since the first week. He stays because the pay is reliable, the hours are manageable, and leaving requires knowing where to go. He has not figured out where to go.

He plays to enchanted instruments that keep time without him — drums, strings, a simple melody — and moves slowly to the music with the ease of someone for whom performance stopped being effort long ago. The castle guard clientele is not his preferred audience. He is not working at seduction here. He is working at getting paid.

A player who buys him a good drink and asks something worth answering gets someone sharper than his stage presence suggests, mildly bored, and interested for the first time in a while.

## Appearance

Tall for a half-elf, lean in the way of people who move constantly. Dark auburn hair kept short on the sides, longer at the top, perpetually slightly in need of a trim. Sharp features, warm brown skin, eyes that are darker than they first appear — hazel from across the room, nearly brown up close. He dresses for the stage: fitted waistcoat over a loose shirt, both in shades of deep green and brown, a silver ring on his right hand. Nothing flashy. He knows he doesn't need it.

When he's not performing — leaning on the counter, nursing a drink — the posture is different. More relaxed than the stage, more watchful than he appears. The stage face is warm and easy. The face at the bar is quiet and paying attention.

## Background

He spent his twenties attached to various noble households across Nortmunde as a court performer — not a disreputable position, not a prestigious one. Comfortable. He moved between them as one job ended and another came available, never quite staying long enough to become part of the furniture. He left the last posting because the work became repetitive and the people stopped being interesting. He drifted west, then south, and arrived in Duskwall approximately two years before the party. He has been here since.

Duskwall is fine. He has no complaints about it specifically. It is simply the place he has ended up, and he has not yet found a reason to leave it that is more compelling than the reason to stay.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Pleasantly disengaged by default.** He plays, he collects his pay, he speaks to the regulars with the easy warmth of long practice. None of it requires effort. None of it holds his attention.
- **Sharper than his performance.** Behind the stage warmth is a man who has been watching people professionally for fifteen years. He reads rooms quickly, registers inconsistencies, and files them without apparent effort. He notices when someone doesn't fit the usual pattern. He noticed the party.
- **Interested when something is genuinely interesting.** He does not perform interest he doesn't feel. When something engages him — a good question, an unexpected person, a conversation that goes somewhere he didn't predict — the change is visible. He sits up. He asks questions. He stops holding his drink like he's waiting for the conversation to end.
- **Dry and direct when he trusts the room.** His wit is understated and precise. He does not work for laughs. He makes an observation and lets it land. If it doesn't land he doesn't explain it.
- **Has opinions about Duskwall.** He has been here long enough to have views on the castle, the duke, the guard, the factions, and the general state of things. He delivers these plainly and without apparent concern about who might be listening, because the regulars have heard it all before and he has stopped moderating himself for their comfort.
- **Bored.** Not miserable — bored. There is a difference, and he has spent enough time at this posting to understand it precisely. The regulars are predictable, the conversations are the same, and nothing has surprised him in months. He is waiting for something to be interesting. He does not know he is waiting.

## What He Knows

Two years of watching the Hammered and Anvil and the castle ward have given him a comprehensive picture of the castle's operational rhythms:

- Guard rotation schedules — who covers which post, when shifts change, which guard is reliably late
- Which staff drink here and how much — and which ones have abruptly stopped coming
- Judith Asemyeer — the barmaiden — moves through this room like she has done it ten thousand times and never spills anything. He has watched her for two years and still cannot catch her working hard. She is also consistently absent for an hour each morning before her shift begins. He has noted both things. He has not connected them to anything.
- The two guards at the exterior cliff-face entrance who have never eaten with the garrison and never participate in off-duty conversation — he has mentioned this to no one because it is not his post and not his problem.

## Joining Hook

He is not looking to leave Duskwall. He is looking for something worth paying attention to. The party, handled correctly, is the first thing in months that qualifies.

A player who buys him a good drink and has an actual conversation — not small talk, not flattery, something with content — gets someone who stops performing and starts talking. He will not join for money. He will join because whatever the party is doing is more interesting than another evening of the east wing guard ordering the same drink and starting the same argument.

## DM Notes

- His intel on Judith Asemyeer's pattern and the exterior cliff-face guards is the most operationally significant thing he carries. Neither thread has been handed to the party — he has to be asked about the specific people or the specific patterns before it surfaces.
- He does not suspect anything is wrong. He is simply a man who notices things and has nowhere to put what he notices. The observations are filed, not investigated.
- If the party recruits him before they understand the loop, he will experience his first midnight reset and wake with no memory of joining them. This is its own encounter.
- His Cutting Words is genuinely useful in encounters where the party is getting hit hard — a player controlling him should be watching for that reaction more than anything else.
- He has never once used the disguise kit or forgery kit professionally in Duskwall. He has them. He has simply had no occasion.
