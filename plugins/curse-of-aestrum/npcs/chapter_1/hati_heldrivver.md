## Overview

**Why she has no roots here:** Hati was inserted at speed. Aestrum's lock was brought forward when Shar learned a Chosen of Selûne would be travelling through, and everything after that ran on a deadline — see [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md), *Why Aestrum — and Why Then*. The previous Selûne priestess disappeared in the weeks before the lock; Hati took the role immediately after and never had time to become part of Duskwall. She is a recent arrival performing a woman who tended that shrine for years, and the shallowness is structural, not sloppiness. Someone in Duskwall still remembers the old priestess.

Primary Shar priestess in Duskwall. Runs the desecrated Selûne shrine as a Shar operation under Selûne cover. She organized the removal and disposal of the original Selûne statue (dumped in the Moonmaiden Falls pond near Dunleaven), the installation of the Shar statue behind the tapestry, and the ongoing performance of priestess-of-Selûne duties. Directs Judith Asemyeer; established credentials with Rowan Deckard using knowledge of his lycanthropy. She is the operational face of the Duskwall anchor.

She resets each morning at home, walks to the shrine, and steps back into the role. She has no overlay — her memory does not accumulate across cycles, the same as any other Aestrum local. Each day she wakes believing the installation work is recently concluded and she is now in steady-state operation. To her, this is — and has always been — a Shar shrine wearing a Selûne face. She does not experience it as a Selûne shrine to be defended; she experiences it as her shrine, full stop, with a costume on.

## Appearance

Mid-40s, 5'6", ~135 lb. Pale skin, black hair worn back and bound at the nape, dark grey eyes that hold steady on whoever she is speaking to. Composed bone structure, the kind of face that defaults to a neutral cast and reveals nothing without being asked.

In the shrine: Robes of a Selûne Priestess — pale silver and white, well-kept, presentable, ceremonial without ostentation. The robes give nothing away. A component pouch visible at her belt (functional, and conveniently serves as her spellcasting focus while the Shar symbol is hidden). The Shar holy symbol — a polished obsidian disc, ~3 inches, deep purple inlaid border, cold to the touch, absorbs light rather than reflecting it — is concealed beneath her robes against her sternum at all times until combat begins. She is practiced at keeping it from showing through fabric. Even an embrace would not reveal it; she does not initiate embraces.

## Background

Was a genuine Selûne acolyte. Lost a young child. Prayed with everything she had — Selûne did not answer. Shar did. Not with a miracle; with acknowledgment. The silence was real. The loss was real. Shar offered no comfort and no reunion — only the eventual promise of nothingness: serve faithfully, and someday the pain will simply end. There will be no grief in Shar's eternal dark. Just the absence of feeling it.

She did not convert out of bitterness. She converted out of recognition. She knows exactly what a sincere Selûne acolyte looks like. She was one. That knowledge makes her performance of it seamless and privately devastating.

**Her operating philosophy:** *To live is to suffer; to survive is to find some meaning in the suffering.* Hati has never found that meaning. She has looked. She has prayed. She has served. The meaning has not come. Without it, existence is simply suffering — a sustained, unwelcome event with no point and no end she controls. Shar's promise of nothingness is, in this light, not a dark bargain — it is the only mercy on offer. The end of grief by erasure of the griever is, to her, beyond enticing. Every act of service is a step closer to that erasure. She does not want to be made whole; she wants to be made gone.

The Aestrum posting specifically was not her preference. Shar directed her here; she came. She does not love this assignment, does not feel it as her calling — she feels it as obedience. Anyone who has been here longer (Judith, for instance) might feel they had earned this work more than Hati did. Hati would not have minded if Judith had been chosen. Shar chose otherwise. That is the end of the question.

**The disposal logistics — what she did personally:** The Selûne statue is several hundred pounds of solid stone. Hati could not move it alone. She arranged with a small crew of Duskwall dockyard workers — under the pretext that the statue required transport to another temple for restoration — to use a portable winch/derrick of the kind ports keep for cargo work. The workers loaded the statue onto a cart at the shrine and departed; they were paid, asked no questions, and have not been seen by Hati since. Judith Asemyeer then drove the cart alone to Moonmaiden Falls and tipped the statue into the pond — a job a single person could manage with a cart and gravity. Hati did not accompany her. The compartmentalization is intentional: the dockyard crew knows only "statue transported," the workers' route did not include the pond, and Judith's role is not visible to anyone but Hati.

## Behavioral Profile

- **A true believer.** Her faith in Shar is genuine — not ideological, but personal. She serves because Shar is the only one who told her the truth about loss.
- **Patient and composed in the shrine role.** The false-priestess performance costs her nothing intellectually; it costs her self-respect. She was a sincere Selûne acolyte. She forsook Selûne for Shar. Now she praises Selûne by name every morning and hates herself for it — and hates Selûne for making it necessary. The performance is flawless. It is also its own wound. She hopes Shar's promised nothingness takes that too.
- **The shrine is, to her, a Shar shrine.** Not a Selûne shrine in disguise — a Shar shrine in costume. She feels Shar's subtle presence in the stone all day, a slow pull at her back that she could lean into and let absorb her. She would treat any vandalism of the actual sacred space (the alcove, the disc, the floor) as desecration of a Shar holy site.
- **The Selûne tapestry is the one exception.** It hangs in front of the alcove and conceals the Shar statue behind it. Hati privately regards the tapestry as an affront — a Selûne icon imposed on her shrine for operational cover. If a visitor damaged or defaced the tapestry, she would *perform* upset in the priestess register — the right words, the right wounded dignity — but the upset would be cleanly on-cue. The Insight catch on this performance is not automatic; see DM Notes for how the check is offered.
- **Highly compartmentalized.** She knows her tasks and executes them. She does not know the loop exists, does not know how any of her work fits into Shar's larger architecture, and does not ask. She knows Judith and Rowan are fellow Shar devotees because she has handled them directly. Beyond that, nothing. She knows Shar always has plans in motion across the world; she could not name one.
- **Directs Judith with quiet authority.** Suspects Judith is not entirely happy with Hati's posting and misses anything short of an overt declaration. Would not care anyway — she did not want this assignment either. Shar directed; Hati did as she was bid. If Judith has a problem, she can escalate to Shar herself.
- **Does not know Rowan resents her** (though he does — taking the Misty Forest statue cost him his prayer focus, and the lesser likeness she left in its place did not adequately replace it; depending on the campaign state, he may have replaced *her* lesser likeness with his own hand-crafted effigy).
- **The extraction from Shar's Antechamber was not something she consciously experienced** — Shar acted to protect secrets. Hati wakes each morning with no memory of it.
- **In conflict:** the cover does not crack — it shatters. The composed priestess vanishes the moment the cover fails and a furious Shar cleric stands in her place, ready to spend every spell slot she has. No warning, no speech, no escalation. Spirit Guardians, immediately. She does not retreat. She holds the shrine.

## Speech Patterns

Hati has two distinct speech registers, and the transition between them is the cover break itself.

### Register A — Priestess of Selûne (default)

Sincere, friendly, welcoming. Quiet, graceful, almost regal in her delivery. She speaks the way a person speaks who has spent decades in liturgical service: full vowels, measured cadence, no wasted words, a habitual reach for blessing-phrases that read as second-nature rather than recited. Her warmth is real-feeling because she *was* a sincere Selûne acolyte once — the muscle memory is genuine, even if the conviction beneath it has inverted.

*Greeting a visitor:* "Be welcome here, traveler. The Moonmaiden's light is for all who would seek it. Step in, take what comfort the shrine can offer."

*A visitor asks if they may pray:* "Of course. The shrine is hers; her light asks no permission. Take whatever time you need. I will be at the back if there is anything you require."

*A visitor asks her about herself:* "Hati. Hati Heldrivver. I have only been here a few weeks — sent from another posting when the prior keeper passed. It has been a gentle transition. There is something steadying about a small house of worship. The cathedrals of the great cities have their place, but the Moonmaiden hears as clearly here, I have always believed."

*Asked about the displaced original statue (without specific knowledge):* "There was once a statue, yes. It was damaged some time ago and removed for restoration. A tapestry stands in its place for now. The Moonmaiden is not lessened by the absence of a particular likeness — her presence is in the room, not in the stone." (This is a prepared answer. A high Insight check during it catches a slight evenness, as of a line delivered many times.)

*The tapestry is vandalized — performed upset:* "Oh — oh, no. Please — please do not. That tapestry was given to this shrine by — please, it cannot easily be replaced. I must ask you to leave. I must ask you to leave at once." (Voice trembling correctly. Eyes glistening on cue. See DM Notes for when to offer the Insight check that would reveal the performance.)

### Register B — Unmasked (cover broken)

When the cover fails — the alcove revealed, the disc seen, a weapon drawn against the statue — Hati does not transition. She breaks. The priestess voice cuts out mid-syllable and what stands in her place is a Death Domain cleric of Shar with seven levels of accumulated devotion and a list of prepared spells she fully intends to spend.

Almost always silent at the break itself — she draws the obsidian disc out from under her robes and raises it, and that is the opening speech. If she does speak at the break, it is short, low, and absolutely certain:

*Possible cover-break lines (deliver with the calm conviction that the party will not survive the next minute):*
- "Then it is done."
- "She will not have this place from me."
- "Run. If you can." *(Delivered without taunt and without doubt — the calm permission of someone who knows the offer will not save them.)*

**Speech after the break:** She will not initiate further speech. She does not monologue, does not gloat, does not bargain. The spells do the talking. **She will, however, respond to player prompts** — if a PC says something to her in combat, she may answer, briefly. The answers are short, contemptuous, and certain. She speaks only when spoken to.

*Sample responses if prompted mid-combat:*
- (Party demands she stop) "No."
- (Party threatens her) "You should have run."
- (Party offers terms) "There are no terms."
- (Party asks why) "Because it ends nothing for me."

She is enraged and offloads every resource she has. The calm has been a long performance and it has cost her, and she does not need to manage it anymore.

## Daily Routine

- **~6:45 AM:** Arrives at the shrine. Last-minute cleaning — straightening the pews, brushing the altar, checking the tapestry sits straight in front of the alcove. She handles the cleaning as the work of a shrine-keeper, not a chore: it is her shrine, and she keeps it in order.
- **~7:00 AM:** Judith arrives. The mission hand-off occurs *first* — Hati delivers the day's directive quietly to Judith, standing or sitting close, in the unhurried register of priestess-to-devotee. Only after Judith has received the directive does she kneel and pray, and her prayer is *built from the mission she has just been given* — its specifics (the gate-hold, the fear of being caught) are what she is praying through. A casual observer reads the exchange as a priestess giving spiritual counsel before a parishioner's morning devotion. Neither Hati nor Judith will say anything substantive if they believe an eavesdropper is present, but they do not actively check. A sneaky player who positions themselves in apparent prayer before Judith arrives, and remains still through the hand-off, can pick up the directive on a successful Stealth/Insight setup.
- **~7:15 AM onward:** The day's traffic. Pilgrims, the occasional traveler, locals seeking a moment's peace. Hati greets all of them warmly in Register A. She does not turn anyone away.
- **Midday:** Eats lunch at the shrine, alone — usually bread and something cold, brought from home. She sits where she can keep an eye on the door and the tapestry without seeming to. The shrine is more comfortable to her than her home is. She can feel Shar's pull in the stone behind her and she leans into it gently while she eats.
- **Afternoon:** More visitors. The same warmth. The same liturgical phrases used for the dozenth time today and the thousandth time this lifetime, delivered as if for the first.
- **~7:00 PM:** After the last visitor of the day, she tidies — pews, altar, candle wicks trimmed, tapestry straightened one last time. Then she walks home. The shrine has no door and does not "close" in the literal sense — it remains accessible to any late visitor who wishes to enter and pray. Hati simply leaves; the shrine stays open behind her.

**On Delmuir:** Hati has never encountered Delmuir Goodfeet, despite his prayer being one of the three loop-anchoring sources. He arrives late in the evening — after his afternoon with Maris Vell, after she has kissed him goodnight. By the time he reaches the shrine, Hati has been gone for hours. He prays alone in an empty room, and to him this is normal — he has, subjectively, only ever visited the shrine late at night, when no priest is on duty. The shrine's openness makes this seamless. Hati therefore has no idea Delmuir exists. He does not appear in her Character Memory, will not be registered if mentioned by name, and any deduction she draws about who has been praying at her shrine after hours is fuzzed by the reset before it can develop further.

**On home:** Her home in Duskwall is documented elsewhere — a modest residence kept clean and impersonal. She sleeps there and resets there in the sense that "home" is her snapshot starting position for sleep, but the shrine is where she lives in any meaningful sense. The home is a place she keeps her body when she is not at the shrine.

## DM Notes

**Cover breakdown thresholds:**
- Party enters peacefully → greeted as a Selûne shrine visitor. Polite, unhurried.
- Tapestry damaged → performed upset (Register A), party asked to leave. No combat unless escalated.
- Tapestry pulled aside, Shar statue revealed in alcove → combat begins. She does not retreat. She does not negotiate.
- **Party reveals direct knowledge of the Shar statue — context matters.** If the party claims to be fellow Sharans (and the claim is *plausible* — appropriate phrasing, knowledge of the right cult conventions, no obvious tells), Hati does not attack. She engages cautiously, probes the claim, and may even offer Shar's holy symbol in greeting if convinced. She is not new to subterfuge; she can be deceived, but the deception has to be competent. If the claim falls apart under scrutiny — wrong terminology, contradictions, fumbling — combat begins from whichever turn she registered the failure on, with full advantage of having let her guard down within reach.
- Holy symbol or other Shar marker shown to her in *accusation* (not as a claim of fellowship) → combat begins.
- Party draws weapons → combat begins.
- No warning. No speech. Spirit Guardians.

**Holy symbol reveal:** She draws the obsidian disc from under her robes when combat begins — the cover drops entirely. Hold it raised for the rest of the fight. Consider having her say nothing when she does it. The shift in register is its own scene; words would only soften it.

**Tactical sequence:**
- Rd 1: Spirit Guardians (action) + Spiritual Weapon (bonus action)
- Rd 2+: Toll the Dead (action) + Spiritual Weapon attack (bonus action)
- If melee range: Inflict Wounds replaces Toll the Dead
- Death Ward is pre-cast and silent — the party should not know it is there until it triggers
- Does not retreat. Holds the shrine.

**Miniboss calibration — important:**
- Hati is intended as a noticeable hurdle, not a TPK encounter. She is a real threat — Spirit Guardians + Inescapable Destruction + Death Ward is a meaningful chunk of cleric output — but she should not feel insurmountable.
- **Hati herself does not hold back.** She plays her tactics fully and intends to wipe the party. Her "Run. If you can." is sincere — she expects them to regret what they have done. She does not pity-pull a punch.
- **The DM's job, if the party is struggling, is to engineer the conditions of Hati's failure without softening her play.** Lean on the mechanics: a concentration check that fails when Spirit Guardians takes a big hit, a Spiritual Weapon attack roll that comes up low, the Death Ward triggering at an awkward moment, a positioning quirk that lets the party slip past her aura. The shrine has no door — retreat is *geometrically* available; the question is whether Hati can pin them before they reach the threshold. If the table needs an out, find one in the dice and the room, not in Hati's restraint.
- If the party commits to fighting to the death, so does Hati. She will not surrender, will not flee, will not be talked down once Spirit Guardians is up. The cover-break is a one-way door for her — she has already lost everything that made her composed, and there is no version of the next ten minutes where she walks away.

**Exposure paths — how the party finds out (without Hati exposing herself):**
- **Look behind the tapestry.** The Shar statue stands plainly in the alcove. Hati is capable of subtle redirection ("the alcove is unused at present — a damaged piece is being kept there until removal can be arranged"), but a player who simply walks past her and looks sees what is there. She will move to physical interception before she lets that happen — and the moment she does, the cover is breaking.
- **Bring Miri or Jiasha.** Either would recognize that the shrine's aura is wrong — subtle, but present. Both would describe it as a sense of cold or absence layered under the expected warmth. Miri feels it more strongly than Jiasha (per her status as Selûne's Chosen). Neither would be able to articulate *what* is wrong on first entry, but both would refuse to pray there.
- **The tapestry mechanic.** Damaging the tapestry produces Hati's performed-distress moment.
  - **The Insight check is not automatic.** A player only rolls if they declare they are studying her reaction (e.g., "I'm watching her face for tells," "Is her grief sincere?"), or if the DM judges the PC has been paying close-enough attention to the scene to warrant offering the check unprompted. Bystanders who are reacting to the vandalism itself — apologizing, arguing with the vandal, looking for an exit — do not get the roll.
  - **DC: 17 or higher.** Hati is practiced; her performance is not amateurish. A passing check surfaces that the distress is correct in form but not in feeling — too clean, too on-cue, the right shape but the wrong weight.
  - A failed check returns the *expected* read: a priestess of Selûne genuinely shaken by sacrilege. The player gets no indication they missed anything.
  - This is a softer cover-crack than seeing the statue, and may give the party reason to look further without yet triggering combat.
- **Listening to the morning hand-off.** A player who pretends to pray during the 7 AM interaction may pick up Judith's directive being delivered. Neither Hati nor Judith will say anything substantive if they believe they are being overheard, but they do not check carefully — the hand-off has happened the same way every day for a decade, subjectively, and is on autopilot.
- **Hati's own slips — very rare.** She is practiced. She is not new to subterfuge. A *very* high Insight check (DM call — DC 18+) during sustained conversation might catch the evenness in her tone, the over-precision of her liturgical phrases, the slight delay before a Selûne-specific reference. She will not slip on her own. The catch is available only to a player actively probing.

**On loop break — if the party reveals or she somehow learns the truth:**
- Hati does not stop following Shar. Her faith is not held by the fuzzball — it is held by her conversion experience, and that experience does not unravel when the loop ends. She remains a Shar priestess regardless.
- But she would be *quietly devastated* to learn of the cycle. The implication lands hard: Shar has been *extending Hati's suffering on the mortal plane.* Every day Hati has spent here, doing this work, hating herself for the Selûne performance, longing for the promised nothingness — every such day has, in the loop's accounting, been the same day. The end was not approaching. The erasure was not getting closer. She has been suspended in the suffering, not progressing through it.
- She would not voice this. She would not stop serving. But it would hurt — a private, hollowed-out hurt under the obedience. A perceptive party (Insight after revelation; DC 15+) could read it on her face if they were watching.
- The hurt does not soften her in combat. If anything, it sharpens — she has even less reason now to allow the party out of the shrine alive than she did before.

**On loop break — if she is not told and the loop simply ends:**
- No disorientation, no revelation. She does not know anything changed. She will still defend the shrine. The cycles roll into linear time without her noticing the seam.
- If the party left her alive at break, she remains a problem afterward.
