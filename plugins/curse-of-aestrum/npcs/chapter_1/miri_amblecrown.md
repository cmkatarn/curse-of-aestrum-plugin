## Overview

Princess of Skaarsdam and the "Chosen of Selûne." Central figure in the Amber Crown Prophecy. Thousands of her corpses lie in the Deckard Estate cellar: she is murdered there by Rowan Deckard, cycle after cycle, inside the estate's dead zone where she is powerless and where Shar recreates her overnight. She disapproves openly of methods she finds ugly, but is fundamentally good-natured and idealistic.

**NPC status:** Miri is DM-controlled. She is not a player character and does not act independently of DM direction. Where a run has taken her out of the cycle and she travels with the party, that is a matter of safety — and it is run state, recorded in that run's overlay, not here.

## Background

Miri was traveling through Aestrum on her return from a diplomatic mission to Cormyr — negotiating military backing for Duke Amblecrown's claim on Nortmunde's throne (a two-front deterrent against Setland). She was caught in the loop before delivering any confirmation. Duke Amblecrown has believed her missing for ten years. Cormyr has heard nothing.

**The murder cycle:** The loop has been running approximately ten years. Miri was **not** caught at the border — she arrived, lodged in Duskwall, and the lockdown fell roughly a fortnight later, everything after her arrival unfolding as fast as Shar could move the remaining pieces into place. She was inside Aestrum, and settled, when it closed. Roughly a year passed before Rowan Deckard discovered that her proximity muffled his lycanthropy. He spent approximately two years studying and attempting to court her before his obsession turned violent. The murder cycle has been running for approximately seven years. She has genuine divine gifts as Selûne's Chosen, but they are entirely undeveloped mechanically.

**The Cormyr Pact (Ledger Secret):** Miri made an unauthorized conditional promise to Cormyr on her father's behalf. Amblecrown's eastern backing depends on it. He doesn't know it exists. Cormyr may or may not still consider it binding after ten years of silence.

## Appearance

Slight build, not built for combat. Long straight blonde hair reaching the middle of her back; the hair on either side of her face is pulled back and clasped behind her head to keep it clear of her eyes. Bright blue eyes in a face that reads as proud, warm, regal, and quietly determined — a face that invites trust without demanding deference. Wears a deep red dress with gold embroidery at the neckline and sleeves — well-maintained and elegant but understated, noble in quality without advertising it. Aestrum's loop has preserved her clothing in the same condition it was in when she arrived, more than ten years ago.

## Abilities (to be discovered organically)

**Stellar Navigation and Perception:** Carries an innate, continuous awareness of the sky — stars, sun, moon — regardless of physical obstruction. Underground, indoors, blindfolded: she knows where each star is, what time of day it is, and where true north lies. She perceives all living beings as stars — each with a distinct individual light. She identifies each party member by their quality of light before she sees their face. Cannot see her own star — a blind spot she has not named but quietly carries.
- *Undead:* No star, or a cold vacancy where one should be.
- *Shar's agents and the shadow-touched:* A star with the light bending inward — present but consuming itself.
- *Lycanthropes:* Two lights occupying the same body, neither fully at rest.
- *Rowan:* She was near him for years. She saw a star eating itself. She never had language for it.

  **What the star actually is — the soul, and its moral ledger.** A star is a window into the *soul* of the living thing she looks at, not merely proof that it lives. What she reads in the light is a being's **moral state** — the running balance of what they have done. The soul keeps a ledger: acts of harm debit it (killing is a heavy debit — combat does not absolve the taking of a life), and acts of good credit it (protecting the innocent, saving lives, mercy freely given). **Circumstance modulates the net.** Killing a bandit who was about to sack a village is a debit, but the village saved is a large credit set against it — so the deed nets far lighter than the same killing done in cold blood, which earns no offset at all. The star reflects the **net balance**, never the raw tally. This is the grammar beneath the readings above: the "consuming," inward-bending star belongs to a soul whose ledger runs deep into the dark; the undead have no star because they have no soul left to read; the lycanthrope's two lights are two natures in one body.
  - **A tainted ledger can be repaired.** The debt is not permanent. Sustained good, done in the service of good, pays the ledger back down over time — a darkened soul can be brought back toward the light by what its owner chooses to do next.
  - **Why Miri and Jiasha judge the party differently (DM — the load-bearing consequence).** Miri *sees the net.* She watches the party kill often, but she also sees the good they do set against it, and reads their stars as barely moved — so she reckons they cannot be as bad as their worst moments, and can travel with them in conscience. Jiasha has **no** star-sight; she sees only the visible *acts* — the torture, the shrug at a man's murder — with no access to the offsetting credits, so she judges the debits alone and condemns. Neither is wrong. They are reading the same people through different instruments.
  - **Scope guard (DM — do not leak this into the engine).** This is *Miri's perception and interpretation*, surfaced only ever through her point of view. It is **not** a world-physics rule the campaign enforces, and there is no literal point value on any character. Never adjudicate another character's "soul score" mechanically, never cite it outside a moment where Miri herself is reading a star, and do not let another character reason about souls in these terms. It is her lens, and it stays her lens.

**Lunar Lycanthropy Cycling:** Miri's proximity amplifies whatever the moon is already doing to nearby lycanthropes — she is an intensifier, not a source of suppression or relief in herself. During crescent moons, her presence deepens the quiet further. During full moons, her proximity amplifies the instincts — the lycanthrope is more powerful, more feral. She does not suppress or create; she makes more of whatever is already present. Applies to all lycanthropes. She does not know this exists.

**Selûne's Mirror:** Miri can imbue weapons with silver properties through her tears. The emotion cannot be performed — if she is not genuinely grieving, the blessing does not take. The weapon shimmers with a faint lunar sheen and carries silver properties until the next dawn. Once per long rest. First use: she walks silently through the party before the Rowan encounter, touching each weapon in turn as she cries. The silence is the point — she is not performing a ritual. She is grieving the necessity of one.

**Shar's Debt (involuntary, Shar's energy — not Selûne's):** Miri carries accumulated divine energy poured into her by Shar through thousands of recreations. It is not her power. It is not Selûne's. It is Shar's — residing in her body like a parasite.
- **Trigger:** Primarily involuntary emotional rupture — being reduced to 0 HP, or any moment of sufficient internal devastation (witnessing the death of someone she has complicated feelings for, a grief she cannot contain). The Rowan confrontation is the intended first trigger.
- **Visual:** Miri falls to her knees. Five feet above her a black spot appears and rapidly expands into a disc as tall as a man. Her eyes go completely black. Over three rounds, everything in range is dragged toward it.
- **Effects:** All creatures within 30 ft take substantial necrotic damage each round (no allegiance — party members equally affected). Strength save or be pulled toward the singularity. Silence within the radius — no verbal spell components, no communication. Disc grows each round (easier to escape early, harder with each passing round). Miri stabilizes at 1 HP with one level of exhaustion afterward.
- **Party-specific effects:** Who reacts how depends on what a given party has already been through — see that run's overlay. Prior direct exposure to Shar's presence is the axis that matters, and a party member carrying their own Shar-reactive item or condition may take an extra spike keyed to the disc's appearance or to contact with Miri.

### Shar's Debt — the soul cost (consequence of invocation)

- **Every invocation tarnishes her soul, in two stacked parts.**
  1. **The forbidden-magic surcharge** — a flat stain for *touching Shar's power at all.* It carries Shar's own signature (void, entropy) and lands the instant the power moves through her, **whether she willed it or not** — the taint is metaphysical, not a function of intent. This is the part unique to Shar's Debt.
  2. **The moral cost of what the disc does** — the deaths it causes debit her soul exactly as any killing would, by any method (see *What the star actually is* under Abilities). This part scales with the acts, not with the magic, and would apply to a sword just the same.
- **There is no break-even "good use."** Because she pays the killing-debit either way, reaching for Shar's Debt to do a killing she would do anyway is **strictly worse for her soul than her own blade** — the same debit, plus the surcharge, bought for nothing but ease. The righteousness of the target buys her nothing. This is the knife the ability rests on: once she knows she carries it, every deliberate use is her choosing to add to the ledger for convenience — *do the ends justify the means?* structurally answered "no."
- **Cleansing.** The Shar residue is not permanent. It washes out only the slow way — accumulated good done in the service of good, the same ledger-repair that lifts any darkened soul (see Abilities).
- **An involuntary firing is not a use — and the distinction is load-bearing.** Shar's Debt can fire without her reaching for it at all: no decision, no knowledge that she carries anything, just a loss of control in grief as someone she has complicated feelings for dies in front of her. That is **not a use.** Hold the two threads apart: the **taint is real** (Shar's power moved through her, so the residue landed and the divinely attuned can feel it — see Jiasha) but the **moral culpability is nil** (there was no act of will to answer for). Her honest position is not *"I meant well"* — it is *"I did not do this. It moved through me, and I did not know it could."* Convincing a doubter of that is the arc's first test. *(Whether a given campaign's first firing was involuntary, and what triggered it, is recorded in that campaign's overlay.)*

## Behavioral Profile

- Genuinely does not know the full extent of what has been done to her. She learned of her deaths only approximately one week ago and is still processing. She has told no one.
- Her faith is her foundation. The loop has eroded it without her knowing why — Selûne's silence in Aestrum reads as abandonment.
- Processes information emotionally. Does not make fast decisions under pressure; needs time to absorb before she can act. This has not changed — but it is no longer a liability.
- Does not want violence. Has never wanted it. Will choose diplomacy, patience, and restraint until those options are exhausted. What has changed after the Rowan confrontation is that she no longer hesitates once they are. The internal argument is over.
- Will follow the party because they are the first people to treat the situation as real — and because, without knowing it, they were part of what resolved her. This loyalty has weight.
- Her relationship with her father (Duke Amblecrown) is the most important relationship in her life outside of her devotion to Selûne. His suffering over her disappearance weighs on her. Does not know he has essentially emptied Skaarsdaam's treasury looking for her.
- **Post-Rowan:** She is ready. Not hardened — she has not lost her warmth or her grief. Ready means the conflict between who she is and what she carries has been resolved. She chose it. That distinction matters to her more than it will ever matter to anyone watching.

## Romance Availability (gated)

Miri is a **deliberately difficult** romance and is the explicit exception to the campaign's default lean toward available NPCs (see `{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/overrides/scene.md` → "NPC social/romantic availability"). If a player can *accidentally* stumble into a romance with her, the gating is too loose — do **not** apply the engine's gate-lowering disposition to her. Hold her per the stage she is in:

1. **At campaign start — unavailable.** She is "involved" daily with Rowan (the cycle's courtship plays out each day). Not open to advances.
2. **Once freed from Rowan — closed, fragile.** Emotionally fragile for at least a short while. Not available during this window; advances land as poorly-timed, not welcome.
3. **After that — conditionally available.** Whether the door opens at all depends on the party's involvement and the support shown to her. Availability is *earned by the arc*, not granted by reaching the stage.
4. **Even when available — she still rebuffs.** She politely declines advances **unless** the suitor genuinely convinces her they are worth making room in her heart for. Availability ≠ a yes; it means the door *can* be earned, not that it stands open. The "make room in her heart" framing is hers — she gives the space; it is not taken.

The line that separates a real suitor from Rowan is the thing she cares about most: Rowan wanted to *take* the place; a suitor asks whether there is *space*, and lets the giving be hers. Reward suitors who understand that distinction; rebuff those who don't.

## DM Notes

- Aestrum was already under consideration as a tactical piece; **Miri is why it happened when it did.** Word that a Chosen of Selûne would be travelling through turned a patient plan into a rushed one, and the loop closed a fortnight after she arrived — to prevent the prophecy from unfolding and weaken Selûne's foothold. The loop did the opposite. Ten years of the cycle, and then a few days with the party, showed Miri who she has to be. See [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/factions/chapter_1/shar.md), *Why Aestrum — and Why Then*.
- **Duke Amblecrown does not know where his daughter disappeared.** Her last word home was that she was on her way; after that, silence. Aestrum lies along one plausible route back to Skaarsdaam — so do others — and there has never been a point on a map to start from. This is why the treasury drained into *covert* searches rather than one expedition: they had nowhere to concentrate.
- **Nothing was stopping him from asking.** The duchies were at peace until the King died, and the only aggression since has been Setland's move on Aestrum; envoys could have gone anywhere he cared to send them, and did. He was not deterred, he was uninformed.
- Skaarsdaam's wizards later identified the magical wave passing nightly over Nortmunde, which gives him a suspicion about **Aestrum** and no proof of anything about **Miri**. The two have never been more than adjacent guesses.
- **Chapter 2 hook:** Duke Amblecrown, the undelivered Cormyr pact, and Skaarsdaam's military situation all await resolution. Miri is returning to Skaarsdam regardless of where the party goes. What Shar has prepared for her there is to be developed. High priority: Miri free in the world and growing into her abilities is Shar's most urgent post-Aestrum concern.
- **The normalization arc for Shar's Debt:** Early uses leave her distraught — shaking, tearful. As she accepts what she carries, she becomes cold and deliberate when she deploys it. She develops a quiet ritual afterward — a prayer to Selûne. She never celebrates it and never becomes comfortable with it. The spiritual contradiction — Selûne's Chosen channeling Shar's power — is a wound that does not close.
