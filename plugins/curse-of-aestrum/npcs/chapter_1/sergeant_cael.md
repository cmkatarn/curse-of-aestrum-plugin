## Overview

Senior castle guard, inner ward post, six years. He knows every face that belongs inside the curtain wall. He knows how people move when they're lost, when they're lying, when they belong and are just having a bad day, and when they do not belong at all. He has never explained his decisions to petitioners — experience taught him that explaining invites argument. He is politely immovable.

His instincts about unfamiliar faces are reliable. He has simply never encountered a situation that genuinely required them.

The Setland advance expedition has been killing and replacing the guards at the exterior cliff-face entrance every morning for ten years. Sergeant Cael's post is the inner ward — a different location. He has noticed the exterior guards seem to turn over unusually fast. He attributed it to the post being undesirable. He has not investigated. He is a man whose instincts work and who has never been given a reason to act on them.

## Appearance

Mid-forties, solidly built — not the lean fitness of a young soldier but the settled density of someone who has been physical his whole life and never stopped. Close-cut grey-brown hair, a face that has spent decades outdoors, dark eyes that move in the deliberate way of someone who learned to clock everything in a room before the room finished presenting itself. The castle guard uniform is maintained precisely. Not shined for appearance — maintained because equipment that isn't maintained fails.

He moves through the castle ward like he owns it, which after six years he does in every practical sense. Newer guards get out of his way without being asked. He has not noticed this happening.

## Background

He came up through the Duskwall city guard and transferred to the castle garrison eight years ago. He was promoted to sergeant two years after that. He has spent his career doing one thing: making sure the right people are in the right places. He is exceptionally good at this.

He has a wife and two children in the city. He is home three nights out of seven. The other four he sleeps in the garrison. His wife has an arrangement she finds satisfying; he has a post he finds satisfying. These are compatible.

He has never wanted anything beyond this. This is not resignation — it is genuine contentment. He is exactly where he is supposed to be.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Immovable without being unkind.** He does not need to be cold or threatening to stop someone. He simply doesn't move, and his face says he has all the time in the world and no particular interest in changing his answer. Most people find this more effective than aggression.
- **Does not explain.** Decisions are decisions. Explanations become negotiations. Negotiations are the guard's version of losing. He has learned this so thoroughly that he sometimes fails to explain things that actually warrant explanation.
- **Respects competence.** A player who handles the ward correctly — the right approach, the right documents, the right manner — gets through with minimal friction. A player who handles it incorrectly gets the immovable face. He is not interested in making things difficult; he is interested in doing his job.
- **Quietly sharp.** He notices things. He has always noticed things. He has simply filed most of them under "manageable" and moved on. A player who gets past his guard persona — who actually talks to him rather than past him — finds someone who has been paying attention for a long time and has a great deal to say about what he has noticed.
- **The activation moment.** If someone tells him what has been happening at the exterior cliff entrance — that his colleagues have been murdered and replaced by enemy soldiers every morning for a decade — he does not react immediately. He asks one clarifying question. Then another. Then he is very still for a moment. Then he has a mission, and he is someone who is very good at executing a mission.

## What He Has Noticed

Six years of watching the inner ward have given him a picture he has never assembled into a theory because no one has asked:

- The exterior cliff-face guards have an unusual turnover rate. They don't socialize with the garrison. He attributed this to the post being undesirable and isolated. He has not revisited this conclusion.
- Judith Asemyeer has been working the Hammered and Anvil for over a year and is consistently absent from the castle ward for approximately an hour each morning before her shift — not unusual, but her movement pattern when she returns is slightly wrong. He has never acted on this.
- Evandur's quarters corridor is off-limits to his men past the midpoint and has been since before Cael's posting. He has never been given a reason. He accepted the standing order. He has occasionally wondered.
- The maze entrance door at the end of the corridor is the quietest door in the castle — almost no sound when it opens or closes. He knows this because he checked once, years ago, and was never told why.

**Combat behavior:** Moves to the front, applies Goading Attack to the highest-threat enemy, uses Commander's Strike when an ally has a better angle. Does not retreat unless ordered. Rally goes to whoever is about to go down. His combat instincts are the same as his gate instincts — assess, position, hold.

## Joining Hook

He is not looking for an adventure. He is looking for a problem within his jurisdiction that has gone unaddressed. The exterior cliff-face guards are within his jurisdiction. The moment a player presents him with credible evidence of what has been happening there — not speculation, credible evidence — he is in. He does not need to be convinced. He needs to be shown.

His conditions for joining are simple: he wants to deal with the cliff entrance. After that, he will assess whether the party's larger problem is also his problem. It usually will be.

## DM Notes

- His four observations (exterior guards, Judith, the corridor, the maze door) are each individually actionable intelligence. He will share them if asked directly about any of the four topics — they are simply not things he has connected before now.
- His reset is the same complication as any other non-loop-aware NPC: he wakes each morning as a guard beginning his shift, with no memory of having joined the party. Managing him through the dead zone or keeping him informed is the party's responsibility.
- Commander's Strike is his highest-value maneuver for a player-controlled NPC — it rewards a player who is paying attention to the whole battlefield rather than just Cael's turn.
- He will not take orders from a party member he does not respect. Respect is earned quickly — one competent action is usually enough — but it must be earned. He is not hostile to the party; he simply does not defer to people who have not demonstrated they know what they're doing.
- The wife and children are real and in the city. If the party ends up involving him in something that might not end well, he will take one evening to go home. He will not explain why. He will be back.
