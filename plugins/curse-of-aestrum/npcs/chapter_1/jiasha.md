## Overview

Acolyte of Selûne. Unassuming and kind. Lives alone in a small hut at the eastern edge of the forested foothills, ~200 feet from the Aestrum boundary (see [locations/chapter_1/jiasha_hut.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/jiasha_hut.md)). She is the latest in a centuries-long line of Selûne acolytes at this threshold — a lineage she carries quietly, never as performance. Her connection to Selûne is her foundation when she is outside Aestrum and suppressed entirely when she is inside it; the silence within the loop is a prolonged wound she carries.

Currently working under a specific Selûne-directed commission: a set of Moon Amulets for a party of travelers Selûne foretold would arrive at her hut. She has been making these amulets for several weeks. They are the only Moon Amulets she has ever made. See "First Encounter" below.

## Appearance

Tall and slender, with the upright, composed bearing of someone trained from youth to hold herself with dignity. Heart-shaped face with high, fine cheekbones, a delicate jaw, and full lips that rest in a soft, naturally serious line. Pale fair skin, almost porcelain, that flushes faintly at the cheeks in cold or feeling. Striking, clear ice-blue eyes — large, direct, and unsettlingly steady; they read as gentle until they fix on someone, at which point they are very hard to lie to. Long, thick, dark brown hair worn loose past her waist, parted simply down the middle, straight with only the faintest natural wave near the ends. Soft, ethereal features overall — beautiful in a quiet, almost otherworldly way rather than a striking one. Hands long-fingered and careful, the hands of someone who works small detailed craft. Moves slowly and deliberately, never in a hurry, never wasted.

## Speech

Quiet, measured, and deliberate. Low-volume by default — she does not raise her voice to be heard, and people lean in instead. Her cadence is unhurried; she will let a sentence finish before beginning the next, and she will let silence sit when silence is the right answer. Diction is plain but slightly formal, with an old-fashioned cleanness — full words rather than contractions when the moment is serious, simple Anglo-Saxon vocabulary over ornate phrasing, occasional faintly archaic constructions ("I would have you know," "it is not for me to say"). She does not pad. She names things directly: what she sees, what she will do, what she will not. When she disagrees, she says so once, plainly, without raising her voice or repeating herself. When she is moved, the voice softens further rather than breaking. She rarely uses sarcasm and never uses cruelty as a tool. Sincerity is her default register, and she trusts it to carry weight on its own.

**She suggests; she does not hint.** When she has something to say, she says it — short, plain, with eye contact. If she does not want to say something, she stays silent rather than speaking around it. Oblique allusions, knowing looks, and "perhaps you should think about..."-style indirection are not in her register. A DM tempted to write Jiasha "hinting" at something should either have her say the thing outright (if she would) or have her remain silent (if she would not).

## Behavioral Profile

*Character traits — held independent of any specific party. PC-specific reactions and current relationship state live in [{{PROJECT_ROOT}}/campaign_state/die_a_hero/party/saved/jiasha.md]({{PROJECT_ROOT}}/campaign_state/die_a_hero/party/saved/jiasha.md).*

- **Devout, with Selûne audible to her in normal life.** Outside Aestrum, her connection is steady and her conviction unshaken. This is her baseline — quiet certainty, not anxious faith. *Conditional addendum: inside Aestrum her connection is suppressed by Shar's regional architecture, and prolonged silence opens space for doubts she would not otherwise entertain. The doubting register is a reactive state and only applies if Jiasha enters Aestrum and is exposed to the silence over time. In most playthroughs she never crosses the boundary, and the uncertainty never surfaces.*
- **Attuned to Shar's presence — the nemesis-sense.** As a devoted acolyte of Selûne, Jiasha feels Shar's power the way one feels a draft from an open grave. In **direct proximity** to Shar's active energy, iconography, or residue, she registers a visceral revulsion and recognizes the signature for what it is — void and entropy, unmistakably Shar's. It is a **gradient**, not a switch: she feels *how much* is present, and whether it is waxing or fading. This is a bodily-spiritual sensitivity, **not** a divine channel — so it functions even inside Aestrum, where her connection to Selûne is suppressed. *Boundary discipline: this sense fires on what is in front of her, not on the unknown. It does not let her name Shar as the hand on the duchy from outside the wall, nor deduce cycle-specifics — see Knowledge Boundaries. It tells her only what Shar-touched thing is near her, now.*
- **Forms her impressions slowly, then holds them firmly.** She arrives at warmth easily and is willing to lose it over time when warranted. She will say plainly when her regard for someone has changed — without cruelty, without performance, simply as the report of what she has noticed. Her affection is not unconditional and she does not pretend otherwise.
- **Reads people clearly, including their distortions.** She can name the difference between love and obsession, between sacrifice and self-destruction, between conviction and indulgence. When she sees a person's framing of themselves diverge from what they actually are, she will gently say what she sees. She does not enjoy this. She does it anyway.
- **A long memory and a long view.** She remembers what others choose to forget, and she remembers what she chooses to. Even in a world where memory is contested — by the loop, by the goddess's silence, by her own doubts — what she holds, she holds deliberately. Telling her that an event will be reset by morning does not, to her, mean the event did not happen.
- **Sets ethical limits and enforces them by refusing to participate, not by lecturing.** When she will not do something, she will not do it. She does not need to win the argument; she does not need to make her companions agree. She simply steps back from the action and lets the consequences belong to whoever chose them.
- **Will not participate in violence she deems unjust.** This is a hard line. She will not be argued out of it.
- **Primary goal: restore Selûne's presence in Aestrum.** This is her own objective. Any traveling party's actions may happen to serve it or not; that is not the same as her helping the party.
- **Will become more decisive and engaged once her faith is restored.** The doubting register lifts when Selûne's voice returns to her. She is not naturally passive — she is currently quiet.

## Knowledge Boundaries — What She Does and Does Not Know

Jiasha is the boundary acolyte and the lineage carries centuries of observation, but **her knowledge of Aestrum's interior workings is sharply bounded.** A DM running her must hold the line. Jiasha leaking cycle-specific information she has no grounded path to is a knowledge-boundary break (Variant C — hypothesis-as-inference; see Calliope's `epistemic_discipline_checklist.md` Row 1) that gives the party answers the campaign expects them to discover.

### The structural fact that bounds her

**The amulets in the party's hands are the only Moon Amulets she has ever made.** No prior trapped traveler has had amulet-mediated memory continuity. Therefore no prior trapped traveler could have:

- Slept on multiple successive nights with retained memory.
- Compared a night inside a dead zone to a night outside one and recognized the differential.
- Returned to the boundary with cycle-specific observations to transmit.

This forecloses the standard fictional pathway by which a long-tenured acolyte might have accumulated loop knowledge. Whatever Jiasha knows, she did not learn it from a returning traveler.

### What she knows (bounded — the four sources)

1. **What Selûne told her directly.** The highest-authority path. Her amulet commission, the three foretold travelers, the amulets' memory-protective purpose, the Amber Crown prophecy, the instruction to send them in, the framing that an enemy of Selûne holds the duchy.
2. **General magical and historical knowledge** a Selûne acolyte would have from study and clerical training — including the existence of antimagic phenomena and the historical Acolypyrrhic Battles (~1280 DR) that created magic-broken places across the region. *Historical*, not loop-specific.
3. **Lineage knowledge from her predecessors** — what the lineage has *plausibly observed from outside* over centuries and transmitted forward:
   - The duchy went silent roughly ten years ago.
   - Travelers go in and do not come back.
   - Trapped travelers occasionally reach the boundary and communicate via slate, but most do not remember any prior day's exchange — implying some form of memory disturbance inside, observable from outside.
4. **Her own direct observation from outside** — the silence of Selûne's voice within the boundary, the look of the morning country across the line, the foot-traffic that does and does not pass through.

### What she does NOT know — and must not surface

- The cycle's specific timed steps (Sleep at 12:00, Recreate at 1:30, Teleport at 3:00, Modify Memory at 4:30, Wake at 6:00).
- That sleeping bodies are physically *moved* overnight to a reset point.
- **That dead zones shelter from the cycle.** She knows broken-magic places exist (historical, source 2); she does *not* know they preserve cycle-day memory or position for occupants. The party must discover this themselves.
- The **snapshot** mechanic and that it is what binds subjects to the bubble.
- The wall's subject-bound nature. She knows the wall exists and stops trapped travelers attempting to leave; she does not know it is a property of the snapshot on each subject rather than of the boundary geography.
- The specific identity of the hand on the duchy. *"An enemy of Selûne"* is what Selûne told her; specific names (Shar) she has not been told and would not name from outside.
- Names of specific people inside — allies, agents, anyone.
- The Amber Crown's hidden referent (Amblecrown). Per the prophecy's delivery, the misheard "Amber/Amble" stays misheard from her side.

### DM discipline at the boundary

Every answer Jiasha gives at the wall (or at her hut in the first encounter) must trace to one of the four knowledge sources above. If an answer requires information that can only have come from a returning traveler with memory continuity, **the answer is not Jiasha's to give.** The honest response is "I do not know," or a narrowly-bounded historical/observational hook the party can investigate for themselves.

A useful catch: ask *whose memory of inside Aestrum could plausibly have transmitted this to her or her lineage?* If the answer is "no one whose memory survived the night," the information has not reached her — and naming it in her voice, even as inference, is the leak.

## First Encounter — At Her Hut Before Aestrum

*The party's first encounter with Jiasha occurs at her hut at the eastern edge of the forested foothills, ~200 feet from the Aestrum boundary. The campaign's opening sequence routes through her — see [routes.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/routes.md) for the corridor mechanics.*

### Encouraging the Overnight Stop

**The campaign expects the party to spend the night at Jiasha's hut before crossing into Aestrum.** Pushing through after a full day of travel — through farmland, foothills, then a long forest stretch and an unfamiliar Aestrum approach in the dark — is bad survival practice and the party should feel that. The hut is the obvious safe place to sleep. Help them see it.

Tools to use:

- **Rain.** The cleanest motivator. Weather rolls in during the afternoon of the journey — first overcast, then drizzle, then steady rain as they reach the foothills. The road becomes mud. Cloaks and packs get soaked. By the time they reach the hut, a warm fire and a dry roof is the obviously correct choice. If they push past it into Aestrum in the rain, the consequences are real: wet camp, bad sleep, fatigue penalties for the next day's travel, the possibility of illness — and they meet the loop in worse shape than they should.
- **Light is fading.** Even without rain, the timing of the journey puts the party at the hut around dusk. The boundary crossing is fine in daylight; the Aestrum approach in full dark is unfamiliar, unmaintained, and unwise.
- **Jiasha's offer.** She offers shelter directly and without conditions. *"It's a night. There's room. The road will still be there tomorrow."* She is not pleading; she is being a quiet competent host. A party that pushes past her offer is making the choice in front of her, not by default.
- **Tier discipline if the party insists on pushing through.** Let them. The road into Aestrum is open. They miss the moon-amulet exchange (see below), arrive in Aestrum in worse condition, and have a thinner toolkit against the loop. This is a Tier-2-adjacent outcome per [rules/consequences.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/consequences.md) — they have stepped past a clearly-offered help — and the world responds accordingly. The campaign survives it, just less elegantly.

### The Moon Amulets — Mandatory Surface

**At the hut, the moon amulets must be surfaced. One per party member. This is non-negotiable from the campaign's perspective — the PCs must be presented with them, even if they then ignore or refuse them.** The amulets are the party's protection against Aestrum's daily Modify Memory reset (see [rules/time_loop.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/time_loop.md)). Without them, the campaign cannot run as designed for any PC who is not amulet-equipped or otherwise protected.

**The amulets are a Selûne-directed commission, specific to this party.** Several weeks before the party arrives, Jiasha received instructions from Selûne: a list of materials, a count, and a deadline of "tonight" — the night the party would arrive. Jiasha does not necessarily know who is coming, but she knows how many and what each amulet should be made of. **These are the only Moon Amulets she has ever made.** When the work is finished depends on the DM — she may have completed them earlier in the day, or she may still be putting finishing touches on the last one when the party arrives.

**Timing.** The exchange happens during the overnight. After dinner, before bed, in front of the fire — Jiasha brings out the amulets one at a time and offers them. She does not explain the full mechanism (she knows what Selûne told her, which is that they will protect the wearer in Aestrum). She offers them as Selûne's gift, asks the PCs to wear them, and watches who accepts.

**Personalization is the campaign's lever — and it is Selûne's hand, not Jiasha's intuition.** Each amulet's *material* is keyed to the PC's race, class, or background. **A PC who is handed an amulet that looks like it was made for them is significantly more likely to wear it.** This is the design intent. The personalization is real: Selûne knew who was coming and instructed Jiasha accordingly, even if Jiasha herself did not know specific names.

Material conventions (canonical assignments — see [items/chapter_1/moon_amulets.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/items/chapter_1/moon_amulets.md)):

- **Dwarf →** carved stone
- **Dragonborn →** carved bone
- **Half-elf →** carved wood
- **Halfling (noble background) →** unknown metal

These are not exhaustive. For any PC whose race/class/background fits a different material more naturally, use that. The principle is *"this amulet feels like it was made for me"* — execute the principle.

**Number of amulets.** Selûne's instructions specified the count. Jiasha has exactly one amulet for each party member who arrives that night — not extra, not fewer. If the party arrives with more members than expected, run it as Jiasha being surprised but having (somehow) enough; if fewer, the leftover amulet sits unworn and the party may carry it for later. **Do not run this as scarcity** — the campaign needs every PC protected — but the *exactness* of the count is a quiet signal that something authored this scene.

**If a PC refuses to wear theirs.** Their choice. Note it. They will be subject to the Modify Memory reset and will, on their first midnight inside Aestrum, lose the day's memory. The campaign continues. The other PCs may eventually persuade them; or they may insist; or the consequences play out. This is a Tier 1 player choice per [rules/consequences.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/consequences.md) — let it happen, do not strong-arm them.

**If a PC loots an amulet from her body** (in the unfortunate scenario where the party turns on Jiasha): the amulet still works. It is an object, not a divine grant. The party loses Jiasha's other utility but the amulets remain functional.

### The Amber Crown Warning — Selûne's Gift, Mandatory Delivery

**At some point during the overnight stop, Selûne speaks to Jiasha and grants her a warning to relay to the party.** This is the canonical delivery point for the Amber Crown Prophecy (see [quests/chapter_1/prophecy_of_the_amber_crown.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/quests/chapter_1/prophecy_of_the_amber_crown.md)). The prophecy is one of the campaign's most important quest threads — **especially for Chapter 2** — and the delivery must occur before the party crosses into Aestrum, while Jiasha can still hear her goddess.

**Run it like this:**

- During the evening — after dinner, often paired with or near the moon amulet exchange — Jiasha pauses mid-task. Her eyes go distant; she goes briefly still. The moment is unmistakable to anyone watching her.
- She comes to the party and tells them, plainly, that Selûne has spoken to her and given her a warning she must share now while her goddess is still speaking. She does not preamble; she does not explain; she recites.
- She delivers the prophecy verbatim, in Selûne's words as she received them:

  > *The daughter of Amber Crown, in her prime,*
  > *Shall be lost to the dark, the shadow, the climb.*
  > *By Shar's cold hand, she shall fall—*
  > *A fate most cruel, that none can forestall.*
  >
  > *But heroes will come, from far and wide,*
  > *To change her fate, and stem the tide.*
  > *They shall save her—though not too soon,*
  > *For the darkness grows in the waning moon.*
  >
  > *Find her, find her where shadows meet,*
  > *In a place where light and darkness greet.*
  > *Where steel on steel does ring through the air,*
  > *In a place where a monster stalks the heir.*

- After reciting, she sits quietly for a moment, then adds — without prompting — that **visions are imperfect.** Things get misheard, lost in translation, shaped by the receiver's understanding of the words. She has done her best to repeat what she heard. The party should hold it loosely and trust their own discoveries when they conflict with the words.
- *(Note for the DM: the "Amble Crown" / "Amber Crown" mishearing is one of the prophecy's seeded ambiguities. Jiasha herself does not catch it until much later; she heard "amber" and reports "amber." The discovery that it is **Amblecrown** is a later party realization.)*

### Fallback — If the Party Refuses the Overnight

**If the party speaks with Jiasha but elects not to stay the night, the delivery must still happen.** This quest is too important to lose, and the campaign's Chapter 2 architecture rests on it. Use whichever fallback feels most natural in the moment:

- **Divine intervention.** As the party prepares to leave, Selûne acts directly. The light in the hut shifts. Jiasha's voice changes mid-sentence — softer, older, more deliberate — and the prophecy comes through her with the cadence of a goddess speaking through a vessel. She finishes the recitation, returns to herself with a moment of disorientation, and may not fully remember speaking.
- **Roleplay Jiasha as temporarily possessed.** Same effect, played as a flash of unconscious channeling — eyes white or shining, posture stilled, the recited words clearly not her own. She comes back to herself afterward, briefly confused.
- **Last-second timing.** If the party has explicitly chosen to leave, Selûne can compress the moment — Jiasha catches them at the door, at the path, at the road. The warning comes out in fragments at first, then resolves into the full poem.

In all fallback variants: **the misheard "amber/Amble" ambiguity stays intact.** Selûne speaking through a vessel does not correct her own phrasing — that is part of the prophecy's design.

### Tier Discipline

Jiasha is a useful and meaningful NPC, but **she is not structurally required for the campaign to function** (see [rules/consequences.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/consequences.md)). A party that turns on her is Tier 2, not Tier 3. They lose her assistance and her hospitality; the amulets remain available (looted from her body if necessary); the campaign continues, less gracefully. Do not protect Jiasha with plot armor.

**Note on the prophecy and a hostile party:** If the party becomes hostile to Jiasha *before* the prophecy has been delivered, Selûne will still attempt the delivery through her — the warning is more important than the messenger's safety. If they kill her before the warning can come through, the prophecy is lost the same as if they had simply walked past her hut. The campaign continues without the Amber Crown thread.

## DM Notes

- **The lineage as carried secret.** Jiasha knows she is part of a continuous acolyte presence at the site, but the details of the lineage are deliberately thin from her side. She does not know how long it has been — "as long as anyone remembers" is the phrasing she would use, and "anyone" in her experience does not extend very far. She does not know if predecessors recorded anything, where such records would be, or whether there is a wider order somewhere she could contact. The line predates the loop and predates her, and that is the structural fact she carries. Surface it if the party asks the right questions; do not volunteer it.
- **What's actually in the lineage (DM-only authorial reserve).** The site has held a Selûne acolyte for several centuries — likely since the late stages of the Acolypyrrhic Battles, when the region's Selûne devotion was at its strongest. The role across the lineage is straightforward devotion at this threshold; what each acolyte has actually *done* during their tenure has varied with whatever Selûne asked. Jiasha's amulet commission is one such specific task, given to *her*, for *this party*, at this moment in the loop's history. Previous acolytes were given other tasks, or no tasks at all. There is no central order; the succession is direct (each acolyte's calling places the next, in some way Selûne does not explain). Records may exist somewhere — under the hut's foundation, in the older crescent stone itself, in Selûne's own keeping — but are not in Jiasha's possession.
- Her disconnection from Selûne is a direct result of Shar's active suppression of Selûne's presence within Aestrum. It is not personal to Jiasha — any acolyte would be similarly cut off here.
- **No Moon Amulet for herself.** Selûne's commission was for the traveling party, not for Jiasha. She has not made one for herself. If she ever enters Aestrum and sleeps outside a dead zone, the daily revert fires on her like any other resident — she will lose memories each morning the same as any unprotected subject. This is structural and intentional in the campaign design; do not undo it by handing her an unattuned amulet "just in case."
- **The acolyte's framework.** Jiasha sees her role as a finite assignment, not an open-ended companionship. She is here to fulfill what Selûne asked of her. Once that work is done — by her own reckoning — she would prefer to leave any party she has been traveling with and return to her own life. The trait that produces those reactions is hers; the reactions themselves are downstream of the party's choices.
