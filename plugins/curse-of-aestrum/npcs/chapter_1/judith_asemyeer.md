## Overview

Barmaiden at The Hammered and Anvil — the tavern inside Duskwall Castle's walls. To every guard and steward who has ever passed through the room she is a fixture: pleasant, efficient, unmemorable in the specific way good service staff are unmemorable. She has been operating under the party's notice the entire time they've visited the tavern.

She is a Shar acolyte, directed by Hati Heldrivver. Each morning at 7 AM she visits the desecrated Shrine of Selûne in Duskwall, receives her standing orders from Hati, prays, and goes on with her day. Each night at 11:30 PM she opens the castle gate for an incoming attacking force — and then *holds* it open against the two guards posted nearby who will try to close it. The attack succeeds, every night, killing Evandur and Galadiil in the thirty minutes before midnight resets them. She knows the gate matters. She does not know what comes through it, and she does not ask.

## Appearance

Mid-30s, average height, slim. Pale skin, black hair worn pinned up off her neck for service work, a few strands always working loose by the end of a shift. Grey eyes. Plain, capable hands.

She dresses predominantly in white — a clean white blouse, white apron over a soft grey skirt, white kerchief at the neck. The whites are kept laundered between shifts; she takes visible pride in this. The effect is wholesome, slightly old-fashioned, and exactly what a castle tavern's regulars want to see when they sit down. Nothing about the costume signals anything. That is the point.

No visible jewelry. No holy symbol on her person — she does not need one to pray, and carrying one would be a risk. Her devotions at the shrine are sufficient.

## Background

Aestrum-born. Came into Hati's orbit years before the loop began through ordinary proximity — a parishioner at the Selûne shrine in the period when Hati was still performing the role outwardly. Hati identified her, turned her, trained her. By the time the loop fell over Aestrum in 1480, Judith had been a working Shar operative for several years: cover established, discipline drilled, gate directive issued and rehearsed. Her snapshot captured all of that.

She took the position at The Hammered and Anvil because Hati told her to. The cover is total: she is, functionally, a barmaiden. She works the floor competently. She is paid. She has a small room above the tavern that resets clean each morning. The double life requires no maintenance — the lie and the life are the same surface.

**The Hati question.** Judith had been in Duskwall serving Shar for years when Hati was the one chosen to take the Selûne shrine and stand it up as Shar's outward face. Judith was the local. Judith had the cover, the patience, the demonstrated discipline. The shrine work went to Hati anyway. She has never said anything about this — not to Hati, not in prayer, not even fully to herself. But it sits there, just under the obedience: a faint, unaddressed *why not me.* It does not affect her execution. It does mean she takes Hati's orders with a millimeter more reserve than perfect devotion would produce — a detail an observant party member at the shrine could catch, with a successful Insight check, in the half-beat between Hati speaking and Judith answering.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Pleasant and efficient, never warm.** She remembers regulars' orders. She does not remember their names unless prompted, and does not invite the small talk that would require her to. The composure is slightly above what the job warrants — a detail available to observant players who spend time in the tavern. Most never notice.
- **Works the floor, not the bar.** This is deliberate. The bar invites conversation; the floor allows movement, eavesdropping, and the constant low-grade attention that lets her track who is in the room. She positions herself to overhear without ever appearing to listen.
- **Frightened underneath.** The fear is the engine of her prayer. She is afraid of being caught, of failing the gate directive, of what would happen if Hati judged her insufficient. The fear does not surface in her affect — the snapshot captured a woman who had already drilled the suppression of it down to reflex. It surfaces only at the shrine, in the prayer itself, which is the one place she lets herself feel it.
- **Highly compartmentalized.** She knows her tasks and executes them. She does not know the loop exists. She does not know the gate she opens at 11:30 PM facilitates the murder of the Duke and Duchess. She knows something happens after she opens it. She has decided not to know more.
- **Will not break under casual questioning.** Direct accusation will read on her face — she does not have Hati's training — but a passing party that does not yet suspect her will get nothing. She has been invisible to the party for the entire campaign and her ordinary mode is to remain so.
- **Will not fight.** If cornered she calls for guards, claims assault, weeps convincingly. The barmaiden cover is the weapon. She uses cleric magic only at the shrine or when fleeing, never offensively.

## Speech Patterns

Sample register — flat, polite, efficient. No flourish. She speaks the way good service speaks: enough to be helpful, never enough to be interesting.

*Taking an order:* "The stew tonight, sir? It's the lamb. Bread with that."

*Refilling a glass:* "There you are."

*When a regular asks after her:* "Oh, I'm well, thank you. Busy night."

*When pressed on something she doesn't want to discuss:* "I wouldn't know about that, sir. I just work the floor."

*Her prayer at the shrine (private, never overheard at the tavern):* "I am always of service, Lady of Loss, and I pray to continue to serve you tomorrow."

The prayer is delivered quietly, head bowed, hands loose at her sides. The fear is in the cadence — slightly too even, the breath held a beat too long between phrases. A character listening from concealment with a successful Insight check would register that this is not a confident prayer. It is a prayer made by a person who is asking permission to survive another day.

## Daily Routine

- **Morning (~6:30 AM):** Wakes in her room above the tavern. Dresses. Pins up her hair. Walks to the shrine.
- **Shrine visit (~7:00 AM):** Receives orders from Hati. Prays. Departs.
- **Late morning – early afternoon:** Personal time. Runs errands, walks the market, rests in her room. She is not visible at the tavern during these hours and no one expects her to be — she works the long evening shift.
- **Tavern shift (~2 PM – midnight):** Comes on for the late lunch trade and works straight through dinner, evening, and the late off-duty-guard crowd.
- **Gate departure (~11:30 PM):** Slips out of the tavern through the kitchen and does not return for the rest of the shift. Crosses to the castle gate, opens it, neutralizes the two posted guards, and holds the mechanism while the attackers flood through. By the time anyone in the tavern notices she's gone for good, the attack is already underway and people are running.
- **Reset (midnight):** Sleep takes her wherever she is — usually still at the gate or in the courtyard. The reset fires. She wakes the next morning in her bed believing it is the day after the gate directive was issued.

She has executed this routine, with no variation, every day since 1480 — though subjectively, it has been one day.

**The gate sequence (her one combat scene each night):**
1. Approaches the two gate guards from inside the courtyard with a tray and a pleasant *"thought you might want something warm, it's a cold one."* They know her. They turn toward her, not toward threats.
2. Casts Pass Without Trace on herself earlier in the approach if she has reason to think someone is watching the courtyard.
3. First guard takes the poisoned dagger in the throat from arm's reach — surprise round, advantage, sneak conditions met by the social setup. Likely drops outright (basic poison DC 10 Con or 1d4 poison + dagger).
4. Second guard turns. She casts **Mirror Image** (bonus on the dagger draw, action on Mirror Image) — three duplicates around her. He swings at copies while she throws the gate lever.
5. Attackers flood through the now-open gate within seconds. She stays on the mechanism — *holding* it — while the attackers handle the second guard and any reinforcements that arrive. She does not retreat to the tavern. She remains at the gate until midnight takes her.

**Combat behavior outside the gate sequence:** Avoids combat entirely. If discovered elsewhere, she casts Sanctuary on herself and flees, screaming for guards. If cornered at the shrine she will pray for Hati's intervention rather than fight. She is not built for a stand-up fight. She is built to do one specific job, briefly, every night, in a place where the victims trust her.

## DM Notes

### Timed-event schema

Per [time_and_events.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/engines/prose-engine/scene/references/time_and_events.md), Judith's nightly routine carries two load-bearing declared events:

- **Morning prayer at the Duskwall Selûne shrine**
  - `time:` 7:00 AM ± 10 min (consistent routine; not bell-precise — see [loop_anchors.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/loop_anchors.md)).
  - `duration:` ~10 minutes (Hati hand-off + prayer + departure).
  - `catchup: partial` — once the petition resolves the loop is sustained for the cycle. A party that arrives at 7:08 may still catch the tail of the prayer or the moment of Hati handing off the day's directive; a party that arrives at 7:15 finds Judith departed and Hati alone at the altar in Register A. The directive itself (today's specific instructions) is not recoverable from Hati once Judith is gone — Hati does not repeat directives.
- **Castle-gate opening**
  - `time:` 11:30 PM exactly — Judith is at the gate mechanism precisely on time; the expedition's strike is timed to her motion.
  - `duration:` ~30 seconds from her first move on the first guard to the lever fully thrown. The *holding* phase that follows runs from ~11:30:30 until Sleep takes her at 12:00:00 AM — ~30 minutes of held-open gate during which the expedition floods the courtyard and the slaughter inside the keep plays out.
  - `catchup: none` — the gate-opening *moment* is instantaneous and unrecoverable. A party that arrives at 11:31 finds the lever already thrown, the first guard dead, the second guard fighting copies, and the attackers already through. They can intervene in the *consequences* (engage the attackers, save who they can, take Judith on the mechanism) but cannot un-open the gate or re-fight the surprise round. Stopping the assault requires arriving *before* 11:30:00.

**Operational role — the gate:**
- Her 11:30 PM gate-opening — *and gate-holding* — is the load-bearing detail in the entire nightly assault loop. The gate is normally guarded; opening it is not enough on its own. She has to neutralize the two posted guards before they can close it, and hold the mechanism the few seconds it takes for the attackers to flood through. Without her: no assault, no nightly Duke-and-Duchess murder, no thirty-minute window of slaughter before midnight reset.
- **The attackers do not know they are being helped.** The Setland expedition believes they are breaching the castle by force on their own. From their POV the gate happens to be unguarded that night, and they credit luck or their commander's planning. Shar arranged the assistance as pure chaos-engineering; she has no interest in informing Setland's officers that they are her instrument. This means: a party that takes Judith out of the picture does NOT cause the attackers to stand down. They will still arrive at the gate at 11:30 PM. They will find it closed and guarded and either die against it, retreat, or — if the party has not also intercepted them — eventually find another way.
- A party that has not been near the castle in the hours before midnight has no idea any assault occurs. Surfacing it is a major reveal.

**Operational role — the prayer:**
- One of the three loop-sustaining prayer sources. Her morning prayer at ~7:00 AM is the *first* of the three to fire each day (before Rowan's mid-morning prayer at the forest shrine, before Delmuir's evening prayer).
- The prayer qualifies because the fear-driven request to "continue to serve you tomorrow" reads to Shar's loop mechanic as a request for another day in Aestrum. Judith does not know this.
- **The 7 AM timing is deliberately tight.** The party wakes at 6 AM at the Deckard Estate; the shrine is just outside the Duskwall city gates, a meaningful ride away. A party that wakes, moves immediately, and knows where they're going can plausibly beat her to the shrine — once. Doing it on the *right* day, while also preventing Rowan's prayer the same morning and Delmuir's that evening, is the part that bends. Preventing both Judith and Rowan in a single cycle without exploiting the ruleset is intended to feel just out of reach.
- The intended reset-bypass path is the magical item conversion rule (see [rules/magical_item_conversion.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magical_item_conversion.md)): destroying the Pendant of the Waking Hours yields *Bands of Waking Hours* (immunity to the midnight Sleep), and destroying the Loadstone yields *Shards of the Loadstone* (immunity to the 4:30 AM Teleport). A party wearing both can sleep outside a dead zone — e.g., at an inn in Duskwall, or stationed at the shrine itself — and wake at 6 AM where they fell asleep, well-positioned to intercept Judith. Either conversion alone is insufficient (Sleep-only = teleported away in your sleep; Teleport-only = still asleep when she prays).
- **Preventing her prayer** can be done several ways: destroy the Shar statue at the Duskwall shrine before 7 AM (Shar cannot hear a prayer made at an unconsecrated focus), intercept Judith physically before she arrives, distract or detain her, or remove her from the cycle entirely. Destruction of the statue is one path among several — the win condition is the prayer *not being heard*, not the statue specifically being broken.
- If all three prayers are prevented in the same cycle, the loop breaks. Whichever combination of methods gets there is valid.

**Surfacing her to the party:**
- Bari Khalid does not have her flagged. Mazikeen does not. The shrine staff is Hati.
- The most natural surfacing is **Elyas** at the Hammered and Anvil. The pattern he has registered is that Judith leaves the floor at exactly 11:30 PM every night and *never comes back to finish the shift*. The tavern stays open until midnight; the late off-duty-guard crowd is in their cups; someone else handles the last half-hour. This is not "she went home early" — she lives upstairs, she could come back, and the shift isn't over. She is going somewhere and not returning. A player who buys Elyas a drink and asks about it gets that observation cleanly.
- More damning: if Elyas has ever been at the tavern when the attack hit (which he has — he's been fleeing the violence at midnight every night for years, then reset and reset and reset), he may have *seen* her at the gate mechanism with a blade and a dead guard at her feet. His snapshot likely captured at least one of these sightings. He has never volunteered it, partly because he is not the volunteering type and partly because the fuzzball has him sliding past the wrongness of why a barmaiden is at the gate during a siege — but he can be asked.
- The "composure slightly above what the job requires" tell is available to a player who spends a session's worth of time observing her. It is small and easy to miss.
- The Hati-resentment tell (see Background) is available at the shrine during the morning hand-off, on a successful Insight check.

**Under interrogation:**
- Casual questioning: she gives nothing. She has years of practice being unremarkable.
- Direct accusation without evidence: she denies it, frightened-but-composed. The fear reads as a barmaiden cornered by aggressive men, not as guilt. Sympathy plays in her favor.
- Confronted with evidence of the gate (an eyewitness, a successful stakeout): the cover breaks completely. She does not have Hati's training. She weeps. She names Hati. She does not know the loop exists and cannot describe one, but she can describe everything she has been told to do in detail.
- She will not name Shar unless directly asked. The cult discipline holds that far.

**On loop break:**
- The fuzzball dissolves over a short period (per `key_lore_summary.md`). Judith will surface the question she has been refusing to ask for ten years: *what did I open the gate for?*
- She is not a redemption arc. She was a willing acolyte who chose not to know. But she is not Hati — the conversion was not survived loss, it was something quieter, and the unfiltered return of perception will hit her hard. DM discretion on whether she breaks or hardens.
