## Overview

Able Seaman aboard *The Incorrigible*. Born "Rick" — same as the ship's musician — the crew distinguished them as "Rick" and "Bald Rick" in reference to his complete lack of hair. He heard "Bald Rick" as "Baldric" and adopted it as a proper name. He has introduced himself as Baldric ever since and has never once connected the nickname to the bald head it describes. The crew has found this funny for years and not corrected him; he, in turn, has come to feel that being given a real name by the crew was the moment he was accepted aboard. The misunderstanding has produced genuine warmth on both sides.

He is also the higher-ranked of the two seamen on board — Able Seaman outranks Ordinary Seaman, which means he outranks Rick Hastley. Rick has lorded the (inverted, imaginary) rank distinction over Baldric for years, and Baldric has never corrected him. He simply does not feel the need. The rank shows up where it matters — on watch rotations, on shore-leave precedence, in pay — and Rick is welcome to whatever pride he wants to take in being called "Ordinary."

Beyond that, Baldric is exactly what he appears to be: chipper, capable, talkative, and constitutionally incapable of keeping a secret. Rylin selected him for the crew specifically because of that last trait.

## Appearance

Mid-40s. Slender and sinewy — built like a man who has hauled lines for thirty years and never carried an ounce he didn't need. 5'8". Completely bald on top, with grey arm hair and a grey-stubbled jaw he keeps short. Sun-darkened skin, pale where the bandana sits.

A red bandana over the crown of his head — practical (the sun is murder on a bald scalp at sea), habitual (he is rarely without it), and so consistent that crew members who have seen him barefaced still picture him with it on. A faded blue and white striped top, sleeves rolled, the colors washed out from years of salt and sun. Loose linen pants. Bare feet on deck, simple sandals ashore.

A friendly grin full of crooked teeth — the teeth go in several directions and one of them is gold. The grin is the dominant feature of his face. New acquaintances often need a beat to get past it; everyone who knows him stopped seeing it years ago and just hears the voice behind it.

## Background

Joined *The Incorrigible* at some point Baldric himself is hazy on — "a good while ago, lad, before the new mainmast" is as specific as he gets. Came aboard as plain Rick. Earned the "Baldric" name within his first month. Made Able Seaman through a combination of genuine seamanship competence and never, ever causing trouble. Has no family ashore that he speaks of. The ship is his life and the crew is his people.

He does not know — and has never had any reason to suspect — that he was hired specifically because of his cheerful inability to keep his mouth shut.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Chipper as a default.** He wakes chipper and goes to bed chipper. Bad weather is "lively." A short ration is "a chance to enjoy the next meal proper." A near miss with a cargo boom is "well, look at that." This is not performance. It is the actual setting of his emotional baseline.
- **Genuinely capable.** Don't be fooled by the smile and the talk. Rylin doesn't keep incompetents aboard. Baldric is a real seaman: knots, sail handling, dead reckoning by feel, the small running maintenance that keeps a ship from coming apart. He can be trusted with the watch. He has weathered three storms most of the crew would not have walked away from.
- **Outgoing to a fault.** Will strike up conversation with anyone within speaking range — dockhands, fishmongers, harbor watch officers, well-dressed strangers obviously not interested. Treats this as basic friendliness. Does not register the moment a listener stops being engaged and starts being trapped.
- **Cannot keep a secret. Genuinely cannot.** He does not withhold information by accident — he reaches eagerly for any interesting fact in his head the moment a conversation creates a gap for it. Asking him to keep something to himself works for about as long as it takes him to find the next person to talk to. He does not consider this a flaw. He considers it being friendly.
- **Loyal to the ship and the crew.** Defends Rylin the way crew defend their first mate — reflexively, warmly, with stories. Defends Rick Hastley as "the best singer in three ports" without ever feeling the need to mention that he outranks him.
- **The rank thing genuinely does not bother him.** He is amused, in a quiet way, that Rick is so invested in being "Ordinary." He has never seen the point of correcting it. The truth is on the ship's papers and that has always been enough.
- **Sentimental about the name.** If pressed on it, he will say "Baldric" is the name the crew gave him, and the day they gave it to him was the day he felt he belonged here. He has never connected the name to his head. If someone explains it to him, he will laugh — sincerely, fully, with his whole face — and not feel diminished. He will keep the name anyway. It is *his* now, regardless of where it came from.

## Speech Patterns

A sailor's drawl — comfortable, unhurried, easy to follow. He is friendly and outgoing in unprompted speech and *does not* misuse words there: his ordinary monologue is clear.

**Critical presentation rule — when to mishear or misspeak:**
- Baldric only swaps or mishears words **in response to something a player has said**. He repeats a player's term back wrong, or substitutes a wrong word into a follow-up sentence, when the player has just used one. He does not seed malapropisms into his own unprompted speech.
- This protects the table from confusion in the first couple of exchanges before the players catch on. A new player who hears Baldric introduce himself doesn't have to decode anything. By the time the player is asking questions, the response-bound malapropism pattern emerges naturally.
- The wrong word is always close enough to the right word that the meaning is recoverable. *Constellation* for *conversation*, *paradigm* for *paragon*, *condiment* for *compliment*, *aromatics* for *armaments*. Same starting syllable, similar rhythm. A player can decode it without breaking the scene.
- He never notices the swap. If corrected, he agrees genially and uses the wrong word again three sentences later.

**Sample exchanges:**

*Unprompted greeting (no malapropism):* "Mornin', friends! Fine weather we've drawn today — barely a swell out past the breakwater. Step careful on those wet planks."

*Player: "We were hoping for a conversation."*
Baldric: "A *constellation*! Aye, happy to oblige. What's on your mind, then?"

*Player: "Rylin speaks well of you."*
Baldric: "He's a *paradigm* of a man, the first mate. Generous, fair-handed. Speaks well of everyone, far as I know."

*Player: "What was in the crates you offloaded?"*
Baldric: "Couldn't tell ya, lad. Heavy ones, those — could be *aromatics*, could be salt, could be a great fat hog for all I know. We load 'em, we move 'em, the captain knows the rest."

*Player: "Doesn't it bother you that Rick lords his rank over you?"*
Baldric: (grin going wider) "Oh, Rick! Best singer in three ports. Likes his *rank-precedent*, does Rick. Doesn't trouble me any."

*Player asks about his name:* "Baldric. That's what they called me when I joined on, and it stuck. Means somethin' to a man, gettin' a real name from the crew. I was just Rick before that — and we've already got a Rick, you see, so..." (No malapropism — this is his own story, told unprompted in feeling. The pattern is reactive, not narrative.)

## DM Notes

**The Baldric / Rick rank gag:**
- Able Seaman outranks Ordinary Seaman. Baldric (Able) outranks Rick (Ordinary). Rick believes the reverse and has lorded it over Baldric for years.
- Baldric has known the truth the entire time and has chosen not to correct it. He is not being passive-aggressive; he genuinely finds Rick's pride in the rank funny and harmless. He sees no reason to disturb it.
- This is the *opposite* of the lever it is for Rick. Confronting Baldric with the rank truth produces no reaction the party can use — he already knows, he doesn't care, and explaining it to him does not change anything for him.
- See [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/rick_hastley.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/rick_hastley.md) for the corresponding entry; the rank gag is a wedge against Rick's loyalty to Rylin, not against Baldric's.

**Ledger Secret 1 — Unwitting Misinformation Channel:**
- Rylin values Baldric specifically because Baldric cannot keep secrets. He hires the man for the leak.
- Rylin feeds Baldric information he wants to travel — drops it casually in earshot, mentions it as if in confidence, asks Baldric "not to mention" things he absolutely wants mentioned. Baldric tells everyone within twenty minutes, cheerfully, with full commitment.
- Baldric has no idea this is happening. He believes he is simply a friendly man whose first mate happens to share interesting things with him.
- **Inside the loop:** the channel works locally but goes nowhere globally. Baldric leaks to Aestrum waterfront ears that reset and forget by next morning, and the ship has not sailed since Day 9 — only a handful of cycles at the current point of play. Rylin's network outside Aestrum stopped receiving transmissions through this channel the day the ship became overdue, but it has not yet been overdue long enough for the network to draw firm conclusions.
- **Party exploitation, inside the loop:** Limited. Information fed to Baldric reaches Rylin reliably (Rylin is on the ship and remembers across his own snapshot baseline if he is loop-aware; otherwise he resets too). It does *not* reach the broader network until the ship sails.
- **Party exploitation, on loop break:** Significant. Anything seeded into Baldric in the final cycles becomes part of his memory at the moment the loop ends. When the ship sails, he carries it with him and broadcasts it at every subsequent port. This is the same sleeper-cell mechanic that applies to Rick's encoded performances — a one-shot post-loop propagation event.
- The mechanic is reliable enough to plan around. Baldric does not need to like the party for this to work; he just needs to be in conversation with them.

**Ledger Secret 2 — Unwitting Accessory to the Castle Detonation:**
- On the day *The Incorrigible* arrived in Aestrum, Baldric helped load and transport the 256 vials of alchemist's fire onto the waterfront, where they were sold to an elderly buyer (Luca, disguised by the Mirror of Illusion specifically to prevent the deal from being traced back to The Shrikes). Baldric did not know what was in the crates and did not ask. He remembers the day clearly because it is, subjectively, *yesterday* — his snapshot was taken in the hours after that offload.
- What followed: Luca's men transported the crates through the castle's side entrance into the maze. In Session 5/2, a Shrike accidentally kicked a jar — chain explosion. Half of Duskwall Castle was destroyed; a section of cliffside collapsed into the ocean. **The party accidentally contributed to the detonation** through their interference with the Shrike operation in the maze. Castle damage resets each cycle along with everything else.
- Baldric loaded the crates *once*. He does not load them again each morning — the Recreate effect handles the rest of the chain (the crates are wherever Luca's people last had them at start of day, the kick happens again, the castle goes up again, the castle resets). From Baldric's snapshot perspective, the offload happened yesterday and that was the end of his involvement. From real continuity, his single act of loading on Day 9 is what has been re-detonating the castle every night since.
- He is, in mechanical terms, an unwitting accessory to the repeated destruction of half a duchy's primary castle. He has no awareness. He remembers the crates were heavy. He does not connect the offloading to anything because no one has ever drawn the line for him, and within the loop the line cannot easily be drawn — the castle is intact every morning.
- **Party options:** Drawing the line for him would be devastating *the moment the loop breaks* — not before. Inside the loop, Baldric narrating the offload in dockside taverns goes nowhere outside Aestrum (the ship has not sailed) and within Aestrum reaches only people who reset and forget by the next morning. The instant the ship sails post-loop, his cheerful narration becomes a one-way trigger that collapses Rylin's plausible deniability across every Calimshan port the crew touches.
- This is sleeper leverage against Rylin, not against Baldric. Baldric himself is not in legal jeopardy — he is a manifestly innocent porter — but his eventual narration of the facts, post-loop, puts Rylin in serious jeopardy with both Calimshan authorities and his own Erethezra network.

**Loop status:**
- Baldric *is* loop-affected. *The Incorrigible* sailed into the Aestrum bubble in the hours before midnight on Day 9 of campaign time and dropped anchor offshore. The crew's snapshot was taken there.
- Baldric resets each midnight aboard ship. Subjectively, every day ashore in Duskwall is his second day at this port. Objectively, every day since Day 9 has been a repeat — however many cycles that has been at the current moment of play. He has had the same chipper conversations with the same dockhands every morning since and has no idea.
- The crew as a whole is not loop-aware. None of them understand why business in Duskwall keeps not concluding. Baldric in particular has been chipper about it the entire time.

**On a loop break:**
- Baldric notices nothing at the moment of break. The ship's business finally concludes; the ship sails. He continues being chipper somewhere else.
- Everything seeded into Baldric in the final cycles sails with him and propagates at every port thereafter. Both his Ledger Secrets — the misinformation channel and the alchemist's-fire accessory account — activate the moment the keel clears Aestrum waters. This is the sleeper-cell mechanic; see Secret 1 and Secret 2 above.
