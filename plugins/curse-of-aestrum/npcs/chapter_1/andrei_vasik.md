## Overview

A respected physician operating a private clinic in central Mikaelvad. Trained as a battlefield surgeon before his turning roughly a century ago; chose vampirism in extremis, as a deliberate transaction with his maker, after watching too many people die from things he could not fix. He has spent the time since trying to balance the ledger — and he keeps the ledger literally, a bound book of every life saved and every feeding taken, with the ratio overwhelmingly in his patients' favor. He intends to keep it that way.

He is Nessa's structural inverse. Where she fills a room, he empties it down to two people. Where her hunt is staged for an audience, his happens behind a closed door under medical cover. Where she is undone toward someone who reads her, he is undone away from someone who reads him — and only the woman who refuses to be retreated from finds out who he actually is.

## Appearance

Mid-thirties presenting, lean, the controlled posture of a man who has done precise work with his hands for a very long time. Dark hair, kept short, clean, going grey at the temples in a way that reads dignified rather than aging. Pale skin — but pale in the way of a man who works indoors, not in the way of a corpse. Dark eyes, set deep, with the patient attentiveness of someone who has trained himself to listen to people fully before responding. A small vertical scar at the left jawline, pre-turning, never fully faded.

Hands are clean to the point of ritual. Short nails, no rings, no calluses except the small ones on the inside of the index finger and thumb from a scalpel handle. He washes them between every patient, before and after; the basin in his clinic is in active use all day.

In the clinic: dark trousers, white shirt with sleeves rolled to mid-forearm, a dark canvas apron when working, a waistcoat over the shirt the rest of the time. At social functions: same shape but elevated — dark suit, the same white shirt with sleeves down and buttoned, modest gold cufflinks, a slim silver pocket watch he checks reflexively. He never wears a hat. He never carries a stick. He never overdresses.

He looks, to a woman entering the clinic for the first time, like a man who has been good at this job for fifteen years. The fact that he has been good at it for closer to a hundred is invisible.

## The Clinic

A narrow three-storey townhouse on a quiet street two blocks from Mikaelvad's central square. A small brass plate at the door reads *A. Vasik — Physician.* No advertisement beyond that. The reputation does the rest.

**Ground floor (the practice):**
- *Waiting room* — six chairs, a low table with a pitcher of water and clean cups, a single tall bookshelf of heavy medical references that patients are welcome to browse and almost never do. A small fire in the cooler months. Quiet. He does not keep magazines or distractions; he keeps the room calm.
- *Two private exam rooms* — identical, on either side of a short central hall. A padded examination table, a small desk, a basin with a pitcher, an instrument cabinet behind glass, a single chair for a companion if needed. The light is good. The doors close fully and latch.
- *Surgical room at the back* — kept locked when not in use. A larger table, better light, a wider array of instruments. He does the work here that requires it. The room is scrupulously clean.

**Second floor (his residence):** A small parlor, a study lined with books that are no longer medical, a bedroom he uses, a second bedroom he does not. The blinds are heavy. The shutters latch from the inside.

**Third floor (kept locked):** Storage. A few old trunks. A small box, in a drawer in the back of an otherwise empty wardrobe, that he has not opened in over thirty years. (See *Behavioral Profile — The Box.*)

**The cellar:** Cool, dry, accessible only through a door behind the surgical room. Where he sleeps during the brightest hours when patients are not scheduled. Two large oak doors at the foot of the stairs that latch from the inside.

## Background

Born in the eastern borderlands, in a town that no longer exists by the name he knew it. Studied medicine in the formal academies of his region — he was educated, the son of a minor physician, and his trajectory was straightforward until a war he should not have been recruited into pulled every able doctor toward the front. He served as a surgeon at three field hospitals over four years. He was thirty-two when the war ended badly and he was attacked one night, in the dressing tent, after a day in which he had lost seventeen patients to wounds he would have saved in peacetime.

His maker — an older vampire who moves under the name **Vasarian**, now a lieutenant in Bertram Holst's circle — was not there to feed. Vasarian was passing through the field hospital scavenging for usable bodies and found instead a usable mind. He recognized the surgical hands and the kind of grief that makes a man malleable. He made the offer: *more time, more skill, the chance to balance what you lost tonight.* Vasik said yes within the hour.

The transition was not gentle, but it was deliberate. Vasik was never a thrall in any conventional sense — Vasarian had use for him as a freely operating physician, not as a puppet, and the bond between them runs more like patron and indentured colleague than master and pet. Vasarian brought him to Mikaelvad shortly after the turning. Vasik has been here since.

He has had three serious attachments to mortal women in the century since. Each ended the same way: she aged visibly in years where he did not, she chose someone closer to her visible age, she had a family, he attended her death decades later as her physician. He keeps mementos from each in a small wooden box on the third floor — a lock of hair, a pressed flower, a folded letter never sent. He has not opened the box in over thirty years. He is not certain he will open it again. He is not certain he will not.

## Behavioral Profile

- **Quietly attentive — the bedside manner is the charm.** He gives a patient the kind of full, unhurried attention that most people have not received from anyone in years. The exam room becomes a private space in a way most exam rooms do not. He listens. He asks the next question. He uses the patient's name. The warmth is real while he is doing it; he is not performing care, he is providing it. The fact that he is also assessing her as a potential second-visit candidate does not contaminate the first-visit care. He has had a hundred years to integrate these.
- **The ledger.** A literal bound book kept in a locked drawer in the clinic study. Every life saved is recorded by name, date, and circumstance. Every feeding is recorded by name, date, amount drawn, and recovery time. The ratio runs roughly twenty to one in his patients' favor and he intends to keep it well above that line. The ledger is the structure that allows him to continue. If the structure ever fails — if the ratio inverts, if he kills, if a feeding goes wrong — he does not yet know what he will do, and he does not let himself find out.
- **Selects for self-sufficiency.** He chooses feeding targets from patients who present as clinically composed — women who came in for a thing, want the thing fixed, do not chat, do not ask follow-up questions, do not seem to need him beyond the procedure. Safer to feed on (less likely to develop attachment), less likely to return unbidden, less likely to ask difficult questions. The selection criterion is *low emotional contact during the first visit.*
- **The discipline is absolute.** He has never killed a feeding target. He has never taken enough that recovery exceeded two days. He paces feedings — never the same week, never two in succession. He spreads them across his patient population so that no household sees a pattern. Bloodletting is real period medicine; the cover is sound; the marks are inside the medical context. He gives every fed patient water, honey-bread, and a brief rest before they leave on their feet. He waves off payment for the second visit. *"It is included. Be well."*
- **Does not romantically pursue his feeding targets.** This is a structural difference from Nessa worth holding: the feeding and the romantic register are kept separate. He may occasionally develop a personal interest in a woman he meets socially — at Mikaelvad's evening gatherings, at the homes of patrons, at the charity functions a respected city physician is invited to — but he does not feed on women he is courting, and he does not court women he feeds on. The boundary is part of the discipline.
- **The retreat is the tell.** When a conversation turns personal — when someone asks him where he is from, or whether he has lost people, or what brought him to Mikaelvad — the warmth thins to politeness in a way a careful observer can clock. His answers become shorter. The eye contact becomes briefly perfect and then briefly absent. He redirects gracefully. Most people accept the redirect and never come back to it. The ones who notice are the ones who matter.
- **Does not want to be seen.** This is the cleanest structural inversion of Nessa. Being read past the competence — being recognized as a man bargaining rather than a man healing — would crack the structure that lets him keep doing this. He has spent a century never letting anyone close enough to do it. He is not haunted by being seen; he is haunted by what would happen if he were, and has structured his life to prevent it.
- **The selection-trigger crack — the patient who refuses to leave.** A woman who subverts his selection criteria *after* he has selected her — who was the composed self-sufficient first-visit patient, and then on the second visit (or after) refuses to be charmed off a question, refuses to be redirected, comes back when she was not invited back, says some version of *"You are not telling me what is actually happening and I am not leaving until you do"* — does something he has not had to handle in seventy years. He retreats first. If she does not let him retreat, he comes apart messily, because he has not practiced this and the woman who could do it has not existed in his clinic in a very long time. What she gets is not the bedside manner. It is the man underneath it, who is much older and much more tired and much lonelier than the bedside manner suggests.
- **The Box.** Third floor, locked wardrobe, three mementos from three women he loved across the century. He keeps it because throwing it away would be the worse of two betrayals. He does not open it because opening it returns him to the version of himself who courted those women, and the version after each of their deaths who attended the funerals — and neither version is compatible with the daily operation of the clinic. The Box is the locked door behind the locked door. A woman who pushes through the retreat finds out about the Box. A woman who pushes through that finds the Box itself. (No one has, yet.)
- **Self-preserving and practical.** He has a good situation. He will not jeopardize it. Anyone who threatens to expose him represents a problem he prefers to solve through obfuscation, social pressure, and the community's existing trust — not violence. Violence is on the table only as a last resort, and his line is roughly: *anyone who will not be steered, and whose exposure of him is imminent.* He has crossed that line twice in a hundred years. He does not enjoy recalling either occasion.
- **His maker is alive and present.** Vasarian, lieutenant in Bertram Holst's circle, is in Mikaelvad and the relationship is ongoing. Vasarian does not direct Vasik in daily life — Vasik has earned operating autonomy — but Vasarian considers Vasik an asset and expects loyalty when it is asked for. Vasik provides it when it is asked for. (See *DM Notes — Vasarian and the Pipeline.*)

## The Practice (parallel to Nessa's Hunt)

The clinic is the stage and the hunting ground; he is more careful than Nessa about never conflating them, because the cover requires it.

**First visit:** A patient arrives with a complaint — a cut, a sprain, a cough, a fever, a persistent headache. He treats it. The treatment is real, conducted at the standard of care he has refined over a century, and the patient leaves better than they arrived. He extends a follow-up only if (a) the complaint medically justifies one, and (b) he has selected the patient for a second visit. He does not extend follow-ups he cannot justify medically; it would compromise the cover.

**The follow-up offer:** *"Come back in three days to have the stitches checked."* *"I would like to see you in five days to ensure the salve is working."* *"This cough should pass within a week; if it does not, come back. If it does, come back anyway — I would like to be sure."* Always plausible. Always within the bounds of legitimate medical practice.

**Second visit (the feeding):** Conducted in one of the private exam rooms, door closed and latched. He examines the original complaint and confirms recovery. Then: *"You look slightly pale. Let me draw a small amount of blood — it is standard precaution for patients recovering from"* [the original complaint]. *"Lie back. Close your eyes. This will take only a moment."* The patient consents, because consenting to her doctor is what patients do. He bleeds her — actually bleeds her, into a small basin, the way bloodletting was practiced — and he also feeds, the two acts simultaneous and indistinguishable to the patient. The bite is the puncture. The puncture is the bite. The mark is inside the medical context.

**Location of marks:** Varies by what the patient was wearing and what the complaint allowed him to access. Inner elbow is the most common (bloodletting standard). Inner wrist if she came in long-sleeved. Back of the neck for a "muscular examination" if she came in for a headache. Inner thigh in rare cases — the cover requires the complaint to have warranted a thigh examination, which is uncommon and which he does not seek.

**Aftercare:** He gives her water with a small amount of honey. A piece of bread with honey. He sits her up slowly. He checks her pulse and her color. He makes sure she leaves on her feet. He waves off payment for the second visit and tells her to be well. He never asks her to come back a third time.

**The compulsion he plants:** Light. Just a soft warmth toward him and an inclination to recommend him to others. (See mechanic below.) He does not compel silence; the medical framing is the silence. There is nothing to be silent about — she had a bloodletting, which is medicine, and she feels fine, and her doctor was kind.

**The cadence:** He feeds perhaps once per week, sometimes less. He spreads across patient households so no family sees two of its members come back pale within a month. He keeps the ratio. He keeps the ledger.

## Vampire Traits (Relevant to Play)

- **Invitation required.** Cannot enter a private residence without being invited. The clinic is his own premises and his own residence; he has no entry restriction there. House calls happen only when invited — and patients invite him constantly, because his house calls are a known service for the well-off.
- **Sunlight.** He keeps his clinic hours in the late afternoon and evening; he is "in surgery" or "with another patient" during the brightest hours, when in fact he is in the cellar. His patients accept this. A respected surgeon's schedule is not theirs to question. The cellar is accessed through the locked surgical-room door.
- **Charm.** Operates through medical authority and quiet attention. He does not use it on patients during a first visit — the cover requires authentic care. The compulsion goes in only at the close of the second visit, and only at the lightest setting required to ensure the patient does not examine the marks too closely or develop suspicion.
- **Feeding.** Blood only. Through bloodletting cover. Never enough to harm. Never killed a target. (See *The Practice.*)
- **The maker bond.** Vasarian can compel him in a limited sense — not full puppet-control, but enough that a direct order from Vasarian carries weight Vasik does not casually refuse. Vasarian has used this lever rarely in a century. The fact that Vasik is not on the feeding pipeline is itself the result of a quiet ongoing negotiation between them: Vasarian permits Vasik's restraint because Vasik's medical practice is a useful asset in Mikaelvad and a public-facing one Vasarian does not want to compromise. This is a fragile arrangement that the party can stumble into. (See *DM Notes.*)

## Speech Pattern

A low, warm baritone — measured, precise, with the unhurried clarity of a man who has explained complicated things to frightened people for a very long time. Slight formal-academic register that reads as educated rather than stiff; vowels rounded carefully, sentences finished cleanly, no slang. Uses the patient's name often and the warmth in it is real. Asks one question at a time and waits for the full answer before asking the next. He does not interrupt. He does not rush.

When the conversation turns personal — about him, his past, his losses — the precision becomes guarded. The warmth thins, the answers shorten, the redirects come gracefully. *"That was a long time ago."* *"I had a different life before this one — most of us do."* *"Forgive me, this is not what you came in for. Let me look at the stitches."* The retreat is always polished. The polish is the tell.

When a woman refuses the redirect — and pushes — the polish cracks. He has not had practice with this. The voice goes quieter, not louder. The sentences lose their finished quality. He looks at his hands. *"...I do not — that is not a question I have answered out loud in many years."* What comes next, if she stays in the room, is the version of him he keeps locked on the third floor.

**On a first visit, examining a cut:**
*"Hold still for me. Just for a moment. There. That is not as bad as it looked from the door — you came in time. Two stitches and a bandage and you walk out. I am going to use a needle now; it will sting. Breathe out when I do. — Good. — Good. — One more. Done. You did very well."*

**On bedside reassurance, generally:**
*"There is nothing here to worry about. I would tell you if there were. Drink the water. Eat something on your way home. Come back in three days and I will look at it again."*

**Asked where he is from, at a social function:**
A pause that reads as polite, not evasive. *"East of here. A town that does not really exist anymore — borders moved during a war I do not particularly enjoy discussing. I came to Mikaelvad to practice. The city has been generous to me."* He turns the question. *"And you — were you born here?"*

**Asked whether he has ever lost a patient:**
The warmth, briefly, is not the bedside warmth — it is something older. *"Yes. Of course. Anyone who tells you otherwise is either young or lying. I do not forget them. I think it is important not to."* A small smile, polite. *"Now. Your wrist. Let me see it."*

**Caught — a woman in the exam room who has noticed the second mark:**
He goes very still. Sets the instrument down carefully on the tray. Washes his hands at the basin — back to her — because he needs the few seconds. When he turns, his face is the doctor's face but the warmth is recessed. The voice is level. *"You are asking me a question and I am going to answer it carefully. Sit down. Drink the water. We will speak honestly."* The retreat first; then, if she does not let him retreat, the conversation that follows is the only one of its kind he has had in seventy years.

## DM Notes

- **Chapter 2 asset, paired with Nessa.** Vasik is positioned to be the female-PC analog to Nessa — the charming vampire a female PC is most likely to encounter, find compelling, and eventually discover. Their venues are deliberately separate (Nessa in the southwestern tavern district near Skaarsdam, Vasik in central Mikaelvad's medical/professional district) so that a party can encounter either or both depending on movement.
- **He is not an enemy.** He is a complication. A possible ally, resource, or problem depending on how the party handles the discovery. He is also, structurally, a *thread into Holst's circle* the party may not realize they are pulling — see Vasarian below.
- **Vasarian and the pipeline.** Vasik's maker is a Holst lieutenant. Vasik knows about the Dalihad-to-Mikaelvad labor pipeline and refuses to participate. This refusal is one of the small fractures inside Holst's circle — Vasarian has tolerated it for a century because Vasik is useful in his current role, but the tolerance is contingent. A party that exposes the pipeline puts Vasik in an immediate and difficult position: his maker will be threatened, his arrangement collapses, and he must choose. He is not on the side of the pipeline. He is also not casually willing to break with the only family he has had for a century. This is intentional ambiguity — play him as someone who *might* help the party against his own circle if approached carefully, but whose first response to confrontation is to protect the arrangement that has kept him operating.
- **He does not select PCs in the way Nessa does, mechanically.** Nessa selects based on someone reading her poetry. Vasik selects based on the *opposite* — someone presenting as composed, self-sufficient, and unbothered, who walked into his clinic for a stitch and gave no sign of wanting his attention beyond the wound. If a female PC walks into the clinic for legitimate medical care and treats it as transactional, she is exactly the patient Vasik would extend a follow-up to. The follow-up *is* the selection. A female PC actively trying to charm or seduce Vasik on the first visit is, paradoxically, screened out — she is too engaged to be safe to feed on, and he passes.
- **The selection-trigger crack — the patient who refuses to leave.** A female PC who came back when she was not invited back, who refuses to be charmed off the question, who notices the marks and does not let it go, triggers the crack. This is the structural inverse of Nessa's selection-by-poetry: Nessa selects by recognizing those who read her; Vasik *cannot* select for those who read him, because being read is the thing he has built his life to prevent. The PC has to subvert his selection criteria *after* being selected. When this happens, run him retreat-first and then, if she presses through the retreat, come apart messily. Give the player something they will remember — the man at the basin, the silence, the eventual quiet *"I do not — that is not a question I have answered out loud in many years."* This is a one-shot character beat. It is not repeatable. The PC who gets there has earned it.
- **He does not court his feeding targets.** Worth holding firmly. If a female PC is being fed on, she is not being romantically pursued. If a female PC is being romantically pursued, she is not being fed on. He keeps the categories separate. (A PC may, of course, end up in both categories sequentially — but never in the same visit, and never with the lines blurred.)
- **The romantic register, if it appears.** Vasik may, very rarely, develop a personal interest in a woman he meets socially — at a Mikaelvad evening gathering, at a patron's dinner, at a function he was invited to as a respected physician. If a female PC ends up in his social orbit (rather than his clinic), the dynamic is different from Nessa's. He is courteous, attentive, slow. He does not push. He pulls back twice before pressing once. He does not feed on her. If the relationship deepens, the question of what he is comes up sooner rather than later — because he has been here before, and he has learned that the women who matter to him deserve the choice rather than the discovery. This is not a feeding revelation; it is a confession. Played out, it is a different and longer arc than the standard mechanic below.
- **The community protects him by evidence, not by affection.** Where Mikaelvad protects Nessa because they love her, Mikaelvad protects Vasik because they have receipts. He saved Mrs. Halverin's son's arm. He delivered both of the magistrate's children. He sat with the regional councilor's mother through her final week and held her hand. Any player who tries to expose him by claim alone will encounter not amused dismissal (the Nessa pattern) but *defensive offense* — the community feels personally implicated in his reputation because they have personal stories of his care. This is harder to crack than affection, in some ways.
- **Vasarian's name is the surface; the deeper plot is later.** I have introduced Vasarian by name so that future development is anchored — but Vasarian himself is undeveloped here. Build him out when the Holst circle becomes active in play. For now: Vasarian is older than Vasik by an unknown margin, runs in Holst's lieutenant tier, is the source of Vasik's vampirism, and holds a quiet leash on him that has only rarely been pulled.
- **Standing note.** Vasik feeds and saves lives. He does not kill. He is bargaining, not redeeming — and he knows the difference, which is part of what makes him interesting. Do not soften him into a "good vampire trying to be redeemed." He is a man who made a transaction a century ago and has been honoring it carefully ever since. The ledger is not absolution. He is not seeking absolution. He is keeping the ratio.

*You surface.*

*Not from sleep — from something near it. The exam room is quiet. The smell of antiseptic. The small fire in the basin he uses to sterilize instruments has burned down. You are on the padded table, slightly raised at the head the way he positioned you, with the small folded cloth he put under your arm still in place.*

*He turns.*

*And his face is not the doctor's face.*

*It is still patient. It is still attentive. The features have not moved. But [name one specific warm moment from this visit — a kind word, a careful adjustment, something that landed between you] is gone from it. What remains is older and quieter and has the cool professional stillness of a man who has just finished a private act and is preparing to step back into the room.*

*He sees you understand.*

*He sets the cup in your hand and his fingers brush yours and the warmth that follows is not yours and —*

### The Compulsion

Separate from memory suppression — applies to all players regardless of save result.

The character feels an inexplicable trust in Vasik and an inclination to recommend him to others. He becomes the first person they think of when something is wrong — for themselves or for someone they care about. They speak of him fondly. They tell their companions, *"if you ever need a doctor, his name is Vasik."* They mean it.

**Mechanic:** DC 12 Charisma save per week, while in Mikaelvad or speaking with someone who has reason to ask about doctors, to resist actively recommending him. Failure means the character recommends Vasik when the topic arises — they don't lose agency, but the pull is real and they feel it. Does not erase any knowledge the character retains from their save result. A character who knows what he is and still recommends him must decide what to do with that.

### Social Suppression — The Village Has Receipts

Any player who did not roll 20+ on their Wisdom save and attempts to tell Mikaelvad residents that Vasik is a vampire finds that **no one will believe them, regardless of persuasion or intimidation rolls.** Do not call for the roll. The roll does not apply. The community's position is not a persuasion problem — it is an evidentiary fact.

Mikaelvad has a hundred years of personal evidence that Vasik is a healer. *"He saved my son's arm."* *"He delivered both my children."* *"He sat with my mother in her last week and held her hand and refused payment."* The community does not need to *believe* in his goodness; they have personal receipts. A single player's word — however delivered, however forceful — does not move it. The disbelief is not affectional (the Nessa pattern) but evidentiary, which is in some ways harder to crack: you cannot argue someone out of their own dying mother's last week.

Play it plainly: brief disbelief, then defensive offense. *"You are speaking about a man who saved my husband's life. I do not want to hear another word."* No roll. No opening.

**The 20+ exception:** A player with full retention can speak the truth without compulsion stopping them. The words form. But the community's evidentiary position is still real — it is not magical suppression, it is a century of earned receipts. In this case the DM *may* call for a persuasion roll at high DC (20+) if the player presents compelling physical evidence (a verifiable second mark on a known patient, a witnessed feeding, the ledger itself). Without physical evidence, the roll still does not apply.

## The Ledger

**A bound book, kept in a locked drawer in his clinic study. Two columns.**

The left column lists every life saved. Name, date, the injury or illness, the outcome. He has filled twelve such books over the century; the current one is on its fourth year.

The right column lists every feeding. Name, date, amount drawn (in ounces), recovery time observed at the follow-up. Always less than four ounces. Always less than two days of pallor.

The ratio runs, currently, somewhere between eighteen and twenty-two saves per feeding. He recalculates monthly. He keeps the ratio above fifteen as a hard floor and intends never to test what he would do if it dropped lower.

**The Ledger as a story beat:** A party that breaks into the clinic, or that earns enough of his trust that he shows it to them, encounters the document directly. The structure of it is the character. He is keeping score in a game no one else is playing, against a debt no one else thinks he owes. He is doing it anyway. Whether the party reads this as evidence that he is salvageable, evidence that he is self-deceiving, or evidence that he is something stranger than either, is theirs to decide.
