## Overview

Halfling Cleric/Warlock multiclass. "Belmita Goodfeet" is an assumed identity — true name Diada Hearthare. Has a Warlock pact with Coretha, daughter of Leira (Lady of Deception), which the party does not know. Her Cleric/Warlock multiclass reads as divine magic to outside observers; the warlock origin is concealed. Carries the Eyeball Staff. Raven familiar named Beaky. Wears Moon Amulet (some sort of metal) concealed.

## Background

**True identity:** Diada Hearthare. The Goodfeet surname was stolen — she has no blood connection to the clan.

**How she came to join the expedition:** She infiltrated the Charnelhold in Setland under a noble disguise. While socializing in the Lion's Den (the Duke's general assembly chamber), she overheard Quellenna Ilphekiir privately discussing the Aestrum expedition with a royal confidant. Quellenna mentioned reaching out to "the Black Arrows" for special assistance — said with uncharacteristic excitement, notable given Quellenna's usual affect. Belmita saw Quellenna as a significant power player and joined the expedition to gain favor with her — an "in" for confidence game purposes. Her goal was never the taxes.

She found the expedition bounty on public message boards and scheduled her appointment. Her background research on Aestrum turned up only wild rumors (undying lich in the eastern mountains, cannibal denizens) — the consistent thread being that people who venture too far don't return.

## DM Notes

**Warlock Pact — Coretha:**
Pact with Coretha, daughter of Leira (Lady of Deception). Party does not know.

**Pact terms (verbatim essence):** *"I will tether you to my magical font, and through me you may cast spells. You will allow me to have some fun in that exquisite little brain of yours. The deal lasts until you tell me you don't want my power."* The exit clause is the trap: Belmita can end it at any time by renouncing the power. She will not. She knows what is being done to her, she knows why, she knows the cost — and she will not give up the spells. Coretha designed the pact knowing this. The awareness is the point.

**The cycle Coretha feeds on:** Coretha tricks or tortures Belmita. Belmita pushes back, endures, refuses to yield. But she cannot renounce the power. Coretha feeds on the internal conflict — the knowing refusal, the dignity maintained while the mind slowly unravels — and begins again. Belmita's particular torment is that the version of herself Coretha most enjoys is the one that keeps fighting.

**Layers of psychological damage (inherent / by design):**
1. **Coretha's baseline interference:** Constant, oppressive, white noise. Shadows move in directions their sources do not. Written words are illegible to her — jumbled and impossible to read — until she shows them to someone else, at which point the letters resolve perfectly. She is slowly losing her mind, she knows it, and she cannot stop it.
2. **The touch-trigger — Coretha's deliberate fabrication:** Coretha fires a psychic spike (1d6 psychic damage) whenever Belmita is touched by any entity carrying significant Shar metaphysical energy. Coretha's goal is isolation — to draw Belmita away from the party and further inward.

A further, **play-acquired** layer — the Antechamber trauma (a discrete wound from being pulled into Shar's Antechamber during the campaign) — together with the *current* touch-trigger targets and Belmita's false inference about their source, is playthrough state and lives in the saved overlay (`{{PROJECT_ROOT}}/campaign_state/<C>/party/saved/belmita_goodfeet.md`).

**The Eyeball Staff:** Always feels as though it is watching her. When she looks at it directly, the eye is looking elsewhere. She has asked the party whether they feel watched by it; none do. The staff feels as though it is pulling something out of her — not painfully, but persistently. She keeps it close regardless. When she wakes from nightly nightmares, she is always clutching the staff. She does not remember reaching for it.

**Baseline experience:** Hissing whispers run under everything. She is nearly always somewhere else when conversations begin — lost in the current madness Coretha is running — and is surprised by being addressed. She surfaces, reorients, responds. When the conversation ends, she sinks back in.

**Font level as sanity meter:** The fuller Coretha's font, the thicker her connection to Belmita — more interference, more tricks, more noise. As Belmita spends spell slots and the font runs thin, Coretha's presence recedes. A fully depleted Belmita is as close to sane as she gets. A fully rested Belmita has Coretha right behind her eyes. She is aware of this dynamic and cannot escape it.

**The Delmuir Goodfeet exposure risk:** Two detonators exist for revealing her identity theft:
1. **Roleplay:** A direct encounter with Delmuir Goodfeet (an actual clan member, third-born son of the Duke of Beluir, currently in Duskwall).
2. **Mechanical:** If Sage's Bloodline Compass is used to locate Delmuir using the Goodfeet surname, it will not register Belmita as a blood relative — mechanically exposing her identity theft.

**Shar's Debt disc — Belmita's response:** She immediately senses Shar in the disc — not analysis, pure trauma response. She screams and sprints. Because she is already inside the Silence radius when this happens, her scream reaches the rest of the party muffled and distant, as though she is calling from another room while standing next to them. If she stumbles — by the pull, terrain, or the neural spike firing as she moves — Coretha's voice breaks through for the first time in years: *"LET GO!"* The ambiguity is intentional and should not be resolved for the player: let go of what? Fear? The staff? The party?

**Moon Amulet status:** Belmita is wearing her Moon Amulet, concealed, and is protected from the Modify Memory effect of the daily reset. Protection is **per-wearer** — each amulet protects only its own attuned wearer (see [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/items/chapter_1/moon_amulets.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/items/chapter_1/moon_amulets.md)). Belmita's amulet protects Belmita and no one else; if she leaves, dies, or loses it, no other party member's reset-protection is affected. (Theren also wears one, independently protecting Theren.)

## Chapter 1 Departure

**Status:** Planned. Belmita's player is leaving the campaign at the close of what is currently Chapter 1. This section stages her exit. It is built entirely from threads already seeded above — the Coretha isolation arc, the font-as-sanity meter, the Eyeball Staff's "pulling," and the `"LET GO!"` ambiguity — so the departure reads as payoff, not as a beat grafted on at the end.

**Throughline (all branches):** Coretha's pact has been an isolation campaign from the start — the engineered touch-trigger, the stated goal to "draw Belmita away from the party and further inward," the unresolved `"LET GO!"`. **Belmita leaving is Coretha's campaign succeeding.** Whichever branch fires, frame the exit as the culmination of that arc, not as an unrelated event.

**The pivot — the Hati shrine fight.** Stage the departure at the Duskwall-anchor climax (Hati Heldrivver, [`{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/hati_heldrivver.md`]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/hati_heldrivver.md)). Hati is calibrated as a hurdle, not a TPK — the DM is meant to engineer *her* failure through dice and the open-threshold geometry rather than softening her play. Which departure branch is in play depends on whether that safety valve holds.

### Branch A — Sacrifice (ONLY if the Hati fight goes sideways)

The player has expressed she'd like Belmita to die saving the party. Gate it: this branch fires **only** if the Hati fight turns genuinely lethal — the dice-and-geometry valve fails and the party faces a real wipe. Under normal/winning conditions, do **not** invoke this branch; use B or C.

When it fires, Belmita *is* the out — instead of the room saving them, she does:
- **Font-as-sanity payoff:** a fully *depleted* Belmita is "as close to sane as she gets." The moment she dumps every remaining slot to save the party is the one moment Coretha is gone from behind her eyes. She spends her hard-won clarity *on them, freely* — the version of herself Coretha most enjoys (the one that keeps fighting) chooses, for once, an ending Coretha cannot feed on.
- **Eyeball Staff payoff:** the staff has been "pulling something out of her... persistently" all campaign. Her death can be the harvest paying out — she overchannels whatever it has been gathering into one final burst. The staff finishes what it started.
- This is a second valid resolution of Hati's "engineer the conditions of failure" note: a PC's choice rather than a low roll. Hati's own play does **not** soften.

### Branch B — Coretha claims her (default-dark; fight resolves without a death)

No death needed. At or just after the climax, the isolation completes: `"LET GO!"` resolves *toward the pact, away from the party*. Belmita withdraws into somewhere they won't follow — the shrine alcove, a Void-adjacent threshold, or simply **gone one morning, no goodbye.** The dark payoff of the seeded arc: she isn't killed, she's *taken inward.* Leaves a live horror-thread for a possible later return.

### Branch C — Quiet walk-away (default-light)

Her real goal was never the expedition — it was favor with Quellenna Ilphekiir for a confidence game. If that's spent or soured, and with **Delmuir Goodfeet** sitting in Duskwall as a live identity-theft detonator (see her exposure-risk note above), the rational grifter cuts and runs before exposure. Lowest drama; door left open; Coretha unresolved and fully portable to a future return.

### Loose ends to resolve at the table (any branch)

- **The Eyeball Staff** (her only attuned item). In Branch A it has a built-in exit (consumed/spent). In B or C, decide: does it leave *with* her, or stay behind — still watching, now with no anchor to pull on? An ownerless Eyeball Staff in party hands is its own hook.
- **Coretha** survives Belmita in B and C. The pact-holder is now a loose antagonist thread with a known face and a known cruelty pattern — available for re-use.
- **Reset protection is NOT a concern.** Per the per-wearer rule above, Belmita's departure does not weaken anyone else's amulet protection. Do not play her exit as making the party vulnerable to the revert.
