# Connectable Characters — Authoring Rule

Authoring guidance for **characters built to share scenes with the protagonist over
time** — a companion, a recurring foil, anyone the story wants the protagonist able
to grow close to. This file is about how to *design* one so that closeness is
reachable; how attachment is run in play is the consuming work's business.

The principle: **a connectable character must be authorable so that the protagonist
can bond with them at minimal effort.** A character who is hard to connect with is
present for nothing but their place in the scene — a wasted role. The protagonist
should be able to earn at least **camaraderie** (the bond of shared scenes and shared
hardship) with near-zero deliberate effort. Romance or deeper intimacy is optional and
never required; camaraderie is the floor.

---

## Design Flaws That Complicate Bonds, Not Prevent Them

A connectable character's flaws should bite at the **depth and expression** of a
relationship, not at its **formation**. Surface warmth should be cheap; vulnerability
and depth are what get earned over time.

The test: imagine a protagonist who does the small, ordinary things — shares a fire,
stands beside them in trouble, makes a low-effort friendly overture. If the
character's flaws mean the protagonist gets *nothing* — no warmth, no rapport, no
foothold — the flaws are **isolating**, and the character is miswired. Redesign.

**Isolating-direction flaws** (avoid as a connectable character's defining set):
- Distrusts all closeness; reads every kindness as a threat or a maneuver.
- Holds everyone at categorical distance; no one becomes real to them.
- Cold dismissal of others' feeling as weakness.
- Withdraws from contact as a default reflex.

A character can *have* one of these as a deep, secondary wound — but if the whole flaw
set points this way, the protagonist cannot get in, and the character fails its job.

**Warm-direction flaws** (the connectable shape):
- Bonds easily but can't go deep on purpose — deflects sincerity, so camaraderie is
  free and vulnerability is the hard part.
- Over-invests once attached — too loyal, too protective, poor boundaries.
- Expresses care in ways that overshoot or cause trouble.
- Misreads what a connection is — quick to bond, prone to mistaking its nature.
- Connects through structure / shared work and frets when it's absent.

Each of these gives the protagonist a fast foothold and reserves the *difficulty* for
the relationship's interior. That is the target shape.

**Direct the armor outward.** If a character has a hard, defended interior (a common
and good trait), point that armor at their *problems, enemies, or past* — not at the
protagonist. The seal that keeps a survivor functional, the control a wounded person
clings to: aim these at the world, so the protagonist still gets a way in.

---

## Coverage

A connectable character should be able to connect — at the camaraderie level or
deeper — with a **broad span of plausible protagonists**, not just one archetype.
Author their warmth and their interests so that at least half the plausible
protagonists can find a foothold with them. Narrow compatibility (clicks with exactly
one personality type) is the same failure as isolating flaws, one step removed.

Reference the consuming work's relationship conventions for how interest and
reciprocation are run in play; this file only governs that the character is *built* to
be reachable.
