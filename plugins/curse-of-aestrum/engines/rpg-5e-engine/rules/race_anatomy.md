# Race Anatomy Reference — D&D 5e

Recall each race's distinguishing features from canon using innate knowledge, then ensure
the character's appearance section names every feature (with a specific choice in range)
or flags a deliberate departure. **Silent omission is the failure mode this file exists to
prevent** — not lack of knowledge, which the builder already has.

The per-race notes below are **not an exhaustive gate.** They are reminders of the features
prose most often drops, plus this setting's depth conventions and departures. A race not
listed here is subject to the same discipline — build it from canon knowledge. Pin a new
entry only when a setting departure, an unusual depth need, or cross-session consistency
warrants it.

---

## Tiefling

The most common "easy to under-describe" race in 5e prose. Tieflings carry several
features beyond a human baseline; all of the following must be addressed in any tiefling
character's appearance section.

- **Horns.** Location and shape vary by lineage and individual. Typical positions:
  forehead, temples, swept-back along the line of the skull, curling forward, or curling
  backward. Shape may be smooth or ridged. Length ranges from small temple-points
  (an inch or two) to a full ram-curl (a foot or more along the curl). Color usually
  dark — black, charcoal, deep red, deep violet — sometimes matching the skin gradient.
  Specify position, shape, length, ridging, and color.
- **Tail.** **Anatomically attached where a human's tailbone terminates the spine.**
  Length, diameter, taper, tip shape, prehensility, sensitivity, and behavioral use
  pattern all vary by lineage and individual.
  - *Length.* Commonly 2–5 feet from base to tip, but variance is wide. Some lineages
    have shorter (1–2 ft) tails; some longer (5+ ft). Specify both total length and
    where the tip falls when allowed to hang naturally (e.g., "tip rests on the floor,"
    "tip reaches just short of the ground," "tip ends at mid-calf").
  - *Diameter and taper.* Specify diameter at the base and how it narrows along the
    length. Slender tails (1 inch at base) and thicker tails (2–3 inches at base) both
    occur; the tail almost always narrows to a finer point.
  - *Tip shape.* Common shapes: triangular point (most common), diamond-shaped spear
    tip, spade tip, blunt rounded tip, forked. Specify.
  - *Prehensility.* Most tiefling tails are prehensile in the sense that they have
    voluntary muscular control along the full length. *Dexterity* is a separate axis —
    most cannot manipulate small objects or hold things; the tail is closer to an
    expressive limb than a fifth hand. Specify both axes.
  - *Sensitivity.* The tip is commonly as sensitive as a fingertip; in some lineages
    more so. Specify whether the character finds tip-contact pleasurable, ticklish,
    distracting, or neutral. Specify whether they have habits around what the tip is
    allowed to touch.
  - *Behavioral use pattern.* How does the character habitually use the tail? Wrapped
    around a chair leg when sitting? Curled around their own ankle when standing?
    Coiled at the small of the back under clothing? Used as an attraction in
    courtesan / performance work? Reserved for intimate moments? Most tieflings have
    one or more characteristic habits; specify them.
- **Eyes.** Tiefling eyes are commonly solid-color — gold, red, silver, copper, violet,
  black, white — rather than the human iris-and-pupil pattern. Some lineages have
  flecked or two-tone irises (e.g., dark with gold flecks). Many tiefling eyes reflect
  light at low light levels the way an animal's do. Specify color, whether solid or
  flecked, and whether they catch light.
- **Teeth.** Often slightly pointed canines; some lineages have full predatory dentition.
  Specify whether dentition is visibly non-human.
- **Skin.** Any color the lineage allows — red, blue, violet, charcoal, gold, ivory.
  Often warmer to the touch than human by several degrees. May show gradient (one color
  to another across the body) or mottled patterns. Specify color, gradient if any,
  whether warm-to-touch.
- **Hair.** Any color, including non-human ranges (blue, violet, deep red). Often coarser
  or finer than human; some lineages have hair that does not silver with age.
- **Feet / hooves.** Most tiefling lineages have human-form feet. Some (Levistus, Glasya
  in some sources) have cloven hooves; some have clawed toes; some have feet that look
  human but with a slight asymmetry (longer second toe, joint-pad calluses). Specify
  unless human-form.
- **Scent.** Some lineages carry a faint distinctive scent — sulfur, rose, ash,
  petrichor, smoke. Most tieflings do not. Specify if present.

**Hard requirement:** every tiefling character must address horns, tail, eyes, and skin.
Teeth, hooves, and scent must be addressed if non-baseline; may be omitted only if
explicitly noted as human-baseline in those features.

## Dragonborn

- **Scales.** Color and pattern match draconic lineage. Specify color, pattern, distribution
  (which parts of the body are scaled vs. softer-skinned).
- **No tail.** D&D 5e dragonborn are anatomically tailless. (This is the most common
  point of confusion when running them in prose. If you want a tailed dragonborn it is
  a deliberate world-departure and must be flagged.)
- **Breath weapon vent.** Located in the throat. Most dragonborn show some visible
  feature here — a darker patch, a faint ridge, a vent-like opening that closes when not
  in use.
- **Claws.** Light claws on hands and feet — not predatory length, but not human nails.
- **Teeth.** Multiple rows of pointed teeth.
- **Tail.** None. (Repeated for clarity. Do not narrate dragonborn with tails.)
- **Eyes.** Often draconic — slit pupils, color matching scale lineage.
- **Skin / sub-scale.** Where scales thin (inner forearm, throat, palms), the skin
  beneath is visible and is usually a paler version of the scale color.

## Tabaxi

- **Full feline tail.** Length 2–3 feet, expressive, used for balance and signaling.
  Prehensility varies — official 5e is non-prehensile, but campaign variance is allowed.
  Specify.
- **Fur.** Covers the whole body, including face. Color and pattern vary widely.
- **Retractable claws** on hands and feet.
- **Pad-footed gait.** Officially plantigrade in 5e; commonly reskinned as digitigrade.
  Specify.
- **Eyes.** Slit pupils, often gold or green.
- **Teeth.** Feline canines.
- **Whiskers.** Present at the cheeks and above the eyes.

## Aasimar

Three lineages with substantially different physiology.

- **All lineages.** Eyes glow during use of Celestial Revelation (color depends on
  lineage). May show silvery markings under the skin in low light. Slightly taller and
  more symmetric than human baseline.
- **Protector.** Radiant wings manifest during Celestial Revelation. Otherwise human-form.
- **Scourge.** No wings. Body shows faint visible glow during Celestial Revelation;
  this glow burns the aasimar themselves and any close adjacent creatures.
- **Fallen.** Bony, often featherless wings during Celestial Revelation. Skin tone may
  be ashen.

## Genasi

Lineage marks vary by element. All genasi show their element visibly in some way.

- **Air.** Hair stirred by a wind only it feels; pale lightning-line marks on the skin
  or pale blue gradient. Voice carries faintly.
- **Earth.** Crystalline ridges along forearms or temples; skin patterned like stone
  or earth.
- **Fire.** Flame eyes (irises that flicker), ash or coal-toned skin, hair that smolders
  at the tips or moves like fire in low wind.
- **Water.** Faint gill-slits at the neck or sides of the ribs (non-functional in air);
  fin-like ridges along forearms; skin tone teal-to-sea-green.

## Dwarf

- **Build.** Squarer and denser than human; same height range as a halfling-to-short-human
  but with significantly more mass.
- **Beards / facial hair.** Common across all sexes in most dwarven traditions, though
  campaign variance is allowed. Specify presence, length, color, style.
- **Eyes.** Often dark; mountain dwarves sometimes have gold or amber.
- **Ears.** Slightly long, slightly pointed at the tip in some sources; round in others.
- **Hands.** Larger relative to body than human; thicker fingers.

---

## Elf

- **Ears.** Pointed; length varies by subrace. Eladrin shortest, drow longest, wood
  elves intermediate. Specify length.
- **Build.** Slimmer and slightly taller than human baseline.
- **Eyes.** Often a single solid color (gold, silver, violet, green) or larger than
  human; lid shape often slightly different.
- **Facial hair.** Typically absent.
- **Subrace-specific.**
  - *Wood elf.* Coppery or green-tinted skin tones; hair often copper, blonde, brown.
  - *High / Sun elf.* Coppery skin, gold or silver hair.
  - *Moon elf.* Paler, often blue-tinted skin; silver-white hair.
  - *Drow.* Obsidian-to-charcoal skin; white, silver, or pale lavender hair; red,
    violet, or silver eyes; the longest pointed ears among elven subraces.
  - *Eladrin.* Skin tone shifts with seasonal disposition (autumn coppery, winter pale,
    spring rosy, summer golden); ears shortest of the elves.

## Half-Elf

- **Ears.** Slightly pointed; less pronounced than full elf.
- **Build.** Between human and elf — closer to one parent than the other depending on
  lineage.
- **Eyes.** Either parent's range possible.

---

## Halfling

- **Build.** 3–4 feet tall, proportionally compact.
- **Feet.** Often calloused soles capable of going barefoot in most environments;
  the tops of the feet have curly hair in some traditions, smooth in others.
- **Ears.** Slightly pointed in some sources, rounded in others.
- **Hair.** Curly common.

---

## Human

Baseline — no extra-human features. Specify build, coloration, distinctive features
(scars, tattoos, missing limbs, etc.) as normal.

---

## Half-Orc

- **Tusks.** Lower-jaw, protruding past the upper lip. Length varies. Specify.
- **Skin.** Greenish or gray tones.
- **Brow.** Sloped, heavier than human.
- **Ears.** Small, often slightly pointed.
- **Build.** Taller and heavier than human baseline.

---

## Drow

(See Elf → Drow.) Add:

- **Sun sensitivity.** Daylight is uncomfortable. May show squinting, hood-up
  behavior, or visible discomfort in bright light unless campaign-flagged otherwise.
- **Hearing.** Sharper than human or surface elf.

---

## Gnome

- **Build.** 3–4 feet tall.
- **Ears.** Pointed, smaller than elf.
- **Eyes.** Often dark; rock gnomes sometimes have unusual colors (gold, copper).
- **Subrace-specific.**
  - *Forest gnome.* Skin sometimes green-tinted; hair often woodland-toned.
  - *Rock gnome.* Tan or earth-toned skin.
  - *Deep gnome (svirfneblin).* Gray-to-brown skin; bald or sparse hair.

---

## Goliath

- **Lithoderms.** Bony, lichen-toned patches of skin across the body. Pattern varies.
- **Skin.** Pale gray, sometimes with darker mottling.
- **Build.** 7–8 feet tall, heavy.
- **Hair.** Bald or sparse; mountain-clan markings are common as tattoos.

---

## Firbolg

- **Build.** 7–8 feet tall.
- **Ears.** Long, often pointed forward or to the side; bovine-influenced shape.
- **Skin.** Pink, cream, or pale blue.
- **Features.** Broad nose; soft facial structure; sometimes minor bovine influence
  (slight muzzle, eye placement).

## Races Not Listed Above

A race without a section here is **not a gap.** Recall its anatomy from canon (kenku,
lizardfolk, tortle, kobold, goblin / hobgoblin / bugbear, yuan-ti, owlin, harengon,
leonin, satyr, etc.) and apply the same name-or-flag discipline. **Pin a section here
only when** the setting departs from that race's baseline, the campaign wants unusual
depth on a feature (as with the tiefling tail), or you want to lock ranges for
cross-session consistency. When you do pin one, cover at minimum: overall body shape,
distinguishing limbs (tail, wings, extra arms), claws / teeth / horns, distinctive
sensory organs, coloration ranges, and "the thing the prose tends to forget."

## Rule of Use

For **every** character, recall the distinguishing features of their race from canon —
whether or not the race appears above — and ensure each is either:

1. **Named in the appearance section** of the character file, with the character's
   specific choice within (or near) the canonical range, *or*
2. **Explicitly flagged** as a deliberate world-departure in the character's frontmatter
   or appearance section, with a brief reason (e.g., `feet: cloven hooves — Glasya
   lineage` or `tail: none — this character was born tailless, the village considered it
   an omen and called her *the unmarked*`).

For a race that *does* appear above, the listed features are the checklist (the entry
captures this setting's depth and departures). For a race that does not, your canon recall
is the checklist. Either way, silent omission of a distinguishing feature is the failure
mode this file exists to prevent.

Once a specific character's choices are set, they are pinned on **that character's sheet** —
the durable, per-character consistency anchor. The general per-race notes here are
guidance, not the per-character record.

When auditing existing characters retroactively, the same rule applies — the audit pass
should produce either an updated appearance section or an explicit departure flag for
every missing feature.
