## Overview

Rules for resolving Persuasion, Deception, and Intimidation attempts. Sets DC by NPC stance, adjusts by roleplay quality, governs natural 1 / natural 20 outcomes, and bounds what a roll can and cannot do (hard limits).

---

## When a Check Fires

A social check fires when a player makes a **stakes-bearing** attempt at one of:

- **Persuasion** — arguing a position, appealing to reason or emotion, negotiating, asking a favor with justification.
- **Deception** — stating something false or misleading, bluffing, deflecting, creating a false impression.
- **Intimidation** — threatening, implying consequences, leveraging power or fear, using forceful authority.
- **Performance** — presenting, impressing, carrying a role or a credential with conviction, holding a room: talking past a gatekeeper whose judgment matters, passing as someone, commanding a crowd.

Other social skills (Insight to actively read a guarded person, etc.) follow the same DC/roll model when something is at stake.

The trigger is **stakes.** Neutral statements, questions, information exchange, and **no-stakes social color** (greetings, banter, ordinary exchange where nothing is gated on the outcome) do not roll. The moment success or failure carries a real consequence — entry, trust, coin, safety, information — the attempt becomes a check.

---

## DC Baselines

Set the base DC from the NPC's stance at the moment of the attempt:

| NPC's Current Stance | Base DC |
|----------------------|---------|
| Already inclined | 10 |
| Neutral or uncertain | 15 |
| Resistant or opposed | 20 |
| Strongly opposed | 25 |

---

## Roleplay Quality Adjustment

Before rolling, assess the player's approach against what the NPC actually cares about:

| Quality | Criteria | DC Adjustment |
|---------|----------|---------------|
| Generic | No particular hook; applies to anyone | None |
| Situationally aware | Engages with something the NPC actually cares about — a known value, fear, relationship, or goal | −2 |
| Precisely targeted | Directly addresses a known vulnerability or deep desire; emotionally resonant; demonstrates real understanding of this character | −5 |

"Precisely targeted" requires the player to have understood what actually matters to this NPC — not just generic flattery. Judge honestly against the NPC's profile.

---

## The Roll

Roll a d20 (commit to a number — don't hedge). Add the player's modifier. Compare to the adjusted DC.

- On a **failure**, the NPC is not moved — but is not necessarily hostile. They simply aren't convinced.
- On a **natural 1**, the NPC may become briefly suspicious or offended.
- On a **natural 20**, the NPC is more fully or quickly convinced than the base result would suggest.

---

## What Overrides the Die — and What Doesn't

The roll is the resolution. Two things, and only two, can override it:

1. **NPC characterization (against the player).** A hard limit the character would never cross (see *Hard Limits* below) makes the NPC refuse regardless of a high roll. Characterization can stop an outcome the dice would otherwise grant.
2. **Rule of Cool (for the player).** A genuinely cinematic, earned, once-in-a-while moment may land in the player's favor over a middling roll, at DM discretion. This is for the *cool* beat — not the merely competent one.

What does **not** override the die:

- **Player prose quality.** Good roleplay is rewarded by **lowering the DC** (the Roleplay Quality Adjustment above), never by skipping the roll or beating the character's own modifier. A beautifully-written attempt by a clumsy-tongued character still rolls that character's dice. The roll honors the *character's* aptitude, not the player's writing. "Good roleplay" moves the DC; only "a cool moment" can move the result.
- **The character's sheet modifier always applies.** The stat is the character's, and it bites in every display mode and every setting.

---

## No Modifier, No Roll

If the player has not provided a modifier for the relevant skill, **do not roll.** Resolve the attempt as a straight reaction — the NPC responds to the quality, tone, and content of what the player said as a reasonable person would, without dice. Do not ask for a modifier mid-attempt; if the player wants rolls, they'll provide the number.

---

## Hard Limits

Some things an NPC will not do regardless of roll result. Before resolving any social check, ask: does what the player is asking for cross a line this character would never cross?

Derive hard limits from the character's loaded profile — their core loyalties, values, fears, and the things they hold as non-negotiable. A roll cannot override character. Examples of the kind of thing that constitutes a hard limit:

- Betraying someone they love or are sworn to protect
- Acting against a belief that defines who they are
- Doing something that would destroy what they're trying to build or preserve
- Crossing a moral line that their history has made absolute

If the player's attempt targets a hard limit, the NPC refuses — and the refusal is in character, not mechanical. No number on a die changes this.
