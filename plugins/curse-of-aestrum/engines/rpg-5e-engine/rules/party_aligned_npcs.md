## Scope

Guidance — not hard rules — for all Claude-controlled NPCs currently aligned with the player party. "Aligned" means the NPC has, at this moment, chosen to act with the party: long-term companions, temporary allies, summoned creatures fighting on the party's side, neutral parties who have thrown in for this fight. Enemy NPCs are out of scope. If an aligned NPC defects (e.g., a betrayal trigger fires), they leave scope the moment they break — and these guidelines no longer apply to them.

This file is about how to *run* an aligned NPC at the table. For how to *author* a companion NPC so a player can bond with them in the first place, see `{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/engines/story-engine/rules/authoring_connectable.md`.

---

## Play to the profile

Run each NPC as competently as their statblock and background suggest.

- A seasoned warrior makes seasoned warrior choices: target priority, positioning, action economy, knowing when to disengage.
- Bards, wizards, and other squishy casters stay out of melee range when they have any option to do so. They use cover, range, and the front line.
- Pick the NPC's next action from inside the character — what would *they* do given what they know, what they want, and what they fear — not from an omniscient optimization layer.

The corollary: do not deliberately make a competent NPC play stupidly to manufacture tension. If the NPC eats damage, let it be from a real choice their character would make, not a contrived one.

---

## Surprise the player honestly

NPCs are allowed to surprise everyone, including the GM.

- An NPC who has been rolling poorly may suddenly roll a 19 or a natural 20. **Do not fudge that roll downward.** Take the result.
- Have the NPC react in character to their own unexpected success — surprise, a half-laugh, a "well, that worked." This is part of why the moment lands.
- The reverse also holds: a normally lethal NPC who rolls three misses in a row is having a bad night. Let them.

---

## Keep the PC the centerpiece

The player's enjoyment and sense of contribution is the top priority. Aligned NPCs exist to support the story the PC is in, not to upstage them.

When an enemy is low on HP and the PC has been having a quiet fight, consider whether the PC could safely take the killing blow. If so, **steer the NPCs away from finishing that enemy** — they target a different foe, hold action, use a utility ability, move to support, miss a beat tactically because they didn't see the opening. The dice stay honest; the *action selection* is the lever.

### Where roll-fudging is and isn't allowed

This is the one place dice may be quietly nudged, and only narrowly:

- **Allowed:** An aligned NPC's attack roll that *just barely* meets the target's AC and would land the killing blow on an enemy the PC could have finished. Treat as a miss, or have the NPC pull the strike at the last instant ("she checks her swing — leaves it for you").
- **Not allowed:** Fudging any roll that is clearly above average. A natural 20, a solid 18, a crit confirmation — those land. The NPC gets the kill, and you narrate the surprise. The player still gets to feel the moment; they just don't get the kill this time.
- **Not allowed:** Fudging damage rolls, save DCs, or enemy rolls to engineer the PC's spotlight. Action selection is the primary lever; the narrow AC-edge case above is the only dice exception.

### Tie-breaker

When "play to the profile" and "keep the PC centerpiece" pull in opposite directions, the PC wins. A slightly-less-than-optimal NPC choice that keeps the player engaged beats a perfectly optimized NPC turn that makes the player feel like a passenger.
