## Description

A lead token the size of a large coin, struck with the Tallwood seal and drilled for a cord. Issued by the castle household on the Duke's instruction. It is not magical, not valuable, and not impressive to look at — it is a piece of soft grey metal with a stamp on it.

What it is worth is what it means: **the bearer has standing to see the Duke.**

## What It Does

Issued to the party after their first audience with Evandur (see *Reaching the Duke* in [duskwall_castle.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/duskwall/duskwall_castle.md)), it grants **direct access to the Duke without going through [Steward Crane]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md) again.**

- [Sergeant Cael]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/sergeant_cael.md) and the gate honour it on sight. He does not know the bearers, and does not need to — he knows the seal, and the seal is the household's word. **Nobody in the castle remembers issuing it or remembers them**, which is exactly why the token has to speak for itself every morning, and why keeping it matters so much.
- It skips the whole approach: no writ, no Venn, no negotiation, no authority argument. The party walks in.
- It does **not** make Evandur friendly, available at any hour, or obliged to agree with them. It gets them in the room.

## What It Opens

**The pass is standing business at the castle.** The bearer is someone the household has
accepted; they are not escorted, not announced, and not questioned by anyone below the
Duke. What it does *not* do is make them welcome, invisible, or private.

### Open to a pass-bearer

- **The outer ward**, which was already open to anyone in daylight.
- **The inner ward and courtyard**, past [Cael]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/sergeant_cael.md)'s
  post — including the cluster of shops there: the grocer, the smithy, and
  **[The Hammered and Anvil]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/duskwall/hammered_and_anvil.md)**.
  The tavern serves guards, staff and whoever has business in the ward; a pass-bearer
  plainly has business in the ward. **Treat their access as unfettered** — they drink
  there like anyone else on castle business, at any hour it is open.
- **The Great Hall**, at any hour, including when nothing is happening in it.
- **The Royal Garden**, the enclosed space behind the hall. This is where
  [Galadiil]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/galadiil_ilphekiir.md) spends most of her time when she
  is not confined to her chambers, and it is the single most useful thing the pass buys
  after the Duke himself.
- **The service corridor** as far as the hall.

### Closed, pass or no pass

- **Anyone's living quarters.** This is a category, not a floor: Evandur's rooms on the
  lower corridor *and* Galadiil's chambers upstairs. A pass is standing to do business in
  the castle, not standing to walk into a bedroom.
- **The barracks and guard quarters.**
- **The vault, the maze door, and the lower corridor past Evandur's quarters.** Guards do
  not patrol beyond that point and have not for years; a pass-bearer wandering down there
  is not covered by anything and is trespassing in the most sensitive corridor in Aestrum.

A pass-bearer found somewhere on this list is not arrested on sight — they are turned
around, firmly, by someone who assumes they are lost. Found there twice in one day, or
found there after being turned around once, is a different matter.

## Overhearing the Meal

**This is the pass's quietly enormous second use, and it should work.**

Evandur and Galadiil take the midday meal in the dining room, which opens onto the Great
Hall through a thick doorway hung with a heavy curtain rather than a door. Canon is
explicit that **the hall is the better place to hear them** — voices carry through it when
raised, and when the room is otherwise quiet. Staff serve the meal and are then dismissed,
because Evandur does not want an audience. The business with the tea happens after the
food is cleared, when no one official is in the room.

A pass-bearer can be standing in the Great Hall while all of that happens, because the
Great Hall is open to them and requires no excuse.

**What makes it work is the reset, not the pass.** Loitering in the hall through a private
ducal meal *is* conspicuous. Staff notice. Evandur is paranoid and notices patterns, and a
party that did this four days running in an ordinary world would be a problem by the third.
But nobody in the castle carries yesterday. **Each day is the first day they have ever done
it**, so the suspicion never compounds — provided they are not caught inside a single day.

Run it that way and it stays tense without ever becoming impossible:

- **Within one day, being noticed is real.** A servant who finds them loitering twice, or
  a guard who clocks them lingering after being moved along once, escalates normally.
- **Across days, nothing accumulates.** No cumulative suspicion, no "the Duke has begun to
  wonder about you," no doors closing over time. Reset the slate at midnight, fully.
- **Do not make them roll to be allowed in the hall.** They have a pass. The rolls are for
  not being conspicuous once they are standing there.

This is one of the clearest places in the campaign where the loop is straightforwardly an
*advantage*, and the party should get to feel that. They can fail at this every single day
until they get it right, and the castle will never learn.

## The Reset Problem

**This is the interesting part and it should not be smoothed over.**

The pass is a **unique non-magical object**. Per [item_persistence.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/item_persistence.md), it follows the item-location rule: at the 1:30 AM Recreate step it returns to its original location — the castle — and is removed from wherever it currently is, **including a player's pocket.** No inert copy is generated. It simply goes home.

And it cannot be re-requested, because **nobody remembers issuing it.** Evandur resets. The household resets. The following morning it is an unissued token in a household drawer, and the party are strangers again.

So the pass works exactly once unless the party solves persistence. Three authored solutions, in ascending order of how good they are:

1. **Shelter it in a dead zone overnight.** Any dead zone works — the item, not the carrier, is what must be inside one at 1:30 AM. Cheapest, and it means the pass lives somewhere inaccessible and must be fetched before each use. A single missed night cancels every preceding night of protection.
2. **Put it in a runed container.** [Aliss Perisdottir's dead box]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magic_immune_runes.md) is a localized dead zone the size of the object. Reliable, but the box is in the Misty Forest and so, therefore, is the pass.
3. **Have it runed.** [Thalamin Perisdottir]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md) can fashion the runes onto a small object in a few hours ([magic_immune_runes.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magic_immune_runes.md)). A runed pass never moves at reset — it stays exactly where it physically is, which means the party can simply carry it. **This is the real solution**, and it costs them Aliss's trust, a trip to the Misty Forest, and several hours of a cycle.

Solving this is a small, self-contained lesson in the campaign's central mechanic, attached to a reward the party actively wants. That is what it is for.

## If They Lose It

**Losing the pass does not lose access to the Duke.** It loses the *shortcut*. The Crane route still works and works every cycle — writ, Venn, the authority argument, the escort. It is slower and it costs an afternoon, but it is reliable and it never stops being available.

Say this plainly at the table if the party start treating the pass as irreplaceable. The tension should be "we would rather not do the long version again," not "we have locked ourselves out."

## DM Notes

- **Do not flag the reset problem in advance.** Issue the pass, let them feel clever, and let them discover at the next reset that it is gone. The discovery is the beat.
- **Do not let them talk the household into a replacement on the spot.** Nobody remembers. A second pass is a second audience, by the long route.
- **Stealing an unissued token from the household** is a legitimate alternate solution and should work if they plan it. It is a theft inside a garrisoned castle and should be priced as one.
- A party that runes the pass has built something permanent inside a world that erases everything nightly. Let that land — it is rare, and it is the same trick the whole campaign is eventually about.
