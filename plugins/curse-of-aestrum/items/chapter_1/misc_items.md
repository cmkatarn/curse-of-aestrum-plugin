## An Abridged History of Aestrum

A work-in-progress authored by Duskwall book merchant Bari Khalid. Its chapters cover the Acolypyrrhic Battles (origin of dead zones), the Tallwood succession crisis (Heliconine, Lognan, Evandur), the Duchess Ilphekiir substitution, the Loadstone, and the Pendant of the Waking Hours.

## Dead Zone Map of Aestrum

A cartographic document purchased from The Salient Cartographer for 15 gold. Identifies three major dead zones: the Deckard Estate, the Nahamkate Desert area (where the Nahamkate Temple is located), and a third significant dead zone in the forest region. Numerous minor dead zone pockets scattered throughout. The party reasoned that Rowan Deckard is likely sheltering in the forest dead zone.

## Flame-Shaped Quartz Relic of Kossuth

A massive piece of tangerine quartz, intricately carved into the shape of a flickering flame, weighing approximately 25 pounds. Discovered within a hidden chamber behind a statue of Anubis in the Temple of Sathraza. Retrieved by Thag. Later taken to the ruined Temple of Kossuth south of Rockwood — when placed in the reliquary at noon, sunlight through the stained glass window and the quartz created a beam of light directing the party to where a priest had hidden a stolen relic of Akadi centuries ago.

## Gerta

A heavy, ornate two-handed maul discovered within the Castle Vault. Crafted from dark gray metal with a green sheen, the head sculpted to resemble two opposing ram's heads. One side inscribed with glowing Dwarven runes detailing the lineage of owners through generations; the other side bears the name "Gerta." Emits a faint magical aura.

## Greymist

A homebrew poison compound. Derived from combining three individually legitimate medicinal ingredients — a cooling agent used in gout salves, a respiratory sedative used in daily lung tonics, and a binding compound used as a salve base — in specific ratios. In combination and accumulated over months of daily administration, they degrade constitution and cognitive function in a presentation indistinguishable from accelerated aging or a wasting illness. Detection requires knowing what to look for.

Used to poison the King of Nortmunde over approximately two years, administered through his daily respiratory tonic by Councive Drethnal (a Shar cultist). The king suffered from two documented pre-existing conditions — gout and a chronic respiratory weakness — both of which produce fatigue and mental fog. Greymist's effects were entirely invisible inside those known illnesses.

## Hylea's Mead

A species of seaweed unique to the coastline near Duskwall. When pressed, it produces a sweet, honey-like sap. Until approximately twenty years ago it was a key ingredient in local mead production. Before that, used by midwives as a contraceptive. The party observed Duke Evandur Tallwood insisting Duchess Galadiil drink her tea before leaving the lunch table — combined with the manuscript's description of Hylea's Mead, the party suspects the Duke is using it to prevent an heir. Galadiil is fully aware and powerless to refuse.

## The Dundelver Safe

A combination-locked device stolen from Darwinnith Dundelver the First's bedroom. Features slides numbered 1–16 and a rod; incorrect combination attempts trigger an electric discharge (5-foot radius, 1d12 damage, CON save DC 16 — failed save renders affected appendage(s) unusable for 4 hours). Inside was the large uncut ruby that served as the key to the Nahamkate Temple puzzle room.

## The Loadstone

A magical item commissioned in 1458 by Alman Tallwood. Used at the Duskwall docks for moving cargo. A smooth, palm-sized, faintly warm dark stone. The name is a play on words — it carries a *load* from here to there. It is not a lodestone and has no magnetic properties.

**Ability:** Teleports any single item — no people, no creatures — to any point the wielder can see within a quarter mile. The item must fit in the wielder's hand or be touched. Activation is a single action. Range is gated by line of sight; solid obstructions block targeting.

**Charges:** Six per day, recharging at midnight on Aestrum's cycle.

**Restrictions:**
- Will not move living creatures, even small ones.
- Will not move attuned magic items off the body of their attuned wielder.
- The destination must be visible at the moment of activation.

Has been responsible for at least four accidental deaths over its operational history at the docks — cargo materializing into spaces that turned out to be occupied. Currently stolen and in possession of Delmuir Goodfeet, who attunes to and learns to use it fresh every morning (subjectively his "first day" with it — objectively, every day for ten years).

**Conversion:** The Loadstone's teleportation essence can be extracted and bound to protection items — *Shards of the Loadstone* grant immunity to the 4:30 AM Teleport step of the cycle. See [magical_item_conversion.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/magical_item_conversion.md).

## The Mounted Ruby

A very large uncut ruby set in a golden mounting piece, kept in a magical lockbox at the Dundelver Estate. It is the "key" to the Nahamkate Temple gem-puzzle: set on the pedestal among the other gemstones, it completes the sequence that opens the secret passage to the hidden inner chamber. The chamber beyond holds further mounted gemstones.

## Statue of Selûne

A pristine, life-sized stone statue of the goddess Selûne found submerged at the bottom of the Moonmaiden Falls pond near Dunleaven. Removed from the Duskwall Shrine of Selûne by Hati Heldrivver when she desecrated the shrine, and deliberately dumped here — a pointed act of contempt. Jiasha noted its displacement is highly unusual and a sign of Selûne's diminished power in the region.

## Statue of Shar

A life-sized statue of dark basalt found hidden inside the Shrine of Selûne in Duskwall, marking the shrine's desecration. Featureless, representing Shar's connection to the void. It serves as a conduit: when the statue is attacked, Shar can communicate through it and psychically assault the attacker before it crumbles to dust. As a non-magical object, it is perfectly recreated by the daily time loop the following cycle — key confirmation that even completely destroyed non-magical objects are restored.

## Maze Key

**id: item_maze_key.** A non-magical key provided to the party by Duchess Galadiil Ilphekiir. Used to open locked internal doors within the Castle Vault maze. Not unique — several identical copies were found hidden inside the vault's puzzle rooms. The exterior entrance door to the maze does not require a key (handle only). Galadiil procured this from Evandur's hidden quarters; he keeps three.

## Maze Token

**id: item_maze_token.** A heavy, bronze token (~4 inches diameter) covered in distinct engravings. Given to the party by Duchess Galadiil Ilphekiir. Designed to be inserted into slots next to tumbler mechanisms on vault doors — allows a reroll of the tumbler display. During one heist attempt, the mechanism rejected it at a door leading to a four-way intersection, spitting it back out. Galadiil procured this from Evandur's hidden quarters; he keeps three.

## Ilphekiir Ring

**id: item_ilphekiir_ring.** A family heirloom belonging to Duchess Galadiil Ilphekiir. Kept hidden in her quarters — Evandur would not permit a visible symbol of her pre-Aestrum identity. Predates her displacement, the arranged marriage, and Quellenna's resentment. From a time before either sister became a political instrument.

Galadiil will not leave without it. She cannot fully articulate why — only that leaving it behind would feel like agreeing that the woman who wore it no longer exists.

**Chapter 2 significance:** Quellenna's death bounty on Galadiil requires the right eye and the right hand — the hand must bear this ring as proof of identification. The ring alone is not sufficient to claim the bounty. However, a player who possesses the ring or accurately describes it to Quellenna is immediately credible as someone who has genuinely encountered Galadiil. It is proof of meeting, not proof of death.
