## Description

Duskwall's traveler's inn, two streets up from the harbor. The building is tall and narrow — three storeys over a wide common room, squeezed between a chandler's shop and a rope-walk that runs the length of the block. The sign above the door is a wooden plank weathered nearly grey, the name carved deep enough that the paint isn't strictly necessary anymore: *The Last Resort*.

Inside: a long common room with a hearth at the far wall, tables sized for travelers eating alone or in small groups, and a counter that doubles as the registration desk. The proprietor sleeps in a room behind it. The smell is bread, woodsmoke, and the faint marine tang that gets into everything within three blocks of the water.

Upstairs: private rooms on the second and third storeys. Rates posted on a slate by the counter. The rooms are small and clean. The mattresses are straw over board, which is what you get for the price. The third-floor rooms have a view of the harbor.

Behind the inn: a small stable yard with a few stalls, a water trough, and a back gate onto the alley.

## Clientele

Traveler-facing. Sailors between berths, merchants overnighting before a buying run, the occasional out-of-town courier. Locals do not drink here — they go to the Hammered and Anvil or, if they have money, Stella's. The Last Resort exists for people who do not live in Duskwall.

A small steady population of regulars: traveling tradesmen who pass through often enough that the proprietor knows them by name and reserves their preferred room when they write ahead.

## What The Last Resort Provides

**Rooms** — private second- and third-floor rooms, modest rates. Stabling extra.
**Common room meals** — simple: bread, stew, the occasional roast on busy nights. Beer and wine by the cup or pitcher.
**Message holding** — letters and small packages can be left at the counter for travelers expected to pass through. The proprietor maintains a slate.

## The Name

The name is older than anyone currently in Duskwall remembers. The story most often told is that the previous proprietor named it as a joke about being the last lodging before the docks — anyone heading out by ship had to stop here first or sleep on the ship. Another version says it was named for the original proprietor, a man who had run three taverns into the ground and meant the name literally.

The proprietor will tell whichever version a guest seems most amused by.

## DM Notes

- **Party reset point.** This is the default inn for any traveling party that boards in Duskwall. Sleeping here applies the loop reset — the inn is curse-affected, the proprietor is not loop-aware, the rooms reset each morning. A party that leaves anything in their room finds it gone or returned to a stock state at midnight unless they're sleeping in a dead zone instead.
- **The name is layered irony.** The in-world story is genuine (last lodging before the docks). The deeper joke is that this is where travelers always end up — and in Aestrum, where the loop traps anyone who tries to leave, the inn lives up to its name in a way no one in town understands. Do not explain this to the players. Let it land if it lands.
- **Operationally low-stakes.** No active story threads run through this location. It is the party's home base for the first few cycles and a reliable anchor for the cadence of "wake up, leave the inn, do the day's work, return at night, reset." Use it for routine; don't pile hooks here unless one fits naturally.
- **Useful function: the slate.** The message-holding service is a real mechanic. NPCs who need to reach the party can leave word here. Couriers from outside Aestrum (none, currently — the loop) would historically have left messages here. A player who checks the slate occasionally may find ambient world detail.
- **Sailors from The Incorrigible** would stop in if they had shore leave longer than an evening. Currently they have not — Rylin keeps the crew close. If that changes, this is where they go.
