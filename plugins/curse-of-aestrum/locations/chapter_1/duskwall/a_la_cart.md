## Description

A hire yard just inside Duskwall's landward gate, where the road out of the city widens enough to turn a vehicle around. It is in neither the Market District nor the Waterfront — it sits at the city's entrance because that is where wheels are wanted, and because no one wants a yard full of draught horses anywhere they have to walk past twice. A gated yard, a long open-fronted shed, a water trough, a harness room, and the smell of horses and axle grease.

It is run by [Hesper Dunn]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md), who inherited it, and driven by [Owyn Frayle]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md), who does the evening run out to Mirot. Neither of them can be talked into anything.

It is the only place in Aestrum where a traveller can hire wheels, and it is smaller than that makes it sound.

## What Is Actually Here

**Draught horses and working carts.** Heavy animals bred to pull. They do hauling within the city, runs out to the nearer farms, and the occasional load east along the Tine Cross road. A cart moves at walking pace and always has. Hiring one saves a traveller's back and nothing else.

**No riding tack.** Not out of stock — the yard has never carried it. Nothing here has been broken to the saddle, there are no saddles in the harness room, and Hesper will say so without appearing to find the question interesting. See [rules/travel.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/travel.md), *On Foot Is the Only Sustainable Mode*.

**One carriage.** A proper enclosed coach with a matched pair, kept under the shed and kept well. It is the only carriage in Aestrum outside Duke Tallwood's household, and unlike anything else in the yard it is genuinely fast — roughly twice walking pace, which puts Duskwall and Mirot two hours apart instead of four.

**And it is spoken for.**

## What It Costs

**This is not a service most people in Aestrum can buy.** A cart and a day's use of a draught horse is a real expense for a working household — affordable once, for a reason, and resented. The coach is in another category entirely: a luxury, priced for visiting merchants, minor gentry, and people who have stopped counting. Hesper does not discount, does not negotiate, and does not care that the party thinks the number is high. The number is the number.

The practical effect is that **the party will feel the price before they feel the refusal.** A group that walks in expecting to hire wheels the way they would hire a room discovers first that they probably cannot afford the coach, and only then that it would not be available if they could. Two doors, closed in order.

*(Exact coin is a DM economy call and should be set against whatever the party is actually carrying — see [rules/item_persistence.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/item_persistence.md) on how mundane wealth behaves across resets, which is also why a party that farms enough cycles can eventually afford anything. Let them. By the time they can, the coach is still booked.)*

## The Standing Reservation

The carriage is booked, every day, by [Darwinnith Dundelver the First]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md) — the ailing patriarch of the Dundelver estate, in Duskwall for treatment he is not getting any better from. He is too ill to walk twelve miles and too proud to discuss why, so he pays for the coach and has done for long enough that the booking is simply part of the yard's day.

The run, every cycle:

| Time | |
|---|---|
| ~6:00 PM | The coach leaves Duskwall with DD1 aboard |
| ~8:00 PM | Reaches the Dundelver estate in Mirot — an hour after his son has taken the cart home, so the two do not meet |
| ~8:15 PM | The driver turns around |
| ~10:15 PM | Coach, team and driver are back in Duskwall |

**The driver always returns the same night**, and this is load-bearing rather than flavour. Animals do not reset ([rules/travel.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/travel.md)); a team left standing in Mirot overnight would still be in Mirot in the morning, and the yard would lose its horses a pair at a time until it had none. The round trip closes the circuit. It is why the carriage is here every morning and why the booking can repeat forever.

## What the Party Is Told

A party asking after transport gets a straight answer and no sales effort: carts, yes, at whatever the day rate is; the coach, no, it is held. Pressed, Hesper explains without lowering her voice, because it is not a secret — *a gentleman out at Mirot, poorly, has it standing. Every evening. Has for a good while.*

**That is a lead, and it should be allowed to be one.** The refusal hands the party the Dundelver estate, an ill old man, and a daily journey out to Mirot, delivered by a woman who thinks she is making conversation about her bookings. A party that follows it arrives at [the murder]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/quests/chapter_1/the_murder_of_rezibund_highbottom.md) sideways, which is more interesting than arriving at it head-on.

## Stealing It

The coach is not guarded. It is in a gated yard in a working city, harnessed or harnessable, and a party that decides to take it can take it. This is deliberate — see the travel rule for why the campaign never bars the party from transport outright.

**What actually prevents it on the first day is motive, not difficulty.** A newly arrived party has no idea that midnight matters. They are not racing anything, they do not know the boundary does what it does, and they are carrying a sealed letter of introduction they would rather not set fire to by committing a theft in a strange city within hours of walking into it. The urgency that would make the coach worth stealing only exists in hindsight, and by the time they have it they have already been snapshotted and can no longer leave Aestrum at all.

**Do not shut the door any harder than that.** If a party does steal it on day one and drives for the boundary, they have earned the escape and the campaign should let them have it. It is a narrow and strange thing to have thought of, and rewarding it costs less than visibly preventing it would.

**After the first midnight it is a one-day advantage at most.** The team does not reset with them; whatever they take out is somewhere else in the morning, and the yard's horses are gone from the yard for good. See the travel rule.

## Loop Notes

- Hesper and Owyn reset at the yard with everyone else, with no memory of yesterday's conversation, yesterday's hire, or yesterday's theft.
- The carriage and the carts are **objects** and recreate in the yard at 1:30 AM regardless of where they were left. The **horses do not.** A party that works this distinction out has a tool: the vehicle always comes back, the animal never does.
- DD1's booking is not remembered by anyone — it simply is the shape of the day, every day, and has been for ten years.
