## Description

The main jail of Duskwall, constructed within the cliffside near the seaport. Cells and corridors are carved directly from the rock — not built into it, but cut from it. The walls are thick, uneven, and perpetually damp from the sea. Sound carries strangely here: the ocean is always audible, muffled and constant, as if the cliff itself is breathing.

The entrance is at street level near the waterfront. A guardpost at the door, a short carved corridor leading down and in, then the cell block. No natural light below the entrance level. Lanterns in iron brackets along the corridor walls.

## Security

Castle guards staff the lockup on rotating shifts. The cells are iron-barred, standard construction — not built to hold anyone exceptional, because exceptional prisoners go elsewhere. The assumption built into the design is that its occupants are drunk, broke, or disorderly, and will be out in a day or two regardless.

A player who ends up here is not in mortal danger. They are inconvenienced, watched, and waiting.

## Callback Slots

- "The guard on duty" — whoever is working the door or walking the corridor; castle staff, professional, not particularly curious about who comes in or why; assign when a player engages them directly

## DM Notes

- The lockup resets with everything else at midnight. A prisoner who was jailed late in the day wakes at their reset point — not in a cell. This makes the lockup functionally useless as long-term detention for anyone loop-aware. The guards do not know this.
- A player casing the lockup from outside would find a single guarded entrance, no visible secondary access from the street, and cliff face below and above.
- The damp and the sound of the sea are consistent features. A player spending time here notices both.
