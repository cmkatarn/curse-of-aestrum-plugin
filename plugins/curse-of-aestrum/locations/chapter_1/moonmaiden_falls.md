## Overview

A short walk south of Dunleaven along a worn path. The Moonmaiden River originates here — not from rain or snowmelt but from a cluster of freshwater springs high on the slopes of Mount Mumandad, which feed the falls year-round regardless of season. The falls drop over thirty feet from a split in the grey rock face, striking the pond below with enough force to have carved it deep over centuries. The sound is constant and loud — not violent, but total. A white-noise roar that absorbs the surrounding quiet and replaces it with something else entirely. Conversations at the pond's edge require raised voices or proximity.

The pond is deep. The water is cold and clear close to shore; beyond a few feet it darkens quickly. Vegetation clings to the rock faces flanking the falls — moss, low shrubs, a few stubborn flowers on the left bank. Cut log benches face the water near a modest stone collection box, the kind travelers leave a coin in out of habit or reverence without always knowing which. A path branches off northeast toward the Deadery.

The name is Selûne's — the Moonmaiden — and the site has carried it long enough that its origin is not commonly discussed. It is simply what the falls are called.

## The Selûne Statue

At the deepest point of the pond, upright, rests the original Selûne statue from the Duskwall shrine. It is in remarkable condition.

This is because it resets each morning within the loop. The statue entered the pond two days before the cycle began — deposited by a cart that came through Dunleaven at night and did not stop, backed as close to the bank as the ground allowed, ropes cut, the statue toppling into the deep end upright by luck or the angle of the cart bed. Anyone who investigates the statue concludes it has not been underwater for more than a couple of days. That conclusion is accurate. The loop refreshes it each midnight to the state it was in when the cycle started: newly placed, undamaged, pristine.

The statue is recoverable. It rests upright on the pond floor — not buried, not collapsed. A character who can reach it and manage its weight can bring it to the surface. Large, heavy, carved without compromise in the open, lunarly aesthetic of Selûne worship: crescents, stars, open eyes. The opposite in every way of what replaced it in the shrine.

## Faction Reach

- **Selûne:** Nominal. The site carries Selûne's name and the collection box has the quality of a place worshippers have always visited. No active Selûne presence in Dunleaven — the Deadery's hauntings were historically the closest thing to organized religious work the village had.
- **Shar:** Indirect. The statue's placement here was operational — Hati needed the original Selûne statue removed from the shrine before installing the Shar replacement. The pond was convenient and the depth made recovery unlikely. No ongoing Shar interest in this location.

## Seed Secrets

*Vessel-agnostic. Assign to an elevated character at upgrade time.*

- Someone in Dunleaven saw the cart. It came through at night, no markings, did not stop at the well. Two people were on it. It went south toward the falls and came back lighter. The person who saw it did not ask questions at the time and has not volunteered the information since. They will, if asked directly about unusual traffic on the southern path.
- The collection box is fuller than the village's foot traffic warrants. Someone has been making regular deposits — not a traveler, the coin is too consistent. The keeper who empties it has noticed but not investigated.

## DM Notes

- **The statue's condition as a clue:** A player who investigates the statue and succeeds on a DC 13 Arcana or History check recognizes that a stone object underwater for any significant time would show erosion, algae, discoloration. This one shows none of those things. It appears to have been placed recently. This is accurate — but the implication (that it was placed recently) conflicts with anything the party knows about when Hati arrived and when the loop started. The discrepancy is a thread.
- **Recovering the statue:** No special equipment required beyond the ability to reach the bottom and manage significant weight. The statue is large — moving it is a group effort or requires creative application of magic or tools. Returning it to the Duskwall shrine would physically displace the Shar statue. Hati's response to this is immediate and without negotiation.
- **Mount Mumandad:** The mountain is visible from the falls — the rock face the water descends from is its lower slope. The springs are higher up, not accessible from the falls themselves without significant climbing. Mount Mumandad is not otherwise established in the campaign; its slopes and any features above the falls are undeveloped.
- **The path to the Deadery:** The branch northeast from the falls connects to the Deadery footpath. A party that arrives at the falls can reach the Deadery without returning through the village center.
- **The loop and the sound:** The roar of the falls resets each morning like everything else. For a loop-aware character spending the night nearby, the falls resuming at the same volume at the same moment each midnight is one of the more disorienting features of the cycle — a natural sound that is not, in this context, natural.
