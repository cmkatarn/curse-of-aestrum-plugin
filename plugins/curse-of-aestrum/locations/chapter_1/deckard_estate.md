## Overview

A modest farm property on the outskirts of Aestrum, within walking distance of the Misty Forest. Unremarkable from a distance — a working farm gone quiet, fields untended, a small house, outbuildings, and a well. The property sits entirely within a permanent dead zone originating from a divine intervention during the Acolypyrrhic Battles (~1280 DR). No magic functions here. The loop does not reach it. Rowan Deckard has owned and inhabited it for as long as Aestrum remembers.

Amid the general neglect, one thing on the grounds is kept: a small, deliberately tended patch of a tall plant with hooded purple-blue flowers, grown along the sheltered south wall of the house where the untended fields have otherwise gone to weed. It is the only cultivated thing on the property — the one place the neglect stops. (Wolfsbane; see DM Notes.)

The dead zone is why he has never left. It is the only place he has ever felt fully himself.

## Dead Zone Properties

The entire property is an antimagic field equivalent. Within its boundary:

- All spells fail. Magical items lose their properties.
- The loop's Sleep, Recreate, Teleport, and Modify Memory effects do not penetrate. Residents who sleep here are not reset. Their memories persist.
- Physical changes to the property are permanent — nothing here resets at midnight. A broken window stays broken.
- Rowan's lycanthropy is fully suppressed within the boundary. Outside it, his senses are hypersensitive and he is volatile.

The boundary is partially marked by magical charring — the same discoloration and scorch-without-fire-source residue that identifies other dead zones in Aestrum. The Deckard family has worked to remove it over the generations; most of the charring along the visible perimeter has been scraped away or covered. Some remains in the corners of outbuildings, under the eaves of the house, and along the eastern fence line where cleaning was never finished. A character who knows what to look for will find it. One who doesn't may simply notice something is off about the discoloration near the property edge without being able to name it.

## The House

Small and functional. A single-storey structure that has been maintained with the minimum effort required to remain livable. Rowan does not entertain. The house is for sleeping in and not much else.

**Main room:** A table, a chair, a hearth that sees regular use. Sparse. No decoration, no personal effects on display. Nothing here suggests the interior of the bedroom. The one exception to the bareness: several bundles of the hooded purple-blue flowers from the south-wall patch, tied and hung to dry from a beam near the door — some fresh and still colored, some old and gone to grey dust. Not arranged for show; readied for carrying. (Wolfsbane; see DM Notes.)

**The bedroom:** A normal farm bedroom — bed frame, dresser, the expected furniture of a working life. Every surface is covered in the symbols of Shar: drawn, scratched, painted, burned into the wood in varying states of weathering. The walls, the ceiling, and the furniture itself — the iconography is carved into the headboard, the dresser frame, the legs of every piece. The oldest marks are nearly illegible, worn smooth over years. The newest are fresh. He adds to them. This is not a shrine. This is what a man does alone with his walls over ten years when no one is watching. The room smells of smoke and something older. The ceiling reads as devotion that has long since passed the point where a word like "devotion" is adequate.

## The Larder

A detached structure built into a rocky mound approximately 150 feet southeast of the house. A heavy locked door is set into the face of the mound. Inside, no steps — the floor of the cave descends naturally, sloping gradually further underground as it advances. The cave system is natural; the lock on the entrance is not.

A torch brought inside burns more intensely than it should. The air is cold and still.

The bodies are stored in descending order of age — the newest nearest the entrance, the oldest deepest in. The newest are composed, positioned with care. They could be sleeping. Moving further in, the progression of decay tells the rest. The oldest, at the deepest point, are skeletal. Normal decomposition — the dead zone is antimagic, no preservation magic reaches here. Thousands of bodies. All the same woman.

The horror is in the care. The newest ones were placed, not dropped.

## Persistence — Why This Place Is Different

Because the dead zone prevents the loop's reset, **the estate accumulates change**.
Anything left here remains: supplies, notes, modifications to the space all persist
between cycles. In a region where everything else resets at midnight that makes it
uniquely valuable, and it is why a party that finds it tends to keep it.

**Rowan sleeps here.** The estate's dead zone is his anchor — returning inside it each
night is how he preserves his memory across the reset. That is the baseline.

**If a party occupies the estate, he loses it.** Displaced, he shelters in the Misty
Forest dead zone instead — enough to preserve his memory, but not home, and he knows
the difference. **This is a conditional branch, not the default state**: do not render
a displaced Rowan unless it has actually fired in that campaign, and record it in the
run's overlay when it does. See
[misty_forest_dead_zone.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/misty_forest_dead_zone.md).

## DM Notes

**The Shar ceiling:** The bedroom marks represent ten years of solitary worship. Not ceremonial — compulsive. He has run out of empty surface and begun layering over old marks. A player who examines the room carefully can see the oldest symbols beneath the newer ones — an archaeological record of his devotion. The effigy in the Misty Forest shrine is his public worship. The ceiling is what he does when he is not performing faith for anyone.

**The boundary:** Rowan knows the exact edge of the dead zone from years of testing it. He uses the boundary as tactical ground — retreating inside it when threatened. His lycanthropy suppressing immediately upon crossing back in is perceptible to anyone watching: the visible tension he carries outside drains from his body the moment he crosses the threshold.

**The larder lock:** The key is on Rowan's person. The lock can be picked or forced. Forcing it is loud. The larder has not been opened by anyone other than Rowan in ten years.

**Origin of the dead zone:** A god directly intervened at this location during the Acolypyrrhic Battles (~1280 DR), quashing all magic — creating the permanent dead zone. The Deckard clan has owned this land since shortly after. Whether they chose it knowing what it was, or came to understand it afterward, is not recorded. Rowan has never questioned his inheritance of it. He treats the dead zone as simply his — a feature of home, not a mystery.

**What does not reset here:** Everything. The larder contents accumulate. The bedroom marks accumulate. The dead. Nothing here moves backward.

**The wolfsbane (a lycanthropy hint vector):** The south-wall patch and the drying bundles in the main room are Rowan's own supply of wolfsbane — the classic anti-lycanthrope herb of Faerûnian folk belief. Its presence at a dead-zone estate is deliberate and, at first glance, redundant: the dead zone already fully suppresses his change while he is inside it. The resolution is that the wolfsbane is a **travel kit for the hours he leaves the zone** — every day he steps outside its boundary to pray at the Misty Forest shrine (~100 yds beyond the edge) and to go into town courting Miri, and in those windows the beast is *not* suppressed. He grows it locally (the tended patch), cuts and dries it (the hung bundles), and carries it against those exposed hours. The mix of fresh and long-dead bundles reads as years of the same practice.

This is a **deliberate, earn-it hint, not stumble-upon spoor.** The clue runs on cultural association, not a hard 5e mechanic: a character who can identify the plant (a Nature check) and knows what it is *for* (Religion/Arcana, or regional folklore about werewolves) can infer that someone here is managing — or warding against — lycanthropy. The one-cultivated-thing-on-a-neglected-farm framing is what flags it as worth examining; the inference lands harder alongside anything the party learns from Rowan's writings or behavior. A party without the lore sees only "a farmer grew and dried some flowers." Do **not** volunteer the werewolf conclusion — surface the plant honestly and let the knowledge checks and inference carry it.
