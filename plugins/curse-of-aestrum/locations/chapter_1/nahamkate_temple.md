## Overview

A Yuan-Ti temple dedicated to Sathraza, carved directly into the face of a box canyon deep in the Nahamkate Desert. The torches at the entrance have burned without interruption for longer than any record of the temple exists, and their smoke is what finds the canyon for you. The desert air is still enough that the column rises vertically, unbroken, visible above the dune line from well over a mile out once you are past the desert's shallow edge. A map can place you in the right region; the smoke does the rest. There is nothing else in the Nahamkate that produces a fixed vertical plume, and nothing natural that sustains one through the night.

The facade does not prepare you. The canyon narrows until its walls nearly touch overhead, cutting the sky to a strip, and then it opens — all at once, without warning — into a broad chamber of open air, and the temple is there. Two stories carved from the orange-red cliff face, as wide as the canyon itself, as tall as anything the desert has standing. The scale is the first thing. The second is the snakes.

Six columns form the lower story, their full length carved in tight helical scales — not decoration applied to a column but scales that are the column, wrapping it from base to capital, where each shaft resolves into a coiled serpent head facing outward, hood spread, mouth open. Between the columns: deep-set niches containing Yuan-Ti figures in postures of devotion, their lower halves tapering into carved coils that trail down to the plinth. Above the central doorway, a broad triangular pediment, its tympanum carved with concentric rings of serpents spiraling inward around a smooth scaled disc at the center. The upper story rises behind a broken pediment on each side and a central tholos — a circular structure with a domed roof, every surface scaled — flanked by half-columns whose capitals mirror the coiled heads below. At the apex of the whole facade: a serpent, rearing, hood spread wide, carved from the same cliff face and large enough that its shadow crosses the canyon floor at midday.

The torches burn at column height on either side of the entrance. They have burned without interruption for longer than any record of the temple exists.

The temple is ancient. It predates the loop, predates any living memory of Aestrum, and predates the mapping surveys that first documented the desert. The sands have buried and uncovered it more than once.

## Layout

**Canyon approach:** The smoke leads here — it is visible from the desert long before the canyon entrance is. Once inside, the canyon narrows until the walls nearly touch overhead, cutting the sky to a strip and blocking direct sun for most of the approach. The narrowing ends abruptly in an open space — and the facade fills it.

---

### Entry Chamber — Ritual Space

150 feet long, 100 feet wide, ceiling 30 feet overhead. Columns spaced throughout in two parallel rows, their surfaces carved in the same tight helical scale pattern as the facade. The walls carry the same liturgical glyphs as the exterior — readable by anyone with History proficiency or Draconic, covering the temple's dedication to Sathraza and the transformation rite described as a gift of ascension. The floor is worn smooth.

**The ritual:** At the far end of the chamber, a man in desert traveler's garb lies restrained by chains attached to iron rings set into the floor. He is conscious. He watches everything — the Yuan-Ti, the orb, any newcomers — with unguarded wonderment, and without fear. He has not been harmed. He does not know what is happening to him and does not appear troubled by not knowing.

Above him, suspended without visible support six feet off the ground, a translucent orb approximately six feet in diameter turns slowly and emits a soft, low warble — not quite sound, not quite silence. To most observers the orb appears to slowly drip magic downward onto the man, like light bleeding through fog. To Yuan-Ti and Dragonborn, the orb resolves differently: it is a dense mass of writhing snakes, and the dripping is not dripping — iridescent serpents are extricating themselves from the mass and slithering downward into the man's skin.

Around the restrained man, three Yuan-Ti observe and chant: two Malison and one Pit Master. They face the orb. They do not acknowledge anyone entering the chamber unless directly interrupted. Their chanting is low, rhythmic, and continuous.

**The overlook:** Set into the wall above and behind the ritual, an elevated platform — barely a ledge, perhaps eight feet above the chamber floor — where a Low Priest stands and guides the rite. He directs the rhythm below with small gestures. He is the ritual's conductor; the others are its instruments.

**The bone pile:** In the near-left corner, a pile of bones. On investigation, they are of various ages, none appearing older than approximately ten years. They are human and humanoid. There is no obvious cause of death visible on the older remains. Assaneela, if asked, cannot explain them — only that they were present when the Yuan-Ti detachment arrived.

**The back wall:** A large fanged serpent's head carved from the sandstone, floor-to-ceiling, with large emerald eyes. The eyes appear to track movement in the chamber. They do not.

**Assaneela's position:** Assaneela is not in the entry chamber by default. She meets arriving parties at the temple's facade in her archaeologist cover and turns them away politely. She only enters the chamber with the party after the Mounted Ruby has appeared and the arrangement has been struck. Once inside, she moves quietly near the columns and gives the party space to observe; normal speaking volume interrupts the ritual — not catastrophically, but visibly. The Yuan-Ti will pause, the chant will falter, and the Pit Master will admonish the interruption before the ritual resumes. Whispering is safe. See [npcs/chapter_1/assaneela.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/assaneela.md) for first-contact behavior and reveal.

**Ritual progression:** The transformation advances on a timer — roll 1d6 five times, each roll indicating the minutes before the next stage. Over the course of these five stages, the man's arms begin to change. If the ritual completes uninterrupted, his arms become living snakes. He becomes feral shortly after, the degree determined by the duration of the preceding rolls. Ultimately he will die. He does not survive the full transformation.

**Combat:** If combat begins, all Yuan-Ti in the chamber engage immediately except the Low Priest, who continues the ritual. Assaneela engages as well — but first summons the Malison heavy from the gem room.

### Secured Passage

A corridor connecting the entry chamber to the gem room. The floor is compacted sand over fitted stone. Pressure plates beneath the sand surface release snakes from channels in the walls when triggered — the corridor floods rapidly. The plates are detectable (Perception DC 15) by the seam pattern in the sand, or by the faint scale-texture of the plate edges where the sand has thinned. The trap resets each midnight.

### Gem Room

A square chamber, lower-ceilinged than the entry. Five stone pedestals stand in a loose arrangement, each bearing a gemstone in a fitted mount: yellow citrine, green jade, blue lapis, pale clear quartz, and a pink spodumene — the pink stone clearly placed with intention, but sitting slightly wrong in its mount, as if it is a near-fit for something the pedestal was not designed for.

At the back of the room, set into the wall: an alcove with an empty repository sized and shaped for a mounted gem. The mounting mechanism is distinct from the pedestals — it accepts only a gem that has been specifically set in a matching mount. The Mounted Ruby, recovered from the Dundelver Estate, fits this repository precisely. Nothing else does. The Yuan-Ti have attempted the pink spodumene here; it does not engage the mechanism.

**The murals:** The walls of the gem room are dense with carvings, murals, and relief work. Around each pedestal, multiple images and icons appear — animals, figures, and symbols from Yuan-Ti tradition and older desert cultures. Some images appear near more than one pedestal. The arrangement is deliberate.

| Pedestal | Gem (current) | Icons present |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yellow citrine | Anubis (jackal-headed figure), Isis (woman in crown), scarab |
| 2 | Green jade | Serpent (slender, solitary, open setting), gecko, scorpion |
| 3 | Blue lapis | Sea turtle, gecko, ibis |
| 4 | Pink spodumene | Woman figure (carved robes), falcon |
| 5 | Clear quartz | Owl (broad-winged, perched), cobra (hooded, upright), ibex |

**The solution:** The poem Assaneela carries — *Life in the Land Between Waking and Dreaming* — maps each animal to a gem color. Placing the correct colored gem on each pedestal, and seating the Mounted Ruby in the alcove, opens the passage to the treasure room. The pink spodumene is the Yuan-Ti's failed substitute for the ruby; it does not satisfy the alcove mechanism. Gem arrangement (correct solution): Yellow on Pedestal 1, Green on Pedestal 2, Blue on Pedestal 3, Red (Mounted Ruby in alcove) for the alcove trigger, White/Clear on Pedestal 5, Red gem on Pedestal 4 (the woman figure). See DM Notes for disambiguation.

**The Malison heavy:** One Malison remains in the gem room, standing guard. It does not engage unless Assaneela summons it or combat enters the room.

### Treasure Room

Two statues of Anubis flank the entrance, floor-to-ceiling, carved in the same orange sandstone — jackal-headed, staff in one hand, scales in the other. They are decorative. They are not animated.

**Perception DC 18:** Reveals two things simultaneously — a pressure plate set into the floor near the entrance, and a runic engraving on the left wall. The pressure plate, if triggered, closes the door back to the gem room. The runic engraving, if activated, illuminates all torches in the room and opens two circular holes in the walls, twelve inches in diameter each — their purpose is not immediately apparent.

**The relic:** In the center of the room, on a low stone plinth, a large piece of orange quartz carved into the shape of a flame. It reflects and amplifies the torch light if the torches are lit — the room fills with warm moving light when this happens. The relic is the Kossuth relic. It is warm to the touch. Its inner light responds to proximity.

**Perception DC 19** near the relic: A pressure plate is set beneath the plinth. Removing the relic activates it — a twelve-inch circular hole opens in the ceiling. Sand begins to pour in immediately and rapidly.

**The sand trap:** The room fills. Each minute spent in the filling room imposes a cumulative −2 penalty to all dice rolls tied to physical actions, as the sand rises and movement becomes increasingly difficult. The party has minutes, not many.

**Escape options:**
- Back through the gem room door — if the entrance pressure plate has not been triggered, this is straightforward.
- The right-hand Anubis statue: its base covers a weakened section of wall. Toppling the statue into the wall demolishes it, opening a gap into a narrow stone crevice beyond. The statue requires significant effort to move (STR check) and the sand rising in the room imposes the cumulative penalty on the attempt.

---

### Rear Crevice and Exit

A narrow natural fissure in the canyon rock, dark, close-walled, with light visible at the far end. The demolition of the wall has disturbed something sealed within it centuries ago: a mummy, entombed in the crevice and now roused by the impact. It must be dealt with before the party can pass.

Beyond the mummy, the crevice continues — narrow but passable — and opens onto the canyon wall at elevation, well above the canyon floor and some distance from the temple entrance. Getting down to the canyon floor requires minimal effort; the rock face is stepped enough to descend without equipment.

## Assaneela

Yuan-Ti Pureblood priestess of Zehir, working the temple in an archaeologist cover. She meets arriving parties at the facade and turns them away unless the Mounted Ruby is produced — at which point she drops the cover and proposes an arrangement to solve the gem room together. Her objective is the Scale of Ssath'razza, taken from this temple centuries ago by Akadi's followers. She arrived to find the relevant chamber stripped and has been searching the temple's secured spaces since.

She does not remember any prior encounter. Each cycle she is at the temple, pursuing the same research, running the same cover. She will make the same approach on any return visit.

See [npcs/chapter_1/assaneela.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/assaneela.md) for full character profile, speech pattern, and first-contact script.

## DM Notes

**The ritual — loop implications:** The man being transformed resets each midnight with everything else in the loop. Each morning the Yuan-Ti arrive, the man is restrained again, the ritual begins again. He has been subjected to this indefinitely, never completing the transformation, never remembering the previous attempt. The Yuan-Ti do not know this. Assaneela does not know this. Only a loop-aware party watching a second time would understand the full weight of what is repeating here.

**The bone pile:** Origin unknown and intentionally unresolved. Assaneela cannot explain them. The loop context raises a question the party may or may not pursue: if the loop resets the living, where do the bones of those who died in the temple come from, and why only ten years of ages? Leave this open.

**Puzzle disambiguation:**
- Gecko appears at Pedestals 2 and 3. Each is disambiguated by its co-icon: Pedestal 2 has the serpent (green), Pedestal 3 has the turtle (blue).
- Isis appears at Pedestal 1 alongside Anubis. Isis is a woman figure, which might suggest red. But Pedestal 4 has only the woman figure with no competing solution icon — red must belong to Pedestal 4. This forces yellow to Pedestal 1 (Anubis = amber = yellow).
- Cobra at Pedestal 5 resembles a snake, which reads green. But the poem's snake is at Pedestal 2 (slender, solitary, open desert). The cobra is hooded and upright — visually distinct. If a player tries green on Pedestal 5, Pedestal 2 is left without its gem; the solution fails.

**The alcove and the Mounted Ruby:** The alcove accepts only the Mounted Ruby — its mounting mechanism is specific. Placing the ruby in the alcove, with all five pedestals correctly arranged, opens the passage to the treasure room. The Yuan-Ti have pink spodumene in Pedestal 4 and have attempted the alcove with it; neither works.

**Treasure room — perception sequence:**
- DC 18 reveals both the entrance pressure plate AND the runic engraving. A player who finds one finds both.
- The twelve-inch wall holes opened by the runic engraving: purpose at DM's discretion — drainage, airflow, or a second mechanism not intended for the party to find.
- DC 19 near the relic reveals the pressure plate before the relic is lifted. A player who checks before touching has warning.

**Assaneela's reset behavior:** She has no memory of the party. Every encounter begins identically — human disguise, professional framing, reveal under pressure. A party that knows her runs the entire encounter ahead of her, which creates its own kind of interaction: they know what she is, what she wants, and what leverage she has. She does not know that they know.

**The Scale of Ssath'razza:** Taken north by Akadi's followers centuries ago. Current location unknown. Chapter 2 hook.

**Yuan-Ti iconography (wall text):** Readable by anyone with History proficiency or Draconic. The entry chamber text covers the temple's dedication and the transformation rite as doctrine. The gem room text is the puzzle itself — a player who reads it before attempting the room has the solution in hand.
