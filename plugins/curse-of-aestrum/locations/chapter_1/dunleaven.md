## Overview

A small village of perhaps sixty souls in the southeast of Aestrum, historically the funerary center of the duchy. The Deadery — an extensive crypt system southwest of the village — brought generations of mourners, scholars, and the morbidly curious through Dunleaven's one road. That traffic sustained the village. As funerary customs shifted over the decades and the novelty of managed hauntings faded, the mourners stopped coming. The village came on hard times quietly, without drama — the way small places diminish when their reason for existing moves on without them.

The village does not feel sorry for itself. It feels tired. Dunleaven once had a modest exorcism trade alongside the funerary work — hauntings from the Deadery were exorcised as they arose, and that service drew its own visitors. When the crypt sealed and nothing more came out, that work ended too. The people are not unfriendly; they are simply accustomed to the fact that most travelers pass through rather than stop.

## What's Here

- **The road to Duskwall** — runs north through the village; the bridge on the far side of town is broken and has been for years. Carts must ford the stream or turn back.
- **A well** — center of the village, on the road. The gathering point. The first destination for anyone arriving on foot.
- **A cluster of houses** — modest, well-maintained despite their age. Most families have been here for generations.
- **The eatery** — one of the larger houses, ground floor converted into a rough counter-service kitchen. No sign. Travelers find it by asking at the well. Open when the keeper feels like it.
- **The Deadery path** — a worn footpath southwest out of the village leading to the crypt entrance. No markers. Locals know it; visitors ask.
- **Moonmaiden Falls path** — a short walk south to the falls and the pond fed by the Moonmaiden River.

## Faction Reach

- **The Shrikes**: Peripheral. A contact passes through Dunleaven on the Duskwall road occasionally. No permanent presence. The village is too small and too poor to be worth taxing; the Shrikes use it as a waypoint, not an operation.

## Seed Secrets

*Vessel-agnostic. Assign to an elevated character at upgrade time.*

- The bridge petition to Duke Tallwood has been outstanding for over two years. Three letters sent. No response. The village elder who wrote them believes the Duke never received them — someone in the castle is holding the correspondence. A fourth letter is being drafted.
- Something large and stone-shaped was dumped in the Moonmaiden Falls pond some years back by a cart that came through at night and didn't stop. The person who saw it didn't ask questions. They've been thinking about it since.
- The Deadery's hauntings were historically exorcised as they arose — minor work, but steady. Several years ago they stopped entirely. The exorcist who handled them has had no work since. The locals find the silence more unsettling than the hauntings were. Anyone can walk into the Deadery; no one has found an explanation.

## Named Characters

**Pell** — Human, keeper of the eatery. Worn Professional. Has run the counter since her husband died; the work is the point, not the profit. Knows every traveler who's come through in the last decade — not because she's curious, but because there haven't been many. Will answer questions about the road, the Deadery, and the pond if asked directly. Does not speculate.

## DM Notes

**The Trambeathen tomb entry:** The Deadery itself is open access. The Trambeathen tomb specifically has two doors. The outer is a locked wooden door — conventional lockpicking DC. Behind it is a large stone door that is immune to magic — the loop does not operate on it, spells cannot open or affect it. Operated by a wheel crank set into the rock wall beside it. The hauntings stopped when this tomb was sealed; the rest of the Deadery is accessible and undisturbed.

**Inside the crypt:** The lesser skeletons were sealed in an inner vault years before the current campaign — their remains are there but contained. The Runic Skeleton (carrying the Eyeball Staff) still patrols the crypt interior day and night. A gargoyle serves as a secondary defense — specifically positioned to protect against discovery of the Pendant of Waking Hours hidden within. No one outside the crypt knows any of this.

**The skeleton threat:** If the Eyeball Staff is stolen and both doors left open, the Runic Skeleton will emerge and move toward the village. The well is the first destination — the road draws it. The gargoyle follows if the inner sanctum was disturbed. This is a consequence of specific player actions, not a scripted encounter. Dunleaven has no defense against it.

**The bridge:** A simple hook with outsized goodwill potential. Fixing the bridge costs materials and a half-day of work. To the village it would be transformative. The elder (unnamed — assign from callback slot) would know things about the road, the crypt, and the pond that aren't otherwise accessible.

**The petition:** The fourth letter is being drafted. If a player intercepts it, delivers it personally, or investigates why the first three were never answered, this thread connects directly to the Duskwall Castle administration and potentially to Galadiil or Evandur depending on who handles ducal correspondence.

**Dolores's cabin (hidden):** A homestead cabin sits a quarter mile east of the village, screened by a rise and a stand of trees. It is **not** to be mentioned in any narrated description of Dunleaven. It surfaces only when:

- A PC has passive Perception 16+ while in or moving through the village (notice the chimney smoke, or the worn track east through the grass), **or**
- The party actively searches the surroundings for something else (climbing for a vantage, scouting the perimeter, asking *"what else is out here?"*), **or**
- The party asks a Dunleaven resident about the cabin directly.

Dunleaven residents know Dolores lives out there and consider her one of their own. They will **not** volunteer the information. Asked directly, they answer plainly and briefly — *"Dolores. Quiet woman, keeps to herself, she's all right."* — and do not elaborate unless pressed. See [dolores_cabin.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/dolores_cabin.md) and [dolores.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/dolores.md).

**The pond:** The Selûne statue is recoverable — large, intact, in relatively shallow water. Returning it to the Duskwall shrine is not required to break the cycle. It would displace the Shar statue and anger Hati considerably.
