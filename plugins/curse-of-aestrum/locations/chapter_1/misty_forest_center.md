## Overview

A **three-storey stone house at the Misty Forest's centre**, home and workplace of
[Aliss Perisdottir]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/aliss_perisdottir.md) and her husband
[Thalamin]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md).

**It is visibly out of place.** The stonework is tight-fitted and mortar-free, in the
manner of Dalihad's guild masons — not locally built. The couple immigrated before the
loop began and **came south with the stones of the house already shipped.** Aliss knows
the stonework is unusual; she does not advertise the connection, but will confirm it if
asked.

*(The id is `loc_misty_forest_center` because that is the spelling the campaign already
referenced it by. The place is the house.)*

## The Three Floors

| Floor | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ground** | Thalamin's **smithy** — a working forge. Laid out below. |
| **Second** | kitchen and dining room; the stair up. Laid out below. |
| **Third** | Aliss's bedroom **and** her psychic parlor — one room, both jobs. Laid out below. |

## The Smithy — Ground Floor

Entered from the front of the house; a **lattice-paned window** in the far wall is the
room's daylight. Stone walls, plank benches, and a working floor kept clear around the
centre.

Reading the room from the door:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Centre of the room** | the **anvil**, on its stand, hammer to hand — the room is arranged around it |
| **Near-right corner** | the **coal pile** |
| **Right wall, middle** | the **grinding wheel** |
| **Right wall, far end** | stacked **ingots** — the stock |
| **Far wall, centre** | a **shaping stand** with finished armour on it |
| **Far-left corner** | a **bucket of finished work**, tools and pieces standing upright in it |
| **Left wall** | tool and stock storage, hung and leaning |

**The arrangement is a working one, not a display.** Heat and fuel sit together on the
right — coal by the door end, wheel and stock further in — so the fetching happens along
one wall and the middle stays clear to swing in. Finished work migrates leftward, away
from the fire: the stand on the far wall, the bucket in the corner past it.

**What a party sees on entering:** a man working at an anvil in the centre of a lit
room, with the day's finished pieces already set aside behind him. He does not stop.

**The loop makes the stock strange.** Anything Thalamin finishes and leaves in this room
is gone by morning — the bucket and the shaping stand are cleared by the reset like
everything else. Only work put into the dead box, or carried into another dead zone,
survives the night. He knows this and is patient about the arithmetic; it is why the
finished pieces sit *waiting* rather than accumulating.

## The Kitchen — Second Floor

The household's living space, and the **only route to the third floor**: an internal
stairwell runs up from here to Aliss's parlor.

Reading the room from the door:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Far wall** | the **stairwell up**, taking most of the wall |
| **Centre**, backed against the stairs | the **table**, two chairs |
| **Centre-right** | the **stove**, under its hood, pots standing on it |
| **Around the stove** | prep counter and standing stores — a churn, jars and bottles, whatever the larder is holding |

It is a working kitchen for two people, not a hall. The table seats exactly the
household.

**Every client walks through it.** Aliss receives on the third floor and the stair to
it starts here — so anyone who comes to the forest for a reading passes the stove, the
table, and whatever Thalamin's wife was doing before they knocked. The family's
domestic room is the corridor to her business, and neither of them appears to mind.

**The larder never runs down.** This floor resets like everything else: a meal eaten
tonight is uncooked in the morning, the stores are whole again, and the washing-up is
undone. Ten years of dinners have consumed nothing. Whatever is on the table when a
party arrives is what is on the table *every* day at that hour — not a coincidence, and
not hospitality laid on for them.

## The Parlor — Third Floor

**One room, doing both jobs.** It is Aliss and Thalamin's bedroom *and* the room where
she receives clients. There is no separate consulting space.

Reading the room from the top of the stairs:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Straight ahead, far wall, centred** | the **bed** — a bed for two; they share it |
| **Centre of the room** | a **support beam**, with **Aliss's table** beside it |
| **Left wall** | a **large window** looking out into the forest |
| **Left of the window** | a **writing table** |
| **Right of the window** | a **nightstand** |

Also in the room, position not fixed: a **wardrobe**, holding clothing, and a **chest**,
holding her working kit — the crystal ball, her tarot deck, a myriad of other divination
articles, and several kinds of incense. **The chest is where the instruments live when
they are not on the table.**

## The Table Is a Bet She Has Already Placed

**The table's default is the crystal ball**, but that is a resting state, not a rule.

Aliss gets **occasional glimpses of what her next client will need**, and when one
comes she **sets the table for it in advance** — the ball, the cards, whichever
instrument the glimpse called for, drawn from the chest before anyone knocks.

What happens next depends on whether she was right:

| The glimpse | What she does |
|---|---|
| **Right** — this client needs what the table is set for | She **makes a point of it.** The table was laid for you before you came, and she will let you notice. |
| **Wrong, or early** — the glimpse was misleading, or it belonged to a client still to come | She **says nothing about the table**, and quietly adds or removes the ball to do what *this* client actually needs. |

**So the party will only ever be told about the times she was right.** The misses are
corrected in front of them without comment and never acknowledged. That is not
deception exactly — she is a genuine diviner having an ordinary professional day — but
it is stagecraft, and it is the reason her reputation is what it is.

**For the DM, the tabletop is a decision, not a description.** What is sitting on it
when a party walks in encodes whether her foresight landed *for them* — and if it
didn't, whether the glimpse was simply wrong or was meant for someone arriving later.
Either is playable; the second is quietly the more interesting, because it means
somebody else is coming.

Her serious register is the cards. The vision locating Jiasha is delivered exactly that
way — diction sharpening, the cards turning slowly, each image spoken aloud as it
arrives. See
[the_abduction_of_jiasha.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/quests/chapter_1/the_abduction_of_jiasha.md).

**Clients are received in the bedroom.** Whatever the party expected of a fortune
teller's parlour, this is a married couple's room with a table in it — the bed is the
first thing visible from the stairs, and it is *directly ahead*. Combined with the walk
through the kitchen to get here, a reading at this house means being taken all the way
into two people's private life. Aliss does not treat that as remarkable. It is worth
letting the party notice that she doesn't.

## Getting Here

Reached by the **Perisdottir path** — a maintained walking path running south off the
4WI–3WI road, ~2 miles and about 45 minutes on foot, requiring no woodcraft. **The path
terminates here**; it does not continue further into the forest as a maintained route.
From Duskwall the whole trip is ~6 miles, about two hours. See
[routes.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/routes.md) for both segments.

Leaving the path inside the forest is where navigation becomes difficult — the mist is
consistent and the canopy closes off the sky. The [dead zone]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/misty_forest_dead_zone.md)
lies north-west of the house, just west of the path.

## The Daily Cycle — a house that changes by the hour

**This is not a place with one state.** It has the same state every day, and that state
runs on a clock:

| Hour | Aliss | The house |
|---|---|---|
| **Waking → an hour or two in** | resets like everyone else; **functionally not loop-aware** | an ordinary morning; she lives the routine |
| **Mid-morning onward** | her eye catches the **dead box** and the day's truth lands | she reads her notes; **she is loop-aware** |
| **After she has read** | tells Thalamin, then receives clients | the parlor is open for business |

**The dead box** is a magical container immune to the daily reset, holding years of
accumulated notes on every client she has ever seen. It is the only reason her
cycle-awareness survives midnight — and finding it is a thing she does *late*, which is
why the hours lost to the morning routine are described on her sheet as part of the
daily cost.

**A party's arrival hour therefore decides who they meet.** Morning, and she is a
fortune teller who does not know what year it is. Afternoon, and she is the woman who
has been waiting years for them. Check the hour before running the scene.

This cycle **repeats** — it is not a one-way transition. Every day resets it.

## What Happens Here

**Aliss reads for clients, for money.** She is a known quantity in Duskwall — the
fortune teller in the forest, expensive or free depending when you go — and she uses
the loop to expand her clientele, gaining new customers daily who have no memory of
prior visits.

**Thalamin works the forge**, and the loop makes his economics strange: work finished
and stored in the dead box or another dead zone **persists**; anything stored elsewhere
resets with the day. He offers essence extraction — converting an existing magic item
into derived protection items, consuming the source.

## DM Notes

**Two residents, and only one of them is awake to the situation.** Thalamin is
`cycle_aware: true` **only because Aliss tells him each morning, after she has read.**
Before that conversation, on any given day, he does not know either. He is not a
combatant, but a party that threatens Aliss finds him in the doorway with a smith's
hammer.

**The conditional branch:** if Aliss ever leaves with a party, Thalamin wakes each
morning to find her gone, registers the absence dimly, and slides past it — the
fuzzball closes over the question. That state is not the campaign's default and should
not be rendered unless it has actually fired.

**Aliss withholds the wolf.** She warns of the forest's north-west quarter and does not
name what is in it. That withholding is canon; see
[routes.md]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/locations/chapter_1/routes.md) on the naming discipline for that corridor.

## Open Slots — To Develop in Play

- **[layout]** **Where the wardrobe and chest stand** in the third-floor room. Both are
  established; their positions are not.
- **[layout]** **Where the dead box sits.** All three floors are now laid out and the
  box is the one fixture still unplaced — and its placement is *constrained*, not free.
  Aliss wakes in the third-floor room and lives a normal morning; her eye catches the
  box only **an hour or two in**. So it cannot sit anywhere she would look on waking,
  or she would be cycle-aware from the first minute and the daily cost her sheet
  describes would not exist. The writing table by the window, the nightstand, and
  somewhere on a lower floor she does not reach until later are all candidates that
  satisfy that; the bedside is not. **Worth deciding deliberately** — it sets the hour
  she becomes herself.
- **[appearance]** The house's exterior beyond "three storeys, Dalihad stone, out of
  place."
- **[backstory]** Why a Dalihad-stone house was shipped south and rebuilt at the centre
  of an Aestrum forest. The stones travelled deliberately, and nothing says why.
