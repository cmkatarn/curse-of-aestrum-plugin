## Overview

The inner sanctum of the Trambeathen section, accessible only through the magic-immune door. Lisandre sealed this when he left Dunleaven. Everything behind the inner door is what he was protecting.

There are no mounted torches or braziers anywhere past the outer wooden door. Visitors must bring their own light.

## Layout

**The inner door:** A large stone door operated by a wheel crank set into the rock wall. Turning the crank raises the door vertically into the ceiling. Magic-immune — the loop does not operate on it, spells cannot affect it. Its position persists through midnight.

**The circular chamber:** A 50-foot circular chamber immediately beyond the inner door. Alcoves line the walls — Trambeathen family burials, some occupied, some empty. Four slightly-raised mounds are set into the floor with no headstones. These are graves. Each contains a lesser skeleton, each armed with a different weapon type. They do not stir unless disturbed.

**The tunnel:** At the back of the circular chamber, a passage leads deeper in — 5 feet wide, 10 feet tall, 30 feet long.

**The large chamber:** The tunnel opens into a roughly 300-foot diameter cavern. Stalactites descend from above; stalagmites rise from the floor. Natural formation, unworked stone. The scale is disorienting after the narrow tunnel.

**The mausoleum:** Set against the far wall of the large chamber. Rusty ancient lock on the door. Atop the mausoleum, a gargoyle keeps silent watch — it does not engage until prey approaches.

**The sarcophagus:** Inside the mausoleum, a stone sarcophagus contains the remains of Elsinoor Trambeathen, the Pendant of Waking Hours, and assorted minor jewelry.

**The Runic Skeleton:** Patrols the entire zone — both the circular chamber and the large cavern. Its bones are covered in magical runes providing protection, immunity to magic, and amplified power. It carries the Eyeball Staff. Lisandre designed it to be drawn to the staff — the skeleton will always orient toward the staff's location. If the staff leaves the tomb, the skeleton pursues it.

## DM Notes

**The graves:** The four mounds give no indication of what's inside. No headstones, no markers. Players who investigate the floor may notice the slight elevation. The lesser skeletons animate when disturbed — either by digging, or when the Runic Skeleton is engaged and they respond to the combat.

**The gargoyle:** Positioned specifically to protect the mausoleum. Does not engage intruders who remain at a distance. Activates the moment anyone approaches the mausoleum door. A wild creature — does not reset.

**Lisandre's morning scrying:** Each morning upon waking, Lisandre activates the Eyeball Staff remotely to confirm the tomb is undisturbed. He resets each midnight with no cross-cycle memory — but every morning he performs this check from habit, and every morning the view through the eye shows whatever surroundings the staff is currently in. If the staff is gone from the tomb, the morning scrying reveals its new location. He recognizes his own shop through his own eye if the party visits him while carrying it.

**What the sarcophagus contains:** Elsinoor's remains and the Pendant of Waking Hours. The Pendant cannot be removed from a living wearer — Elsinoor died wearing it. On her remains it is simply an item. The minor jewelry has no significance beyond value.

**Elsinoor's murder:** Elsinoor was a gifted enchanter who worked in a windowless study to avoid distraction. Her one remaining obstacle was the limits of waking hours. Lisandre presented her with the Pendant — told her he'd enchanted it so she wouldn't fall asleep while wearing it. She put it on immediately and went to her study. He locked and barricaded the door behind her. When she tried to leave and found she couldn't, she tried to remove the Pendant and found she couldn't do that either. Over the following weeks, sleep deprivation eroded her sanity. She eventually bashed her head against the wall until she died. When the study went quiet, Lisandre arranged a hasty internment.

**Forensic evidence in the sarcophagus:** The remains will show two things to anyone who examines them carefully:
- Fractures to the frontal bone consistent with repeated blunt impact against a flat surface. The pattern differs from a weapon strike — broader, less localized, consistent with a head-to-wall impact repeated many times.
- Wear on the distal phalanges (fingertip bones) consistent with weeks of clawing at stone.

Neither detail is conclusive alone. Together with the Pendant's presence and the sealed chamber, they build a case. A full case still requires corroboration from Lisandre's history — the hasty remarriage, the quick relocation, the estate fire days after he left.
