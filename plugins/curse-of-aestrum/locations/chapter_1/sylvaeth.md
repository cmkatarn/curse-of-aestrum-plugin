## Overview

An old elven enclave hidden in the deep forests and highland vales of Skaarsdaam,
well apart from Duke Amblecrown's human seat. The name glosses, roughly, as *the
remembering wood* — and remembering is its purpose. Sylvaeth is a keeping-place:
a small, insular community of elves who preserve old verse, older history, and a
living tradition of the blade-song. It keeps to itself, asks nothing of the
duchy that surrounds it, and is largely a rumor to the Skaarsdaam humans who
live a day's travel downslope.

## Setting

Reached only by those who already know the way — terraced timber-and-stone halls
built into a forested highland vale, threaded with cold streams and older trees
than anything in the lowlands. Quiet by design. The enclave does not advertise
itself; travelers who stumble near it tend to find the paths turning them gently
elsewhere.

## What They Keep

- **Verse and history.** Sylvaeth holds one of the more complete archives of
  pre-Acolypyrrhic elven poetry in this part of Nortmunde — court verse, war
  elegies, the long memory-songs that predate the human duchies. Scholarship here
  is devotional, not academic; a poem is a thing one *keeps alive by knowing it*,
  not a specimen.
- **The blade-song.** Sylvaeth is one of the enclaves that still teaches
  Bladesong — the wizard-warrior discipline that weds spellwork to swordwork.
  Traditionally a closed elven art, taught within the community and rarely
  beyond it.
- **The old practices generally.** Language, calligraphy, the disciplines of
  attention and memory that the verse and the blade-song both rest on.

## Outsiders and Welcome

Sylvaeth rarely admits outsiders, and almost never humans — but it is not closed
out of malice. The gate is *devotion.* A genuine, sustained love of their work —
the kind that keeps a student bent over the old texts for years with no
expectation of reward — can earn a degree of latitude few outsiders ever see:
lodging, teaching, and slowly, access to practices the enclave guards. The
elves are unsentimental about talent (they have seen centuries of it) but they
are quietly moved by *sincerity*, and they will make room for a sincere student
even one who never masters the art they came for. What such a student is taught
beyond poetry — including the blade-song — depends on what the enclave comes to
see in them.

Standing earned at Sylvaeth tends to persist: an outsider who left in good
regard remains welcome to return, and elves elsewhere who hear of that standing
extend a measure of it.
