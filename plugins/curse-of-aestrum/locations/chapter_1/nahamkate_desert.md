## Overview

The Nahamkate occupies the northwestern extent of Aestrum — a barren, orange-hued wasteland that the duchy's maps acknowledge but its roads do not prepare you for. The road from Mirot does not end so much as surrender. Cobblestones become irregular as sand accumulates between them, then fewer in number as dunes encroach, then absent entirely. The transition from rolling hills to desert is subtle in kind but quick in pace — the vegetation thins, the color of the ground shifts, and the sky seems broader than it was a mile back. The road's original course can still be traced by the fence posts: a low wooden border that once lined the road, now reduced to weathered posts leaning at angles, their bases buried, their tops still reaching above the sand. Following the posts carries a traveler further into the desert than any cobblestone will.

The Nahamkate was once a diaspora of Yuan-Ti civilization. No Yuan-Ti have been documented in the region for decades, but the desert preserves what it covers. Shifting sands regularly unearth or rebury Yuan-Ti structures — temples, passages, relics — as dunes move. What is exposed today may be buried in a season. Snakes and scorpions of species found nowhere else in Aestrum are common here.

## The Dead Zone

The Nahamkate dead zone is identifiable by magical charring — a residue of sustained antimagic suppression visible in the ground: patches of discoloration on stone and compacted sand, fused grains where ambient magic burned out of the earth, scorch patterns on exposed rock that bear no smoke signature and show no fire source. The effect reads, at first glance, like old fire damage. Closer inspection or an Arcana check reveals the consistency of the pattern and the absence of any ignition origin.

Within the charred boundary, magic ceases entirely. Spells cannot be cast. Magical items lose their properties. The loop's midnight reset does not reach this ground. A party that sleeps here retains their memories through the morning.

The dead zone is not co-located with the temple. It lies roughly a quarter-mile off the main desert path; the temple sits further in, in a box canyon, and its magical features function normally.

## Approach

Beyond the fence posts, navigation shifts to canyon formations visible on clear days and whatever maps can be trusted. The Salient Cartographer's Nahamkate Desert Survey notes that interior desert detail should not be trusted beyond a few miles out. The main path into the desert remains navigable; departure from it requires a clearer head and better reference points than most travelers carry.

## DM Notes

- **The charring as a dead zone indicator:** The charring is the intended method of identification for players. An Arcana DC 12 confirms it as antimagic residue rather than fire damage. A character who has encountered another dead zone — the Deckard Estate, the Misty Forest — recognizes the same pattern immediately.
- **The fence posts:** They follow the original road path and extend further into the desert than the cobblestones. A party that notices and follows them saves time. They do not reach the temple — the posts were never intended to mark that route.
- **Yuan-Ti resurgence:** Not intended as a recurring faction. The desert encounter was a set-piece. Signs of Yuan-Ti presence (uncovered fragments, shifted sand revealing stonework) are atmospheric, not hooks.
- **The dead zone as a rest point:** The location works as a long-rest site, but the antimagic boundary means magical items cannot be used here — a tradeoff worth naming if the party camps here.
