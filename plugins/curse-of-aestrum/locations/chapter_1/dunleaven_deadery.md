## Overview

An extensive crypt system southwest of Dunleaven village, reached by a worn footpath from the village center. For generations the Deadery was the funerary center of the duchy — families interred their dead here, mourners came through Dunleaven, and the village made its living on that traffic. As the region's religious practice shifted toward Selûne worship over the decades, funerary customs changed with it. Fewer citizens were brought to the Deadery. The mourners stopped coming. The village diminished quietly, without drama — the way small places do when their reason for existing moves on without them.

The Deadery is open access. The path is unmarked but locals know it; visitors ask at the well. The interior is navigable — most of it. The general crypt is undisturbed. The Trambeathen section has its own doors.

## Layout

**The general crypt:** Stone corridors, family burial alcoves, the accumulated dead of several generations of Aestrum's smaller noble families. Undisturbed. Nothing patrols here.

**The Trambeathen section:** Set apart from the general crypt by two doors. The outer is a locked wooden door — conventional construction, conventional lockpicking. Beyond it lies the inner door, but this is not commonly known — the outer door has been locked since Lisandre Trambeathen sealed it and left.

## Rumors

*Safe to surface in any conversation about the Deadery or Dunleaven.*

- The Trambeathen section has been sealed since Lisandre locked up and moved to Duskwall. Nobody has been through that outer door since. Whether there's anything worth seeing inside is anyone's guess.
- Hauntings arise occasionally in the Deadery — they always have. The local exorcist, Corva, handles them when they come up. Minor work, but steady enough. Part of the texture of living near a crypt.

## DM Notes

**The outer door:** Conventional wooden door, conventional lockpicking DC. No magic, no tricks.

**The inner door:** Magic-immune stone door operated by a wheel crank in the rock wall — the crank raises the door into the ceiling. The face of the door bears inlaid runes in an archaic script — the same script that marks every magic-immune item in the world.
