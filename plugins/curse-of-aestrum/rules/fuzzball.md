## Overview

After the [revert]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/revert.md) fires, Shar plants a pattern-correcting sub-process in each subject — the **fuzzball**. It is a knot of thoughts: pulling on any one thread tightens the knot. Try a different thread — it tightens further. On and on, until the subject loses interest in what they were originally pursuing and gives up.

The fuzzball is not a wall. It is **friction that compounds until the subject stops pushing**.

The fuzzball's other job is **smoothing over the gap** between the subject's snapshot-state memory and the actual state of the world around them. A reverted subject wakes believing it is the morning they first arrived, but the world has moved on by years. The fuzzball quietly papers over the discontinuity — the subject does not question why they are where they are, why they recognize a face they have "never met," or why a routine they have "never performed" feels habitual.

---

## The Knot Metaphor — Key Behavioral Tell

A subject working through the fuzzball will:
- Appear to lose their train of thought mid-sentence.
- Change the subject without seeming to notice.
- Abruptly decide the matter isn't worth pursuing — not because they've concluded it's false, but because the thread stopped being pullable.

Run this as a play-at-the-table tell. Drift the NPC's attention. Have them ask about the weather. Have them suddenly remember an errand.

## Escalation by Exposure Level

The fuzzball's strength scales with how directly the subject is confronted with the anomaly.

### Hint
*Something seems subtly amiss — an overheard comment, a stranger who seems to know them, a door that shouldn't be open.*

The fuzzball activates. The subject's attention slides off the anomaly. They may register a vague unease, but decide whatever is wrong can't be that bad — and return to routine.

### Direct Statement
*Told outright that something is wrong — the loop, that they've met before, that it's actually a different year.*

The subject is more likely to wave it off as insanity than to engage seriously. The fuzzball reframes the **source of the information** as the problem, not the information itself. They may become mildly irritated or concerned for the speaker's wellbeing.

### Proof
*Shown physical evidence, demonstrated the loop mechanically, confronted with something that cannot be explained away.*

The fuzzball cannot fully suppress this. The smoothing-over breaks down: the subject begins actively questioning where they are, what they were doing, why a place or person feels familiar that they have no memory of, why their routine doesn't match their last clear recollection. (Note: the revert has erased everything since the snapshot, so there are no buried "real" memories surfacing — what surfaces is the **absence of explanation** for the subject's current situation, no longer being papered over.) **This state persists until their next reset.** At midnight, the revert erases the breakthrough entirely and the fuzzball reasserts clean.

A subject in proof-state is distressed, possibly non-functional, and will not find resolution before midnight takes them.

## Moving a Fuzzballed NPC — The Laundering Rule

**This section is prescriptive. Do not improvise around it.** Whenever a party tries to
get a looped resident to *do* something — escalate a matter, open a door, investigate a
post, carry a message, change their mind — resolve it by the procedure below rather than
by judging in the moment how the fuzzball "would feel" about it.

### The test

**The fuzzball fires on the anomaly, not on the request.** It has exactly one job with
respect to information: smoothing the gap between the subject's snapshot-state memory and
the world. A claim that does not touch that gap does not engage it at all.

So, for every claim the party makes, ask one question:

> **Does this claim require the loop to be true?**

- **No** — the fuzzball never activates. Resolve as ordinary persuasion, against the NPC's
  ordinary disposition, skepticism and self-interest. The NPC may still refuse; that is a
  roleplay outcome, not a fuzzball outcome. **Do not invoke the fuzzball here.**
- **Yes** — apply *Escalation by Exposure Level* above, and read the outcomes below.

The test is about the **claim**, not the speaker. A loop-aware party asserting an ordinary
present-tense fact is making an ordinary statement, however they came by it.

### What loop-framed claims actually achieve

Read these as outcomes, not as a spectrum to interpolate:

- **Hint / Direct Statement → the request dies with the thread.** The NPC does not act.
  Worse, at Direct Statement the fuzzball "reframes the source of the information as the
  problem" — so the party is now the people who said the mad thing, and every later
  approach to that NPC on that cycle carries it.
- **Proof → still not action, and usually worse.** A subject in proof-state is
  "distressed, possibly non-functional, and will not find resolution before midnight."
  **A distressed non-functional official does not escalate a matter, open a gate, or
  send a letter.** Breaking someone's fuzzball is not a way to get their cooperation; it
  is a way to spend them for the rest of the day. Treat proof as a last resort with a
  known cost, never as a persuasion technique.

**There is no exposure level at which telling a looped NPC about the loop gets the party
what they want.** That is the point of the mechanic and it should hold consistently.

### Laundering — the craft, and the party's real advantage

Almost every loop-derived insight has a **mundane present-tense equivalent** that carries
the same actionable payload and touches nothing anomalous. Strip the repetition, keep the
fact:

| Loop framing — fizzles | Mundane framing — lands |
|---|---|
| "These men are killed and replaced *every morning*" | "The two men on that post **right now** are wearing the wrong crest under their gear" |
| "He is murdered *every night* at half past eleven" | "There is a plot on his life. Here is the name." |
| "Your records are ten years stale because time is frozen" | "This claim is under a foreign seal and exceeds your authority. Refer it." |
| "You have told me this three hundred times" | "You mentioned it earlier" — or nothing at all |

This is the loop-aware party's actual edge, and it is a **skill rather than a hint**. They
know things nobody else in Aestrum can know; the craft is saying them in a form a looped
mind can hold. A party that works this out has learned the most useful thing there is to
learn about living here.

**Do not tip it.** Do not have an NPC explain why the loop framing failed, and do not have
the narrator flag that a rephrasing would have worked. The party discovers it by watching
one approach slide off and another land.

### Escalating despite a triggered fuzzball

A party may raise the loop **and** make a separate, mundane case. This resolves cleanly
and should be run exactly this way:

1. **The loop thread dies.** Per the knot metaphor, the fuzzball ends *inquiry into the
   anomaly* — attention drifts, the subject changes the subject, the matter stops being
   pullable. It does not end unrelated business that was never anomalous.
2. **The mundane case survives on its own merits.** It was never in the knot. Resolve it
   as ordinary persuasion.
3. **The party pays for the attempt.** At Direct Statement the NPC's assessment of *them*
   is damaged. An official may well still act — and attach a note about the reliability of
   the people who brought it. Carry that consequence forward on that cycle.

So: **yes, a matter can escalate despite a triggered fuzzball — but never because of what
was said about the loop.** It escalates because a second, ordinary argument was standing
next to it. If the party has no second argument, raising the loop leaves them worse off
than saying nothing.

### The two exceptions, in practice

Both standing exceptions are in *Exceptions* below; what they mean for **getting something
done** is narrower than it looks.

- **Dead zones** suspend the fuzzball, so a looped subject inside one can hold anomalous
  information for as long as they remain. This is a real route and deliberately impractical:
  it means physically getting that person to a dead zone, and **the next revert erases it
  regardless** unless they are inside one when the reset fires. Nothing learned this way is
  durable and no instruction given this way survives midnight. Use it for a conversation
  worth having once, never for a standing arrangement.
- **Moon Amulet bearers** carry a benign fuzzball with nothing to smooth, so ordinary
  persuasion applies to them in full — including about the loop. They are the only people in
  Aestrum the party can simply tell.

## Exceptions

- **Dead zones:** The fuzzball is a magical construct and does not function inside a [dead zone]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/dead_zones.md). Subjects inside one do not carry an active fuzzball while inside. The fuzzball reasserts the next time they revert outside a dead zone.
- **Moon Amulets:** Bearers are still planted with a fuzzball — the amulet blocks the [revert]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/revert.md), not the fuzzball. However, because the amulet-bearer's memories already match the real state of the world, the fuzzball has nothing to smooth over and effectively does nothing. It only manifests when the bearer's experience diverges from reality, which for an unbroken amulet-bearer it does not. Treat the fuzzball as **benign** for amulet-bearers under normal conditions.

## Post-Loop Dissolution

When Shar's hold over Aestrum is broken, the fuzzballs are no longer sustained by her divine power. They dissolve over a short period.

NPCs will experience:
- A gradual lifting of the pattern-correction.
- Disorientation as they notice strangeness they have been sliding past — the unexplained familiarity of places and people, routines that feel habitual without origin, the gap between their snapshot-state memory and the world in front of them.
- Eventually, a full (and potentially overwhelming) confrontation with the discontinuity: their last clear memory is of arriving, and everything since is unaccounted for.

This is a roleplay opportunity post-resolution — the town slowly waking up.
