## Overview

Some discrete objects in this world are **immune to magic** — antimagic field equivalents in portable form. Every such object bears **inlaid runes in an archaic script**. The script is the visible signature of the magic-immunity; without the runes, no item is immune. With them, the immunity is total within the object's bounds.

Treat a runed item as a localized [dead zone]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/rules/dead_zones.md) the size of the object itself.

---

## The Script

- **Ancient and unread.** Almost no one alive can translate it.
- Sages may recognize it as a known archaic language without being able to render it.
- A character with relevant background, very specific training, or access to the right text may be able to read fragments.
- **Consistent across all magic-immune items**, regardless of the maker or the object's purpose. This consistency is the signal.

---

## Documented Items

- **Aliss Perisdottir's dead box** — a foot-square iron box, brass-bound, runes inlaid in the iron face. Holds her years of accumulated notes, immune to the daily reset.
- **The Trambeathen inner door (Dunleaven Deadery)** — a stone door operated by wheel crank, runes inlaid in its face. Holds its position across midnight resets regardless of what state it was left in.

---

## Running the Convention at the Table

When the party encounters a magic-immune item, **describe the runes as part of the object**. Don't flag them. Let the player notice.

- A player who recognizes the pattern from a prior encounter (Aliss's box, then the Deadery door, or vice versa) has **identified the convention without needing it explained**.
- A player who asks about translation can be told the script is archaic and the literate few.
- The runes are a recurring signal: *this object does not obey the rules of this world.*

If a player wants to commission or manufacture a new runed item, that is a major plot beat — there is no known living maker. Surface it as a quest, not a service.

---

## Known Maker — Thalamin Perisdottir

[Thalamin Perisdottir]({{PLUGIN_ROOT}}/npcs/chapter_1/remaining_npcs.md) — Aliss's husband, the smith at the Misty Forest house — can **fashion the runes onto items**, though he cannot read the script. The craft is mechanical to him — a learned pattern he reproduces faithfully without comprehension.

**Time required scales exponentially with the item's size:**

- **Small object** (e.g. a box the size of Aliss's dead box) — a few hours.
- **Chest-sized object** — about a day.
- **Larger than that** — DM discretion.

**Hard limit:** within a single day he cannot produce a container large enough to hold anything larger than a halfling. Anything human-sized or above is a multi-day project at minimum, and the party should treat it as such when planning.

This is a load-bearing constraint for any plan that relies on shielding a person, creature, or large object from the loop, magical detection, or magical effects. He is also a single point of failure — if he is unavailable, no new runed items are being made.
