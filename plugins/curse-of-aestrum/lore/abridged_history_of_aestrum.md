# An Abridged History of Aestrum

*by Bari Khalid*

---

## Foreword

Aestrum is a land of striking contrasts. Though it is nestled in the serene embrace of a secluded valley and boasts the tranquil air of an unchallenged duchy, its history is one fraught with inexplicable, often magical, occurrences. One might expect such a peaceful land to be free of the tumultuous tides that define the histories of more famous realms, but in truth, Aestrum's tale is one woven with mystery and mysticism, of gods who walked among mortals, and battles that altered the course of history in the most peculiar of ways.

Among the events that have shaped this quiet region is the infamous Acolypyrrhic Battles of the late 1200s—a series of conflicts that, at the time, seemed as baffling as they were catastrophic. Fought between acolytes of nearly a dozen deities, the battles may explain why some areas of Aestrum positively hum with magical energy, and why yet other areas behave as magical dead zones. Historians differ in their accounts, but the general consensus is that the battles were marked by sudden and violent shifts in the laws of nature. The skies turned an unnatural red, and a haze of ethereal fog clouded the valley for weeks. What makes these battles particularly notable, however, is the subsequent disappearance of many combatants—vanishing without a trace as if they had been burned from the land.

But the mystical occurrences in Aestrum are not limited to its bloody history. The land's most perplexing and long-standing feature is its deep connection to the divine. For many centuries, Aestrum's people revered Selune, the Moon Goddess, as their primary deity. Her light guided them through the darkest nights, and her blessings were felt in the calm of the valley and the fertile soil that sustained their crops. Temples dedicated to her dotted the landscape, and festivals in her honor were held with great fanfare. In the year 1477, however, something remarkable and tragic happened that forever altered the spiritual fabric of Aestrum: The Great Withdrawal of the Gods. During this event, Selune—along with the other deities of the realm—withdrew from the mortal plane. Priests and followers were left bewildered, as the divine presence that had once been so palpable receded into the ether for all but those held in special favor by their respective Gods.

In the wake of this divine withdrawal, Aestrum underwent a spiritual upheaval. With fewer souls who could truly hear the words of their Lady, many in the region became less pious, or began to revere other deities in shrines or temples which were closer to their own home. Where once Selune was revered above all others, the valley's numerous temples now stood either abandoned or taken over by worshippers of some other quieted God. Those few who remained loyal to the Moon Goddess struggled to make sense of her sudden withdrawal, and the divine guidance they had once received seemed to fade into distant memory. Despite the fall from grace, Selune's followers devoutly maintained a single shrine in her honor within the city of Duskwall itself — a quiet cave-shrine off the road that climbs to the castle. It is a solitary testament to the devotion that once characterized the people's relationship with their goddess.

What remains now of Aestrum's ancient spiritual practices is a mixture of uncertainty and myth. The lands that once shimmered with divine favor now appear as silent witnesses to the passing of an age. Yet, for all the absence of gods, there are still whispers—whispers of strange phenomena, spectral figures in the forest, and cryptic messages spoken on the wind. Though the gods have withdrawn, the valley remains alive with mystery, as if the land itself retains a memory of the magic that once infused it.

As you read this account of Aestrum's history, take note not only of the political shifts and wars that have defined its path but also of the moments when the veil between the mortal realm and the divine thinned, when magic and mystery were no longer abstract concepts, but living, breathing forces that shaped the course of the valley's destiny.

The history of Aestrum is not simply one of politics and borders, but of mystical events that defy reason. And perhaps, in uncovering these stories, we may come to understand just what it is that makes Aestrum such an enigmatic and fascinating land.

## The Acolypyrrhic Battles — 1280

During this time, Selune was still the predominant deity of the land, though others had small temples and shrines throughout the land. One such temple rested high in the mountains South of Rockwood, and it was built by The Brothers and Sisters of the Purifying Flame — worshippers of Kossuth, a fire primordial. This temple and its devotees lived peacefully within the valley, though a small band journeyed into a neighboring region sometime in the beginning of the year, and when they returned they brought with them a stolen aspect of Akadi.

Some writings of the time claim the theft was prankish in nature, while others claim the theft was at the behest of Kossuth as a way of jostling for rank among the elementals. The end result, however, was an enraged sect out for blood.

It is important to note here that the winds are fickle and difficult to interpret; an East breeze may turn West in a moment's time. Such was the anger of the followers of Akadi. Guided into Aestrum by the winds of Akadi, her followers often beset upon the first temple or shrine they came across, ransacking each as they searched for the stolen relic. This in turn riled the other deities, elementals, and their followers, until eventually a great many entered open conflict within Aestrum.

With battles driven by the will of the Gods themselves, Aestrum became a magical hotbed. Rare magical creatures became common, unexplainable events and miracles occurred on the daily, and the skies turned unnatural colors in the night. Many of the acolytes which fought in these battles became empowered by the magical energies of their benefactor Gods up until they were consumed in magical fire, hence the name of the collection of conflicts during this time. Those acolytes were consumed wholly, leaving behind no trace of their being.

As the battles raged, the followers of Akadi were eventually guided South of Rockwood and into the mountains where the temple of Kossuth stood. At this point in time the temple was known throughout all of Aestrum and even beyond for its magnificent stained glass windows, some of which depicted the mid-day sun and the flames so often associated with Kossuth. Those windows did little to fortify the temple however, and the followers of Akadi ransacked the temple, though their missing relic was never found. Instead, they pilfered a relic of Kossuth. They descended the mountain with that relic, and the battles abated as Akadi seemed to be satiated with what was deemed a tit-for-tat exchange of relics.

The band of Akadians was last seen heading North out of Mirot, once again guided by the winds of their Mistress. As the Akadians left the region, so did the violence — though the after-effects of such a concentration of magic can still be felt throughout Aestrum today.

One of the most well-documented battles took place toward the end of the conflict, South of what is now Duskwall, on the land of the Deckard clan. This battle included forces from four deities and raged for over one whole day. According to battle scribes, at least one God directly intervened in the battle and quashed all magic in the area in order to pause the fighting. To this day, what is left of clan Deckard's estate is a magical deadzone.

## On Magica

Perhaps as a result of the Acolypyrrhic Battles, Aestrum has seen a deluge of magical items. Many of these are minor boons, or items commissioned through craftsmen in Duskwall. There are, however, also those documented through stories and fables, though even these have been lost to the wilderness or avoided due to the curses so commonly placed on items of such importance as a way of deterring theft. Indeed, it must be the desperate soul who searches for these items.

My favorite tale of a magical item is the cautionary fable of Aric the Woodsman. In the fable, Aric is foraging for tinder for his campfire. In the middle of The Misty Woods, he finds a barren patch with a skeleton garbed in men's clothes in the center. The flora surrounding this patch seems to grow away from the center, as if even the plants wanted not to do with the remains. Around the neck of the skeleton is a talisman of finely engraved copper. Aric, of course, takes the talisman and places it on his own neck. In the coming days, Aric finds he is stronger and faster than before, able to run down his prey with less effort than had he used his bow. His stamina is greatly improved as well, and he can run not only fast, but far.

Aric's village is amazed at his abilities and honors him with gifts and status — even hosting a celebration in honor of each successful hunt — yet Aric is unable to find satisfaction or happiness in either celebration or accomplishment.

To better understand the talisman, Aric journeys out in search of the barren patch where he found the item, hopeful he might find some clue as to why he is no longer happy. He grows tired as he heads deeper into the woods, until at last he finds a barren patch. To his surprise, the skeleton in this patch is wearing a dress and seems older than even the skeleton on which he had found the talisman. Confused, Aric continued further into the forest, the talisman growing heavier with each step. After only a few minutes more he found another barren patch — this one with the skeleton of a child. Exhausted, Aric continued on and found patch after patch in that forest until he finally collapsed from exhaustion.

Unable to move, Aric watched as the plants near him stretched their limbs away from him.

Of course, not all magical items are cursed. The Pendant of Waking Hours has no actual curse, though its owner — Elsinoor Trambeathen — eventually went mad from use due to lack of sleep. Seeing what happened to Elsinoor, her husband had the ring buried with her in Dunleaven.

Commissioned items are often the safest magical items, as there's no chance of unknown history or questionable provenance. Though commissioned items are fairly common, the most well-known item here in Duskwall is actually The Loadstone. Commissioned in 1458 by Alman Tallwood, The Loadstone has been a boon to the docks of Duskwall. The Loadstone operates by way of teleporting reasonably sized cargo in view of the bearer from one location to another location also in view by the bearer.

Now, even without curses, magical items may be dangerous. The Loadstone itself has been the cause of no less than four deaths of maidens due to a lack of concentration by the bearer; as the enchantment teleports an item from one visual location to another, those making use of the stone must be sure not to glance at passerby.

## Flora and Fauna

Aestrum is currently home to some unique species, though many more have died off, been hunted to extinction, or have been driven off in recent generations.

Our central forest — The Misty Forest — is home to no less than seventeen species of fungus found nowhere else in Faerun. Some of these species are edible, though one must be careful as the edible ones more often than not appear similar to those which are poisonous or hallucinogenic.

The Misty Forest is also the source of nearly all lumber in the region, as the yucca-type plants which grow in the mountainous regions are of poor quality for building or crafting. Though the oaks of the Misty Forest are of arguably the same quality one would find outside of Aestrum, the forest does seem to grow at a far greater clip than other forests, resulting in high yields overall.

Some creatures within the Misty Forest have also been known to grow to unnatural sizes, with older generations claiming deer larger than horses, and bears as big as a hut. Creatures nowadays are often of normal size, though the occasional hunter does come back with a noteworthy kill. The growth rate of the trees and animals within the forest beg the question of whether the Acolypyrrhic Battles could have caused such a unique change in how this biome grows.

To the Northwest, Mirot was once known for a great variety of snakes, though nearly all were driven out. The Nahamkate Desert to the North of Mirot, however, still lays claim to many species of snakes and scorpions not found elsewhere. This is no surprise, as the region was once a diaspora of the Yuan-Ti civilization. While no Yuan-Ti have been seen in decades, their temples still regularly become unearthed or buried as the sands of the Nahamkate Desert shift.

Even the coastline near Duskwall has a unique species of seaweed: Hylea's Mead. When pressed, this seaweed produces a sweet honey-like sap which up until twenty years ago was used as an ingredient in local mead production. Before that, it was often used by midwives as a form of contraceptive.

## Royal Violence

Philosophers like to claim that those who have actual power fear only losing that power. When it comes to inherited power, those in power have only family to fear. Such was the period between 1428 and 1460, where Aestrum saw a new ruler at an average of every four years.

In the year 1410, Telemachus the Virile was thrown from horseback and killed during a hunting expedition, leaving behind his wife, Heliconine, and nineteen children, thirteen of which were potential heirs to the throne. Naturally, the throne went to the oldest surviving son, Talian, who was already married and nearly thirty years of age. Talian, however, was childless, and before he and his wife could conceive an heir of their own, Talian was found dead. The official cause of death recorded by the officiant of the new Duke — Talian's younger brother Salender — is listed as old age, but Talian's body was never officially interred, and a large number of staff under Talian disappeared immediately following his disappearance.

Immediately after this transition, Heliconine became a much more prominent figure than she had been during the reign of her husband. She served in the court as something approaching a royal advisor. She did so as nearly each of the thirteen potential heirs of Telemachus had a turn upon the throne of Aestrum. Of those thirteen, one fled the region, two were killed while fulfilling their duties as knights, one died in a tavern brawl, and one was actually caught, tried, and hanged for treason. The other eight heirs eventually gained control of the throne by either directly murdering their sibling (and that sibling's heirs) or arranging for such a murder.

During this time, Aestrum suffered. While still free from worry of conquest, the populace faced daily uncertainty when it came to regional management. Constant replacement of royal staff and administrators had resulted in an inability to become experts in those roles, let alone proficient. Perishables rotted on the docks because no infrastructure survived in order to move them. Crime was rampant as town guards were replaced every few years with new guards loyal to the new Duke.

Eventually, Lognan Tallwood — the last living child of Telemachus — ascended to the throne after yet another dubious death of a Duke: Lognan's older brother Brandache. Lognan's first order of business was an expeditious trial of Heliconine, who Lognan had declared a traitor to the duchy. Heliconine was found guilty, gagged, then bound to the remains of Brandache before being entombed alive in mortar. The mortar was left to harden in the main courtyard of what was at the time Dawnwall Castle for two weeks before it was then crane-lifted over the Eastern wall of the castle and dropped into the crashing waves below.

## The Coronation of Evandur Tallwood

With no treacherous siblings to worry about, Lognan ushered in a period of stability and consistency unlike anything the region had seen for more than a generation. To escape an association with the actions of his family and instill a more positive outlook on the future, Lognan renamed Dawnwall Castle to Duskwall. He worked to rebuild a tattered infrastructure and rebuild loyalty and trust in the crown.

Over time, Lognan sired seven children of his own, of which four were boys. Lognan had always had an affinity for the sea, and he often took his children out on the royal ship he had commissioned, The Survivor. Lognan's wife, Berenice, however, harbored a fear of deep water, so she always remained ashore when Lognan ventured out onto The Cathar Sea — the inland sea on whose Western shores Duskwall is built.

As he raised his children with his wife Berenice, he cautioned his children against turning on one another and made clear to them that Evandur would be Duke upon his passing. Lognan stressed to the younger boys — Lucretius, Jaugr, and Malston — that they should all work to support Evandur for the good of the region and the family. He often spoke to them about the treachery of his siblings, and how he had only survived all those years because his mother had always needed another heir, lest the current Duke begin disregarding her instructions.

Evandur listened intently during these childhood lessons, though history would come to show that he learned an unexpected lesson.

When Lognan managed to be the only child of Telemachus to die of natural causes in 1474, the region mourned. Evandur organized funeral rites out on The Cathar Sea aboard The Survivor for the entire family — sans his mother, who still would not board a boat.

I was present that day as nearly the whole city gathered in the Waterfront district to bid farewell to the ruler that had guided us out of arguably the darkest period our region has ever seen. We solemnly watched as Evandur Tallwood led his siblings aboard the Survivor alongside a regal casket carted by four large horses.

Shortly after all of the siblings were aboard, the ship pulled away from the dock. Within moments, a murmur arose from those closest to the water, and Evandur Tallwood appeared on horseback, riding quickly back toward the castle. Some among the crowd seemed to know what was occurring and watched the coming events unfold in paralyzed terror, while those with children rushed them away.

Unbeknownst to all but a few of Evandur's most loyal servants, The Survivor had been filled with sulfur, charcoal, and saltpeter the previous day. After making a show of boarding The Survivor with his siblings, Evandur had disembarked the vessel on a dinghy and rushed back to shore.

When he arrived at the castle, he ordered the siege ballistae be lit, and then commanded they target The Survivor.

Some in attendance weeped as the ballistae struck their target and the ship exploded into a magnificent fireball, but many on-hand understood the cold, calculating logic of what they had just witnessed. This extreme solution would prevent any violent claims to the throne for the foreseeable future.

## The Duchess Ilphekiir

Upon Evandur's ascension to the throne, there was a rush of suitors, as one would expect when a crown is passed on. Evandur quickly secured a trading contract with the Ilphekiirs — a noble family of Half-Elves from the North. Part of this contract was an arranged marriage with Quellenna, the youngest of their two daughters, as the elder sister, Galadiil, was already arranged to marry a prince far to the North.

Unfortunately for Quellenna, the prince to whom her elder sister was arranged to marry came down with a wasting disease and the marriage to the prince was called off. Desperate to maximize their influence, the family arranged for Evandur to instead marry Galadiil, and Quellenna was instead assigned as a liaison to Setland, a neighboring region of Aestrum.

Evandur and Galadiil were wed in 1475 under glorious blue skies and amid much fanfare, but with hardly any family from either side. Within days of the marriage, however, it became clear that Evandur would continue his rule with the same caution and reservations against potential heirs as he had started with.

To this day, Galadiil has yet to provide an heir.
